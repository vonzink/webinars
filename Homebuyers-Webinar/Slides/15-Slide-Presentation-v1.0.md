## Can I Actually Buy a Home?

Presentation purpose: a focused 15-slide first-time homebuyer seminar that feels like a premium keynote, not a mortgage class.

Target runtime: 22 to 28 minutes plus Q&A.

## Slide 001 - Can I Actually Buy a Home?

- Purpose: Open with the audience's real question, not the mortgage industry's agenda.
- Educational objective: Define the seminar around one decision: whether buying fits the buyer's life.
- Emotional objective: Let the audience feel seen, not judged.
- Visual objective: Make the opening feel like an Apple keynote: one huge question, abundant whitespace, tiny brand presence.
- Key message: You are not behind. You just have not been shown the map.
- Visual concept: White slide, official MSFG logo small in the lower right, enormous title: "Can I actually buy a home?" A thin-line home outline draws beneath the word "home."
- Speaker notes: "Most people think the first question is, what rate can I get? It is not. The first question is whether buying fits your life."
- Animation recommendations: Fade in question over 0.5s. Draw the home outline in one smooth stroke. Hold for one beat.
- Graphic recommendations: Custom single-line home icon. No photo. No decorative shapes.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Ask attendees to type one word: confident, curious, unsure, overwhelmed.
- Questions to ask: "Which word best describes how homebuying feels right now?"
- Transition to next slide: "If you feel unsure, there is a reason. You are entering a process full of people who do this every day."

## Slide 002 - You Are Doing This Once

- Purpose: Normalize uncertainty.
- Educational objective: Show that the buyer is surrounded by repeat players: lender, realtor, seller, underwriter, title, insurance.
- Emotional objective: Remove shame from not knowing the process.
- Visual objective: Replace paragraph explanation with a relationship map.
- Key message: You are doing this once. Everyone else does it every day.
- Visual concept: One buyer icon in the center. Six professional roles orbit around it in a clean radial map.
- Speaker notes: "This does not mean the process is against you. It means you deserve translation."
- Animation recommendations: Buyer appears first. Roles appear one at a time clockwise.
- Graphic recommendations: Use one consistent thin-line icon family. Keep labels short.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Quick poll: "Have you spoken to a lender before?"
- Questions to ask: "Which role on this map feels least clear?"
- Transition to next slide: "So tonight is not a tour of mortgage jargon. It is a map."

## Slide 003 - The Five Decisions

- Purpose: Give the short seminar its structure.
- Educational objective: Preview the five decisions: rent vs buy, payment, comfort, cash, offer.
- Emotional objective: Create confidence that the presentation is finite and navigable.
- Visual objective: A clean five-step path that returns later as the recap.
- Key message: You only need five decisions to start clearly.
- Visual concept: Horizontal path with five labeled markers: compare, calculate, fit, prepare, choose.
- Speaker notes: "You do not need to learn the whole mortgage industry tonight. You need the decisions that keep you from being surprised."
- Animation recommendations: Path line draws left to right. Each marker appears with a soft fade.
- Graphic recommendations: Reusable five-step journey map.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Ask which decision they came to understand.
- Questions to ask: "Which of these five decisions feels most unclear right now?"
- Transition to next slide: "The first decision is not emotional. It is a comparison."

## Slide 004 - Rent vs Buy Is a Comparison

- Purpose: Reframe the rent/buy question.
- Educational objective: Teach that rent vs buy should compare monthly cost, timeline, flexibility, risk, and equity.
- Emotional objective: Lower pressure by saying renting can be the right answer.
- Visual objective: Use a premium calculator panel integrated into the slide.
- Key message: Renting is not failure. Buying is not automatically wisdom.
- Visual concept: Split layout. Left: two doors labeled "Keep renting" and "Explore buying." Right: reserved live calculator area for Rent vs Buy.
- Speaker notes: "A good lender should be willing to say not yet if the math says not yet."
- Animation recommendations: Doors fade in together. Calculator frame appears after the question is explained.
- Graphic recommendations: Live calculator slot with four input labels: rent, target price, cash available, expected years in home.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Ask attendees to think of their current rent and how long they expect to stay in their next home.
- Questions to ask: "If buying only made sense after five years, would that change your thinking?"
- Transition to next slide: "If buying stays on the table, the next mistake is looking at price instead of payment."

## Slide 005 - Price Is Not the Number

- Purpose: Break the habit of thinking in purchase price only.
- Educational objective: Teach that a home price becomes a monthly payment made of several parts.
- Emotional objective: Reduce payment shock by revealing hidden components.
- Visual objective: Show a purchase price transforming into a payment stack.
- Key message: You do not live inside a purchase price. You live inside a monthly payment.
- Visual concept: Large "$450,000" morphs into stacked blocks: principal, interest, taxes, insurance, mortgage insurance.
- Speaker notes: "When buyers get surprised, it is usually because someone let them think the purchase price was the whole story."
- Animation recommendations: Price dissolves into stack. Each stack segment labels itself in sequence.
- Graphic recommendations: Reusable payment stack infographic with official palette only.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Ask which payment component they had not considered.
- Questions to ask: "When you hear a monthly payment online, which of these might be missing?"
- Transition to next slide: "Now we move the dials that build the payment."

## Slide 006 - Payment Calculator

- Purpose: Run or reserve the mortgage payment calculator.
- Educational objective: Demonstrate how price, down payment, rate, taxes, insurance, and MI affect payment.
- Emotional objective: Turn abstract anxiety into visible cause and effect.
- Visual objective: Show one large monthly number with a clean live calculator module.
- Key message: A payment is not guessed. It is built.
- Visual concept: Right side: calculator frame. Left side: live payment stack and large estimated payment. Include placeholder for calculator link/embed.
- Speaker notes: "Do not memorize this example. Watch the relationship. The point is cause and effect."
- Animation recommendations: Highlight one input at a time. Pulse only the payment number when the input changes.
- Graphic recommendations: Reserve live embed area. Static fallback: three scenario cards for lower price, base case, higher rate.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Let the audience choose one input to change first.
- Questions to ask: "Which dial do you think changes the payment fastest?"
- Transition to next slide: "The calculator can tell us what passes. It cannot tell us what feels comfortable."

## Slide 007 - Approved Is Not the Same as Comfortable

- Purpose: Separate lender approval from household readiness.
- Educational objective: Explain that approval is a ceiling, not a recommendation.
- Emotional objective: Build trust by saying the thing buyers rarely hear.
- Visual objective: Show the gap between "approved" and "comfortable" as a physical distance.
- Key message: The lender can approve a ceiling. You still need a floor plan.
- Visual concept: Architectural cross-section of a home: top line labeled "approved ceiling," lower green zone labeled "comfortable plan."
- Speaker notes: "Our job is not to cheer every approval. It is to help you choose a payment you can live with."
- Animation recommendations: Ceiling drops in first. Comfort zone draws beneath it after a beat.
- Graphic recommendations: Custom home cross-section diagram.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Poll: "Would you use the maximum approval if the lender offered it?"
- Questions to ask: "What would make a payment uncomfortable even if the lender said yes?"
- Transition to next slide: "Now we use the affordability calculator to find both numbers."

## Slide 008 - Affordability Calculator

- Purpose: Run or reserve the affordability calculator.
- Educational objective: Show the difference between lender ceiling and life-ready range.
- Emotional objective: Give buyers permission to choose comfort over maximum.
- Visual objective: Two large numbers side by side with a visible gap between them.
- Key message: A smaller approved plan can feel bigger because it protects your life.
- Visual concept: Live calculator panel with inputs for income, debts, savings, target payment. Output cards: "can pass" and "can live with."
- Speaker notes: "The goal is not to make the number bigger. The goal is to make the plan better."
- Animation recommendations: Reveal "can pass" first. Pause. Reveal "can live with." Draw the gap bracket.
- Graphic recommendations: Include a labeled gap visual. Keep all numbers hypothetical until approved.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Ask attendees what they would protect first: savings, childcare, travel, debt payoff, or maintenance.
- Questions to ask: "Would you rather know your ceiling or your comfortable number?"
- Transition to next slide: "Comfort only becomes real when it fits inside the month."

## Slide 009 - Monthly Budget

- Purpose: Run or reserve the monthly budget calculator.
- Educational objective: Teach that housing must fit inside the buyer's actual monthly life.
- Emotional objective: Make homeownership feel responsible instead of reckless.
- Visual objective: Replace a budget paragraph with a dashboard.
- Key message: A home should change your address, not erase your life.
- Visual concept: Budget dashboard with seven tiles: housing, debt, food, childcare, savings, maintenance, life. Reserved live calculator area shows "room left."
- Speaker notes: "This is where we stop treating buyers like loan files and start treating them like households."
- Animation recommendations: Tiles build in groups. Remaining-room meter animates last.
- Graphic recommendations: Live budget calculator placeholder plus static fallback with comfort zones.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Ask attendees to name one category they refuse to sacrifice for a home.
- Questions to ask: "What monthly number would let you still be yourself?"
- Transition to next slide: "Now that the monthly side is visible, we need the upfront cash side."

## Slide 010 - The 20 Percent Myth

- Purpose: Remove the largest false blocker.
- Educational objective: Explain that 20 percent down is one strategy, not the universal entrance fee.
- Emotional objective: Create possibility without making eligibility promises.
- Visual objective: Turn "20%" into a reveal of multiple paths.
- Key message: Twenty percent is one path. It is not the entrance fee.
- Visual concept: Giant "20%" splits into smaller path cards: 0%, 3%, 3.5%, assistance, larger down payment.
- Speaker notes: "Twenty percent can be a great strategy. It is not the universal requirement people think it is."
- Animation recommendations: Ask poll first. Reveal smaller paths only after audience commits.
- Graphic recommendations: Mark all program details as delivery-date verified before use.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Poll: "What down payment do you think is required?"
- Questions to ask: "Who has heard 20 percent from family, friends, or online?"
- Transition to next slide: "But down payment is only one piece of the cash you need."

## Slide 011 - Cash to Close

- Purpose: Reserve closing cost worksheet and cash-to-close estimate in one integrated moment.
- Educational objective: Explain that cash to close includes down payment, closing costs, prepaids, escrows, deposits, credits, and reserves.
- Emotional objective: Convert a vague fear into a visible savings target.
- Visual objective: Use a waterfall chart instead of a text-heavy fee list.
- Key message: Cash to close is calculated, not guessed.
- Visual concept: Left: closing cost worksheet placeholder with sections. Right: cash-to-close waterfall ending in one large final number.
- Speaker notes: "Some costs are fees. Some are timing. Some can be offset. The worksheet keeps them from hiding."
- Animation recommendations: Build the waterfall one line at a time. Reveal final cash number last.
- Graphic recommendations: Live worksheet slot and cash-to-close calculator slot. Static fallback: labeled worksheet plus waterfall.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Ask which lever they would want to explore: credits, down payment, closing date, or assistance.
- Questions to ask: "Would you rather lower monthly payment or lower cash to close?"
- Transition to next slide: "Once you know the monthly number and the cash number, you can compare offers intelligently."

## Slide 012 - The Lowest Rate Is Not Always the Cheapest Loan

- Purpose: Introduce APR comparison.
- Educational objective: Teach that rate, fees, credits, and time together determine the better offer.
- Emotional objective: Protect buyers from rate-shopping panic.
- Visual objective: Show two loan cards and a hold-period timeline.
- Key message: The cheapest loan depends on how long you keep it.
- Visual concept: Loan A and Loan B cards side by side. Reserved APR comparison calculator area. Timeline slider beneath with "winner can flip."
- Speaker notes: "Rate matters. It just does not live alone."
- Animation recommendations: Show Loan A and Loan B. Let audience vote. Then move timeline and reveal the winner flip.
- Graphic recommendations: Live APR calculator placeholder plus static fallback with 2-year, 5-year, 10-year cards.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Poll: "Which offer looks cheaper at first glance?"
- Questions to ask: "How long do you honestly expect to keep the first mortgage?"
- Transition to next slide: "The same time question applies to buying down the rate."

## Slide 013 - Buydown Break-Even

- Purpose: Reserve interest rate buydown calculator and teach break-even.
- Educational objective: Explain paying upfront for a lower rate as a trade between cash now and payment savings later.
- Emotional objective: Give buyers a decision rule instead of a sales hook.
- Visual objective: One break-even timeline with a live calculator placeholder.
- Key message: A buydown is not good or bad until you know the break-even point.
- Visual concept: Scale: upfront cost on one side, monthly savings on the other. Timeline marks the month where savings catch up.
- Speaker notes: "If someone presents a buydown without a break-even point, they are giving you half the analysis."
- Animation recommendations: Reveal upfront cost, reveal monthly savings, draw break-even line last.
- Graphic recommendations: Live buydown calculator slot. Static fallback: cost divided by monthly savings.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Ask whether they would rather keep cash or lower payment.
- Questions to ask: "How long would you need to stay before paying upfront feels worth it?"
- Transition to next slide: "Now we turn the whole seminar into a simple plan."

## Slide 014 - Your Seven-Day Homebuyer Plan

- Purpose: Convert learning into action.
- Educational objective: Give a practical one-week sequence.
- Emotional objective: Replace overwhelm with momentum.
- Visual objective: A seven-day calendar with one task per day.
- Key message: You do not need to buy this week. You need to get clear this week.
- Visual concept: Seven-day strip: gather numbers, check credit, set comfort payment, review cash, check programs, compare lenders, ask questions.
- Speaker notes: "The goal is not speed. The goal is clarity."
- Animation recommendations: Calendar days flip in one at a time. Final day highlights "ask questions."
- Graphic recommendations: Make this a reusable downloadable worksheet later.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Ask which task they can complete in under 30 minutes.
- Questions to ask: "Which of these would make you feel clearer by next week?"
- Transition to next slide: "Here is the invitation: bring us the questions before you bring us the application."

## Slide 015 - Questions Before Applications

- Purpose: Close with trust and invite Q&A or follow-up.
- Educational objective: Direct people to the right next step without pressure.
- Emotional objective: Leave the audience feeling capable and supported.
- Visual objective: Clean white closing slide with official MSFG logo, presenter contact/QR area, and a single Q&A prompt.
- Key message: Bring us your questions before you bring us your application.
- Visual concept: Left: "What should we calculate together?" Right: contact card with Seth headshot, QR placeholder, and optional booking link slot.
- Speaker notes: "No pressure. Ask the question that feels too basic. That is usually the question everyone else has too."
- Animation recommendations: Thank-you line appears first. Contact module fades in second. Keep the slide still during Q&A.
- Graphic recommendations: Use official raster logo on white only. Use Seth headshot and QR if approved for the event.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Open Q&A by category: payment, cash, credit, programs, rates, process.
- Questions to ask: "What number do you want to understand before you leave?"
- Transition to next slide: End taught content. Move into live Q&A.
