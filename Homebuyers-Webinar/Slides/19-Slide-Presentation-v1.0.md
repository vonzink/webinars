## Slide 001 - The Homebuyer's Playbook

- Purpose: Open with confidence, not mortgage jargon.
- Educational objective: Frame the webinar as a practical map for first-time buyers.
- Emotional objective: Make the audience feel guided by an educator.
- Visual objective: Establish a premium MSFG keynote with Seth visible as the presenter.
- Key message: Thirty minutes of clarity can prevent expensive confusion.
- Visual concept: Dark green title slide, large editorial title, Seth portrait, MSFG LLC and NMLS line.
- Speaker notes: "Tonight is not about pressure. It is about understanding the numbers and the process before you make decisions."
- Animation recommendations: Fade title first, then Seth, then company line.
- Graphic recommendations: Use Seth cutout and official logo only on intentional white field.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Ask people to write down the one number they want to understand.
- Questions to ask: "What would make buying feel clearer by the end of tonight?"
- Transition to next slide: "Here is the map we will follow."

## Slide 002 - Today's Roadmap

- Purpose: Set the six-topic structure.
- Educational objective: Preview myths, budget, loan programs, process, mistakes, and next steps.
- Emotional objective: Lower anxiety by making the session finite.
- Visual objective: Match the reference roadmap with clean left text and six fly-in items.
- Key message: Knowledge builds confidence.
- Visual concept: White editorial roadmap with numbered topics on the right.
- Speaker notes: "These are the six things buyers need before the process feels manageable."
- Animation recommendations: Press space to reveal the six topics one at a time.
- Graphic recommendations: Keep the six lines simple and readable.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Ask which topic brought them to the webinar.
- Questions to ask: "Which one of these feels most confusing right now?"
- Transition to next slide: "Before we calculate anything, we need to clear out two common myths."

## Slide 003 - The 20 Percent Myth

- Purpose: Remove the largest false blocker.
- Educational objective: Teach that 20 percent down is one strategy, not a universal entrance fee.
- Emotional objective: Create possibility without overpromising.
- Visual objective: Use a giant 20 percent reveal and path cards.
- Key message: 20 percent is a strategy. Not the entrance fee.
- Visual concept: Dark slide with poll buttons, giant 20 percent, and multiple down-payment paths.
- Speaker notes: "Twenty percent can be a strong move. It is not the only door into homeownership."
- Animation recommendations: Let the audience answer first, then reveal the path cards.
- Graphic recommendations: Keep program labels broad and compliance-safe.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Run the down-payment poll.
- Questions to ask: "Who has heard 20 percent from family, friends, or online?"
- Transition to next slide: "The second myth shows up when people start comparing rates."

## Slide 004 - Lowest Rate Myth

- Purpose: Stop rate-only shopping before it creates bad comparisons.
- Educational objective: Introduce that rate, fees, APR, and time need to be compared together.
- Emotional objective: Reduce panic around daily rate movement.
- Visual objective: Show a lower-rate ticket against a fuller cost comparison.
- Key message: Rate matters. It just does not live alone.
- Visual concept: Two premium comparison tickets with buttons for rate, fees, and time.
- Speaker notes: "A lower rate may be better, but not because it is lower. We need to know what it costs and how long it has to work."
- Animation recommendations: Build the two tickets, then use buttons to change the teaching note.
- Graphic recommendations: Use the APR card as the stronger visual anchor.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Ask which offer they would choose at first glance.
- Questions to ask: "Would you pay more upfront for a lower monthly payment?"
- Transition to next slide: "Now we move from myths into the monthly decision."

## Slide 005 - Rent vs Buy Is a Comparison

- Purpose: Reframe rent vs buy as a financial and lifestyle comparison.
- Educational objective: Compare rent, target price, and expected years in the home.
- Emotional objective: Give permission for renting to be a responsible answer.
- Visual objective: Split narrative and live calculator panel.
- Key message: Renting is not failure. Buying is not automatically wisdom.
- Visual concept: Two clean choice cards on the left, live rent-vs-buy slot on the right.
- Speaker notes: "The question is not whether buying is always better. The question is whether buying fits this buyer, in this season."
- Animation recommendations: Fade the two doors together, then reveal the calculator.
- Graphic recommendations: Use the break-even line as the teaching visual.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Ask for a sample rent number.
- Questions to ask: "How long would you need to stay for buying to feel worth it?"
- Transition to next slide: "If buying stays on the table, price is not the number we live with."

## Slide 006 - Price Is Not the Number

- Purpose: Shift attention from purchase price to payment.
- Educational objective: Break payment into principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and mortgage insurance.
- Emotional objective: Prevent payment shock.
- Visual objective: Transform a large price into a stacked payment model.
- Key message: You live inside a monthly payment.
- Visual concept: Big purchase price, arrow, five clickable payment components.
- Speaker notes: "Most online numbers are incomplete. A real payment has pieces."
- Animation recommendations: Price appears first, then stack segments build from top to bottom.
- Graphic recommendations: Keep color hierarchy from dark green to bright green.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Click the component buyers know least.
- Questions to ask: "Which of these do online calculators often leave out?"
- Transition to next slide: "Now we move the dials that build that payment."

## Slide 007 - Payment Calculator

- Purpose: Demonstrate cause and effect in the monthly payment.
- Educational objective: Show how price, down payment, rate, escrow, and MI affect payment.
- Emotional objective: Turn anxiety into visible levers.
- Visual objective: One dominant payment number beside live inputs.
- Key message: A payment is built.
- Visual concept: Large payment hero with range controls and scenario buttons.
- Speaker notes: "Do not memorize the example. Watch the relationship between inputs and payment."
- Animation recommendations: Pulse the payment number when inputs change.
- Graphic recommendations: Use scenario buttons for clean live demo choices.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Let the audience pick the first dial to move.
- Questions to ask: "Which dial do you think changes the payment fastest?"
- Transition to next slide: "Passing a calculator is not the same as living comfortably."

## Slide 008 - Approved Is Not Comfortable

- Purpose: Separate lender approval from household comfort.
- Educational objective: Explain approval as a ceiling, not a recommendation.
- Emotional objective: Build trust by protecting the buyer from overextension.
- Visual objective: Show approved ceiling, comfort zone, and foundation line inside a home diagram.
- Key message: A good approval still needs a life-ready payment.
- Visual concept: Architectural cross-section with slider-controlled comfort zone.
- Speaker notes: "Our job is not to cheer every approval. It is to help you understand what the approval would do to your month."
- Animation recommendations: Reveal the ceiling first, then the comfort zone.
- Graphic recommendations: Keep the home diagram simple and architectural.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Ask what would make a payment feel uncomfortable.
- Questions to ask: "Would you use the maximum approval just because it was available?"
- Transition to next slide: "The affordability calculator helps us find both numbers."

## Slide 009 - Affordability Calculator

- Purpose: Find the difference between what can pass and what can work.
- Educational objective: Compare income, debt, lender ceiling, and comfort range.
- Emotional objective: Give buyers permission to choose margin.
- Visual objective: Two large output cards with a visible breathing-room gap.
- Key message: A smaller approved plan can feel bigger because it protects life.
- Visual concept: Dark live calculator with income and debt sliders.
- Speaker notes: "The goal is not to make the number as high as possible. The goal is to make the plan resilient."
- Animation recommendations: Reveal can pass first, then can live with.
- Graphic recommendations: Use the gap bar as the memory hook.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Ask what life category they refuse to sacrifice.
- Questions to ask: "What would you protect first: savings, childcare, debt payoff, or flexibility?"
- Transition to next slide: "Comfort only becomes real when it fits inside a full month."

## Slide 010 - Monthly Budget

- Purpose: Put housing inside the household month.
- Educational objective: Show how housing competes with debt, food, childcare, savings, maintenance, and life.
- Emotional objective: Make responsible buying feel possible.
- Visual objective: Use dashboard tiles instead of budget paragraphs.
- Key message: A home should not erase your life.
- Visual concept: Budget allocator with room-left meter.
- Speaker notes: "This is where we stop treating buyers like loan files and start treating them like households."
- Animation recommendations: Build tiles in groups, then animate the room-left meter.
- Graphic recommendations: Keep the housing and life tiles larger than the smaller expense tiles.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Let the audience choose one category to protect.
- Questions to ask: "What monthly number would let you still be yourself?"
- Transition to next slide: "Now that the budget is visible, we can compare loan paths."

## Slide 011 - Loan Programs

- Purpose: Teach program comparison without making eligibility promises.
- Educational objective: Introduce conventional, FHA, VA, USDA, and down-payment assistance as paths.
- Emotional objective: Replace program confusion with a menu of possibilities.
- Visual objective: Dark five-path comparison board with a live detail panel.
- Key message: Loan programs are paths. Not promises.
- Visual concept: Five selectable program cards and one detail strip.
- Speaker notes: "The point is not to decide your program from one slide. The point is to know that different paths exist and need verification."
- Animation recommendations: Cards appear as a row, active detail appears last.
- Graphic recommendations: Use broad descriptions and avoid rate or qualification promises.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Click the path the audience is most curious about.
- Questions to ask: "Which path have you heard of before?"
- Transition to next slide: "Programs affect cash, but cash can also come from different places."

## Slide 012 - Down Payment Options

- Purpose: Broaden the buyer's understanding of upfront cash.
- Educational objective: Explain savings, gift funds, seller credits, and assistance.
- Emotional objective: Create options while reinforcing documentation.
- Visual objective: Four clean source cards with one explanation panel.

## Slide 013 - The Buying Team

- Purpose: Explain the roles around the buyer.
- Educational objective: Define lender, Realtor, seller, underwriter, title, and insurance.
- Emotional objective: Remove shame around not knowing who does what.
- Visual objective: Use an orbit map centered on the buyer.
- Key message: You are not supposed to know everyone's job.
- Visual concept: Buyer node in the center with role buttons around it.
- Speaker notes: "Everyone around this transaction does this all the time. The buyer usually does it once. That is why translation matters."
- Animation recommendations: Buyer appears first, then roles orbit in.
- Graphic recommendations: Use role labels, not decorative icons, for clarity.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Click the role that feels least clear.
- Questions to ask: "Which role feels most mysterious?"
- Transition to next slide: "Once you know the roles, the next number to understand is cash to close."

## Slide 014 - Cash to Close

- Purpose: Turn closing costs into a visible estimate.
- Educational objective: Explain down payment, closing costs, prepaids, escrow, credits, and timing.
- Emotional objective: Replace vague fear with a savings target.
- Visual objective: Pair worksheet rows with a waterfall chart.
- Key message: Cash to close is calculated. Not guessed.
- Visual concept: Worksheet on the left, cash waterfall on the right.
- Speaker notes: "Some cash is down payment. Some is cost. Some is timing. Some can be offset. The worksheet keeps it visible."
- Animation recommendations: Build worksheet rows, then waterfall bars, then total.
- Graphic recommendations: Use toggles for seller credit, assistance, and later closing.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Ask which lever they want to test.
- Questions to ask: "Would you rather lower cash to close or lower monthly payment?"
- Transition to next slide: "Now we return to rate shopping and compare offers correctly."

## Slide 015 - APR Comparison

- Purpose: Show how two loan offers can flip depending on goal and time.
- Educational objective: Compare lowest cash, lowest payment, and best over time.
- Emotional objective: Protect buyers from rate-only decision making.
- Visual objective: Two loan cards plus comparison controls.
- Key message: The cheapest loan depends on how you compare it.
- Visual concept: Loan A and Loan B cards with winner highlight and time slider.
- Speaker notes: "Rate matters. Fees matter. Time matters. APR helps compare cost, but the buyer's plan still matters."
- Animation recommendations: Reveal Loan A and Loan B, then switch modes live.
- Graphic recommendations: Keep the winner highlight obvious but restrained.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Ask the audience to vote before switching modes.
- Questions to ask: "Which offer looks cheaper at first glance?"
- Transition to next slide: "The same time question applies when buying down a rate."

## Slide 016 - Buydown Break-Even

- Purpose: Teach the break-even rule for paying upfront to lower rate.
- Educational objective: Explain upfront cost divided by monthly savings.
- Emotional objective: Give buyers a decision rule instead of a sales hook.
- Visual objective: One timeline marker that moves with inputs.
- Key message: A buydown needs a break-even point.
- Visual concept: Calculator inputs beside a horizontal break-even line.
- Speaker notes: "If someone presents a buydown without the break-even point, they are giving you half the analysis."
- Animation recommendations: Reveal cost, reveal savings, then animate the marker.
- Graphic recommendations: Make the break-even month the only dominant output.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Ask whether they would rather keep cash or reduce payment.
- Questions to ask: "How long would you need to stay before the upfront cost feels worth it?"
- Transition to next slide: "Now we name the mistakes these tools help prevent."

- Speaker notes: "These are not meant to scare you. They are meant to give you checkpoints."

- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Ask which checkpoint feels most likely to surprise them.
