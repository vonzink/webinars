# Definitive Seminar Blueprint v1.0

# The Homebuyer's Playbook
## Can I Actually Buy a Home?

Premium first-time homebuyer seminar for Mountain State Financial Group.

Runtime target: 42 minutes taught content plus Q&A.

Design system: `/Users/zacharyzink/MSFG/Webinars/presentation-system`

Core promise: by the end, the audience understands mortgages better than almost every first-time buyer they know.

## Experience Principles

- One educational objective per slide.
- One emotional objective per slide.
- One visual objective per slide.
- Every calculator appears because the story needs it, not because the tool exists.
- The presenter is the guide. The slides are the visual proof.
- The audience should feel smarter every 3 to 4 minutes.
- All numbers are hypothetical unless replaced with verified, delivery-date figures.

## Act 1 - The Fear Becomes a Map

### Slide 001 - Cold Open: The Question

- Purpose: Open with the audience's real internal question.
- Educational objective: Define the seminar around one decision: "Can I actually buy a home?"
- Emotional objective: Replace embarrassment with permission to ask basic questions.
- Visual objective: A single enormous question on white, with a small MSFG footer.
- Key message: You are not behind. You just have not been shown the map.
- Visual concept: Full-screen type. One small animated home outline appears under the word "home."
- Speaker notes: "Most people think the first question is what rate can I get. It is not. The first question is whether buying fits your life."
- Animation recommendations: Fade in the question, then draw the home outline in one calm stroke.
- Graphic recommendations: Custom thin-line home icon, no photo.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Ask for chat reactions: "confident, unsure, or overwhelmed."
- Questions to ask: "Which word best describes how homebuying feels right now?"
- Transition to next slide: "Before we talk about rates, we need to talk about the feeling behind the question."

### Slide 002 - The Room Is Not Behind

- Purpose: Normalize the audience's uncertainty.
- Educational objective: Explain that first-time buyers are competing in a process built for repeat players.
- Emotional objective: Make the audience feel seen instead of judged.
- Visual objective: Show one first-time buyer icon facing a room of professional icons: lender, realtor, seller, underwriter, title.
- Key message: You are doing this once. Everyone else does it every day.
- Visual concept: Minimal relationship map with the buyer centered and every professional orbiting around them.
- Speaker notes: "This does not mean the process is against you. It means you deserve translation."
- Animation recommendations: Buyer appears first, then each professional role fades in around the buyer.
- Graphic recommendations: Use one consistent outline icon family.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Quick poll: "Have you talked to a lender before?"
- Questions to ask: "Who in this map feels most intimidating?"
- Transition to next slide: "So here is the promise for the next 42 minutes."

### Slide 003 - The Promise

- Educational objective: Preview the eight decisions the buyer will understand.
- Emotional objective: Create confidence that the seminar has a clear structure.

## Act 2 - The Monthly Number

- Key message: You do not live inside a purchase price. You live inside a monthly payment.
- Visual concept: Large home price dissolves into a vertical stack: principal, interest, taxes, insurance, mortgage insurance.
- Speaker notes: "When buyers get surprised, it is usually because someone let them think price was the whole story."
- Animation recommendations: Morph price into payment stack segments.
- Graphic recommendations: Reusable payment stack infographic.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Ask which payment piece they have heard of before.
- Questions to ask: "Which part of the monthly payment surprises people most?"
- Transition to next slide: "Let's name each part before we calculate it."

### Slide 009 - The Payment Stack

- Educational objective: Define principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and mortgage insurance in plain language.
- Emotional objective: Make the payment feel explainable.
- Visual objective: Show a labeled stack with each segment proportional enough to teach, not exact.
- Key message: The payment is five jobs wearing one number.
- Visual concept: Tall clean stack with short labels and tiny definitions.
- Speaker notes: "Principal is the loan going down. Interest is the cost of borrowing. Taxes and insurance protect the property and community. Mortgage insurance may be temporary."

- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Ask viewers which item they did not know was included.
- Questions to ask: "When you hear a monthly payment online, which of these might be missing?"

## Act 3 - Approved vs Comfortable

### Slide 013 - Approved Is Not the Same as Ready

- Purpose: Separate lender approval from household comfort.
- Educational objective: Explain that approval is a ceiling, not a recommendation.
- Emotional objective: Build trust by saying the uncomfortable truth.
- Visual objective: Show two lines: "maximum approved" above "comfortable plan."
- Key message: The lender can approve a ceiling. You still need a floor plan.
- Visual concept: Architectural floor-plan metaphor with a red ceiling line and green comfort zone.
- Speaker notes: "Our job is not to cheer every approval. It is to help you choose a payment you can live with."
- Animation recommendations: Approval ceiling drops in, comfort zone draws beneath it.
- Graphic recommendations: Custom home cross-section diagram.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Poll: "Would you use the maximum a lender approved?"
- Questions to ask: "What would make a payment uncomfortable even if the lender said yes?"
- Transition to next slide: "The industry has a ratio for the ceiling."

## Act 4 - Cash To Start

### Slide 019 - The 20 Percent Myth

- Purpose: Break the largest false blocker.
- Educational objective: Teach that many first-time buyers do not need 20 percent down.
- Emotional objective: Create possibility without overpromising.
- Visual objective: Show "20%" shattering into smaller minimum examples.
- Key message: Twenty percent is one path. It is not the entrance fee.
- Visual concept: Large 20 percent block cracks into 0, 3, 3.5, and assistance labels.
- Speaker notes: "Twenty percent can be a great strategy. It is not the universal requirement people think it is."
- Animation recommendations: Poll answer appears, then real examples reveal.
- Graphic recommendations: Use program labels carefully and verify before delivery.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Poll: "What down payment do you think is required?"
- Questions to ask: "Who has heard 20 percent from family, friends, or the internet?"
- Transition to next slide: "Lower down payment does not mean no strategy. It means choosing the right path."

### Slide 020 - Loan Program Ladder

- Purpose: Compare major loan program categories without a table dump.
- Educational objective: Explain that loan programs solve different buyer situations.
- Emotional objective: Reduce program confusion.
- Visual objective: Show a ladder with program rungs: Conventional, FHA, VA, USDA, DPA.
- Key message: The best program depends on the buyer, not the loudest headline.
- Visual concept: Vertical ladder where each rung has one buyer-fit label.
- Speaker notes: "A program is a tool. You would not pick a tool because it sounds nice. You pick it because it fits the job."
- Animation recommendations: Build the ladder rung by rung.
- Graphic recommendations: Use consistent simple icons: shield for VA, home for FHA, map pin for USDA, hand for DPA.
- Opportunities for live audience interaction: Ask attendees which program they have heard of.
- Questions to ask: "Which program name feels most confusing?"
- Transition to next slide: "Assistance deserves its own moment because the rules can be local."

### Slide 021 - Assistance Is a Map, Not a Promise

### Slide 032 - Buydown: Discount or Strategy?

- Purpose: Introduce interest rate buydowns.
- Educational objective: Explain paying upfront to lower the rate and payment.
- Emotional objective: Stop buyers from assuming lower payment is automatically better.
- Visual objective: Show cash now trading for monthly savings later.
- Key message: A buydown is a trade. The question is when it pays you back.
- Visual concept: Scale: upfront cost on one side, monthly savings on the other.
- Speaker notes: "If someone presents a buydown without a break-even point, they are giving you half the analysis."

## Production Notes

- Use the official MSFG palette only.
- Keep body text under 25 words on slide unless the slide is a worksheet or downloadable artifact.
- Use live calculator embeds only after the presenter has framed the question.
- Every calculator needs a static fallback slide.
- Every number-heavy slide needs the educational disclaimer.
- Verify program claims, assistance details, and any market-sensitive assumptions before delivery.
- The presenter should never say "you should buy now." The presenter should say "you should understand the numbers before deciding."
