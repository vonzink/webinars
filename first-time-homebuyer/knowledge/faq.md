# FAQ — pre-drafted Q&A answers

For the live Q&A block (40:00–55:00) and the follow-up email.

**Answer discipline:** ≤60 seconds each. One long answer starves ten people. If
it's file-specific, say so and take it offline — that's a real answer, not a dodge.

---

## Getting started

**Q: How much do I need in savings before I even call a lender?**
Nothing. The call is free and nothing is committed. But for planning: down payment
+ closing costs + prepaids + an emergency fund you do **not** spend on the house.
That last one is the piece people skip and then regret in month four.

**Q: Will talking to a lender hurt my credit?**
A conversation, no. A pre-approval requires a hard pull, which typically has a
small, short-lived effect. And mortgage inquiries inside a shopping window
(commonly described as 14–45 days depending on the scoring model) count as one.
The system was built so you could shop.

**Q: How long does the whole thing take?**
From serious start to keys: often 2–6 months, most of which is house hunting.
Contract to closing is typically 30–45 days.

**Q: Do I need a real estate agent?**
Not legally. Practically, as a first-time buyer, yes. Ask directly how they're
compensated and read your buyer agreement — that has changed in recent years and
you should understand what you're signing.

## Money

**Q: What if my income is different every month?**
Variable income — commission, bonus, overtime, self-employment — is usually
averaged over 24 months, and generally has to be stable or increasing. A declining
average is a problem. If you're commissioned, get a lender to look at it *early*,
because how it's calculated surprises people.

**Q: I'm self-employed. Am I doomed?**
No, but the arithmetic is different: you qualify on **net income after write-offs**,
not gross revenue. A great year written down to nothing for taxes qualifies as
nothing. Talk to a lender the year *before* you buy, so you can plan it with your
CPA rather than discover it.

**Q: Can my parents give me the down payment?**
Usually yes on most programs, with a documented gift letter and a paper trail
showing where the money came from and that it isn't a loan. **Tell your lender
before it hits your account.** Undocumented deposits turn into a two-week chase.

**Q: What's earnest money and do I get it back?**
A deposit showing you're serious, usually 1–3%, held by a neutral party and
credited toward your cash to close. It's refundable if you exit within your
contract contingencies and their deadlines. Blow a deadline and it may not be.
Deadlines are contractual dates, not suggestions.

**Q: Do I have to put the same amount down as my pre-approval says?**
No. Down payment is a decision you can change up to closing, within program
minimums. More down = lower payment and possibly no MI; less down = more cash in
your pocket. There's no single right answer.

**Q: Should I use my 401(k)?**
Possible via loan or withdrawal, and there are real tax and penalty consequences
plus the opportunity cost of money out of the market. It's sometimes right and
often not. This is a CPA and financial-planner question, not a mortgage question —
get a real answer before you do it.

## Programs & down payment

**Q: What counts as a "first-time homebuyer"?**
Most programs define it as: no ownership interest in a primary residence in the
**last three years**. So you can have owned before and qualify again. This
surprises a lot of people out of assistance they're eligible for.

**Q: Is FHA worse than conventional?**
Different, not worse. FHA is more forgiving on credit and often prices better in
the 600s. The tradeoff is that on most FHA loans with under 10% down, mortgage
insurance lasts the life of the loan, while conventional MI is removable. Run both.

**Q: Can I use down payment assistance with any loan?**
Often, but not universally — it depends on the specific program and the first
mortgage. Have your lender run the layers (state / metro / county / employer) for
your county and income. Terms and funding change, so check on the day you're
actually shopping.

**Q: I'm a veteran but I used my VA loan before. Can I use it again?**
Almost certainly yes. VA entitlement is not once-per-lifetime — it restores. Get
your Certificate of Eligibility and find out where you stand.

## Rates, points, APR

**Q: Should I pay points?**
Compute the breakeven: cost of points ÷ monthly savings = months. If you won't
clearly keep *this exact loan* past that, don't. And note it's the loan, not the
house — refinancing ends the loan even if you never move.

**Q: When should I lock my rate?**
Generally once you're under contract, for a period that covers your closing date
with room. Longer locks usually cost more. On new construction with a long
timeline, ask specifically about extended locks and what an extension costs — that
is a real expense people don't see coming.

**Q: What if rates drop after I lock?**
Depends on the lender — some offer a one-time float-down under conditions. Ask
*before* you lock, because "do you offer a float-down" is a question with a real
dollar value and it's free to ask.

**Q: Fifteen-year or thirty-year?**
Fifteen has a lower rate and far less total interest but a much higher required
payment. A common middle path: take the 30 and pay it like a 15 when you can —
you keep the flexibility to stop in a bad month. See the $100 experiment.

**Q: ARM or fixed?**
For most first-time buyers, fixed. An ARM can make sense if you have a genuinely
short, known horizon, but "I'll probably move" is not the same as knowing.

## Process

**Q: What's the difference between pre-qualified and pre-approved?**
Prequal is a conversation. Pre-approval involves a credit pull and collected
documentation. The strongest version is a fully underwritten approval, where a
human underwriter has already reviewed you and only the house is left. Ask for it
by name if you'll be competing.

**Q: What happens if the appraisal comes in low?**
Options: seller lowers the price, you bring the difference in cash, you split it,
or you exit under an appraisal contingency if you have one and it's within its
deadline. Know before you write the offer which of those you can actually do.

**Q: Do I need a home inspection on new construction?**
Yes. New houses are built by humans on deadlines. Ideally inspect twice — before
drywall and before closing.

**Q: What's a rate lock extension going to cost me?**
Varies by lender and length; it is not free. Ask up front, especially on new
construction where delays are normal.

## Credit

**Q: What score do I need?**
Depends on the program. FHA goes meaningfully lower than conventional; VA has no
industry-wide minimum though lenders set overlays. There's no single number, and
lender overlays vary — which is one of the arguments for a broker who can shop it.

**Q: Should I pay off all my debt before applying?**
Not automatically. Paying down **revolving** balances helps utilization fast.
Paying off a car with your down payment money can hurt more than the DTI
improvement helps, because you need the cash. Run it with a lender before you move
money.

**Q: I have a collection on my report. Am I done?**
No. Treatment varies by program and by whether it's medical, and by amount. This is
exactly the kind of thing that feels disqualifying and often isn't.

## The hard ones — answer honestly

**Q: Should I buy right now?**
I don't know, and anyone who answers that confidently for a room of strangers is
selling something. What I'd ask: is your income stable, do you have reserves
*after* closing, and will you be here 3+ years? If all three are yes, you're in a
reasonable position. If one is no, that's worth examining before the market is.

**Q: Am I going to regret this?**
Most people regret **overbuying**, not buying. Almost nobody says "I wish I'd
bought a bigger house." A lot of people say "I wish we hadn't stretched." That's
what Priya's story is about.

**Q: What's the catch with your free Loan Estimate review?**
There isn't one, and you're right to ask. If I tell you your other lender's offer
is good and it costs me the deal, you'll remember who told you the truth. That's
the whole strategy. It's a slow one.
