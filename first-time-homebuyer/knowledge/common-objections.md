# Common Objections

The audience is silently arguing with you the entire time. An objection that never
gets named never gets resolved — it just quietly discounts everything after it.

**The rule:** name the objection *before* the audience thinks it. Pre-empting an
objection is worth roughly ten times what answering it later is worth, because
answering later looks defensive and pre-empting looks honest.

Two of these cut against Zach's own financial interest. Those are the most
valuable ones in the file. **Do not soften them.**

---

## OBJECTION 1 — "You're a mortgage broker. Of course you say this."
**Felt from:** minute 0 · **Address at:** slide 002, reinforce at 067

> "State it before they think it. 'My bias, up front: I make money when people get
> mortgages. So here's how you check my work — everything I teach you today is
> verifiable against a government form that every lender in America has to give
> you. You don't have to trust me. You have to be able to read page two.'"

**Reinforcement (slide 067):** telling the story where the builder's lender beats
him and he says so. One demonstration of arguing against your own interest is
worth more than an hour of assurance.

---

## OBJECTION 2 — "Everyone says rates are going to drop. I should wait." ⭐
**Felt from:** minute 18 · **Address at:** slide 041

> "Nobody knows. Not me. Every forecast for the last several years has been
> revised, usually more than once. So any plan that requires being right about
> rate direction isn't a plan — it's a wager."

> "What I can tell you is the structural thing: **rates are refinanceable. Purchase
> prices are not.** If you buy at a higher rate and rates fall, you can refinance —
> and no, that's not guaranteed and nobody should promise it. But if you wait and
> prices rise while rates fall, you've traded a fixable problem for a permanent
> one. That's a real asymmetry and it's worth understanding."

> "**And the honest other side:** if you're not financially ready, waiting is
> correct, and no rate environment changes that. Timing the market is a bad reason
> to buy. Being ready is a good one."

Never say "buy now." Never say "rates are going up." Both are trigger phrases for
exactly the urgency this webinar promised not to use.

---

## OBJECTION 3 — "My parents/coworker said I need 20% down."
**Felt from:** minute 13 · **Address at:** slides 032–033

> "They're not lying to you. They're telling you what was true for them, and 20%
> genuinely does avoid mortgage insurance. It just isn't *required*, and treating
> it as required has cost a generation of buyers more in rent than it saved them
> in PMI."

Never make the audience defend a family member. Frame it as *outdated, not wrong.*

---

## OBJECTION 4 — "Renting is throwing money away, right?"
**Felt from:** minute 7 · **Address at:** slide 004, again in Q&A

> "No — and be careful with that phrase, it's usually deployed to make people feel
> bad. Renting buys you flexibility and a capped, predictable cost. Owning buys
> you equity and an uncapped, unpredictable one. Neither is throwing anything away."

> "The honest version: **owning tends to win over long horizons and can absolutely
> lose over short ones.** If there's a real chance you move in under 3 years, the
> transaction costs alone — buying and selling — often eat any equity you'd build.
> Renting isn't a failure state. Sometimes it's the correct answer, and I'd rather
> tell you that now than after you're in escrow."

---

## OBJECTION 5 — "I'll get denied. Why embarrass myself?" ⭐
**Felt from:** minute 4 · **Address at:** slide 011

The most common **unspoken** objection in the room, and the one that costs the
audience the most.

> "'No' is almost never the answer. **'Not yet, and here's exactly what changes
> it'** is the answer. In my experience the average 'not yet' is three to nine
> months of specific, boring, do-able steps. The only way it becomes three years
> is if you never make the call."

Reinforce at slide 069 — this objection *is* the most expensive mistake.

---

## OBJECTION 6 — "The builder's incentive is way better than shopping around."
**Felt from:** minute 33 · **Address at:** slide 067

Answered by *conceding it*. Story 8 shows the builder winning by ~$6,100 and Zach
telling the client to take it.

> "Sometimes it wins. What's never right is taking it without running the
> comparison — and the reason builders can offer these at all is that hardly
> anyone runs it. It takes ten minutes and you learned the skill six minutes ago."

---

## OBJECTION 7 — "This is a lead magnet. The real pitch is coming."
**Felt from:** minute 0, decays if slide 005 lands · **Address at:** 005, resolved at 070

Slide 005 makes five specific promises. Slide 070 keeps them: downloads with no
form, and Door 1 is explicitly "take the kit and leave."

> "If I break one of those, call it out in the chat. Seriously."

Inviting the audit is what makes the promise credible. The resolution is behavioral
— gating the downloads would falsify the entire webinar retroactively.

## OBJECTION 8 — "My credit is bad. This isn't for me."
**Felt from:** minute 30 · **Address at:** slide 063

> "You don't need a 780. Pricing moves in tiers, not smoothly — 735 to 745 can
> matter, 780 to 800 usually does nothing. And utilization is 30% of the score and
> can move in one billing cycle. Find out which tier you're in and whether you're
> close to the next one. That's a five-minute question, and the answer is more
> often 'you're closer than you think' than people expect."

---

## OBJECTION 9 — "I don't have time for a 20-minute call."
**Felt from:** minute 39 · **Address at:** slide 070

Answered structurally — Door 2 requires no call at all. Forward a PDF, get it
marked up. The low-friction door exists precisely for this objection.

## Objections that must NOT be answered live

| Objection | Why | What to say |
|---|---|---|
| "What rate would I get?" | Compliance. A rate to a room is a fiction. | *"I won't quote a rate on camera — anyone who does is quoting something you may not qualify for. Slide 42 is why."* |
| "Is [specific lender] good?" | Defamation risk, and it's not the lesson | *"I won't grade competitors. Send me their Loan Estimate and I'll show you how to grade it yourself."* |
| "Should I buy this specific house?" | Not your lane | *"That's a conversation with your agent and your own numbers. Happy to run the financing side."* |
| "Can I write off ___?" | Tax advice | *"That's a CPA question, and get a real answer — the general internet answer is wrong more often than it's right."* |
