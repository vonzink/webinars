# Analogies

An analogy is a loan against something the audience already understands. Interest
comes due when the analogy breaks — so every one here has a **stated breaking
point.** Name the limit when you use it, or someone will over-apply it and be
confused later.

**Rule:** one analogy per concept. Two analogies for the same idea is worse than
none — the audience spends their attention reconciling them instead of learning.

---

## ⭐ APR = sticker price vs. out-the-door price
**Slide 044**

The interest rate is the sticker on the windshield. APR is closer to the number on
the final invoice — it folds in certain lender costs. Two dealers can advertise the
same sticker and hand you very different totals.

**Breaks when:** APR assumes you keep the car for its entire life. Nobody does. That
break *is* the teaching point on slide 046.

---

## ⭐ Escrow = a subscription your lender runs for you
**Slide 021**

They collect a twelfth of your taxes and insurance each month, hold it, and pay the
bills when they come due.

**Breaks when:** it's not their money and it's not a fee. And unlike a subscription,
the price changes without asking you — usually once a year, usually up.

---

## ⭐ DTI = a suitcase weight limit
**Slide 024**

The airline doesn't care what's in the bag. It cares what it weighs. Underwriting
doesn't care whether that $500 is a car you love or a car you regret.

**Breaks when:** unlike an airline, they weigh *some* items and not others.
Childcare can be $1,500/month and weighs zero. Your actual life weighs everything.

---

## ⭐ Points = an annual gym membership instead of monthly
**Slide 048**

Pay upfront, pay less per month, come out ahead only if you stay long enough.

**Breaks when:** the gym membership expires; a mortgage buydown lasts as long as
the loan does. That's why the breakeven is in months, not years.

---

## ⭐ Amortization = a seesaw
**Slide 051**

Early on, interest sits heavy on one end. Every payment moves a little weight
across. On Maya and Dev's loan the seesaw doesn't level until **month 218**.

**Breaks when:** unlike a seesaw, you can push. That's the $100 experiment.

---

## Pre-approval = a passport
**Slide 012**

Showing up to write an offer without one is showing up at the airport with a
library card. Correct name, wrong document, and the person at the counter cannot
help you.

**Breaks when:** a passport doesn't expire in 60–90 days. Pre-approvals do.

---

## Underwriting = TSA for your money
**Slide 014**

They're not accusing you of anything. Everyone goes through it. They will ask you
to take out the same laptop you already showed the last person — because it's a
checklist, and the checklist doesn't know what you already did.

**Breaks when:** unlike TSA, you can call them and ask what's taking so long. Do.

---

## Credit score = a GPA, not a report card
**Slide 063**

A running average, not a snapshot. One bad semester doesn't erase four good ones,
but it does drag the average, and the average recovers slowly.

**Breaks when:** GPA weights all semesters equally. Credit weights recent behavior
far more heavily — which is good news if you're recovering from something.

---

## Rate lock = a hotel reservation
**Slide 042 / Q&A**

You've reserved a price for a window of time. Show up inside the window and it's
honored. Show up late and you're paying whatever tonight's rate is — and extending
the reservation costs money.

**Breaks when:** you can't cancel a lock and rebook for free the way you can a
hotel. Some lenders offer a float-down; most don't unless you ask.

---

## Section A of the Loan Estimate = the labor line on a repair estimate
**Slide 056**

Parts cost what parts cost — everyone pays roughly the same for the appraisal, the
recording fee, the taxes. **Labor is where shops differ.** Section A is the labor
line, and it's the only line where the lender is genuinely competing.

**Breaks when:** Section C (title, settlement) is *also* shoppable and most people
never do. Two shoppable lines, not one.

---

## The bank's budget vs. your budget = a doctor clearing you for a marathon
**Slide 020**

The physical says your heart can take it. It doesn't say you'll enjoy mile 19, or
that you have time to train. Approved is a medical clearance, not a training plan.

**Breaks when:** don't push this one. It runs out fast. Say it once and move.

## Retired — do not use

- **"Your house is your biggest investment."** It's a house. Framing it as an
  investment leads people to overbuy and to treat a home as a leveraged bet. Say
  *"the largest purchase of your life"* instead — the exact phrasing used on slide 001.
- **"Renting is throwing money away."** Manipulative and not true. See
  `common-objections.md` #4.
- **"Marry the house, date the rate."** It's clever, it's everywhere, and it
  smuggles in a promise nobody can make — that a refinance will be available. The
  moment you use it you've sold urgency dressed as wisdom. Banned.
- **"Mortgage = death pledge" etymology.** Fun trivia, zero teaching value, and it
  frightens the audience for no reason.
