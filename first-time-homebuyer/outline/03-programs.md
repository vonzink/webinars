# Module 4 (file 03) — Down Payment & Programs

**Time:** 13:00 – 18:00 (5:00) · **Slides:** 030–039
**Delivery position:** FOURTH, after affordability.
**Job:** Demolish the 20% myth with evidence, then give an honest, tradeoff-aware
tour of the programs — including where each one is genuinely the wrong choice.
**Artifact:** The Program Decision Tree

---

## The one thing this module must accomplish

More first-time buyers are stopped by the belief that they need 20% down than by
any actual underwriting constraint. This module exists to remove that belief and
replace it with a *correctly calibrated* one — not "put nothing down," but "here
is what each option actually costs, including the ones that cost more."

The diagnostic poll (031) before the reveal (032) is the mechanism. Letting them
be publicly wrong first is what makes the correction stick.

### 13:20 — Slide 032 — THE REVEAL

|---|---|---|

| FHA | **3.5%** | **$15,750** |

> "So — a chunk of this room just told me they need $90,000. The real number, for

> "Where did 20% come from? It's not made up. **At 20% down you avoid mortgage
> insurance.** That's a genuine benefit and it's the origin of the number.

### 14:00 — Slide 033 — Story 3: Tom & Ashley's six years

> "Tom and Ashley decided they'd do it right. Twenty percent, no mortgage
> insurance, no shortcuts. They put their heads down and they saved."

> "It took six years. And they did it — they hit their number. I want to be clear
> that I admire the discipline, because it is real discipline."

> "But here's what happened during those six years. Home prices in their target
> area went up. So the 20% target went up with them — it's a percentage of a
> moving number. Every year they saved, the finish line moved. And they paid six
> more years of rent, which bought them exactly zero equity."

> "Meanwhile, they *were* eligible for a 3%-down conventional loan the entire
> time. They'd have paid mortgage insurance — call it a bit over a hundred dollars
> a month — for a handful of years until they hit 20% equity through payments and
> appreciation, and then it would have come off. Permanently."

> "**They spent six years avoiding a temporary fee.**"

> "Now — the honest counterweight, because I said I wouldn't sell you anything.
> This is not a law of nature. If they'd bought right before a downturn, the story
> reads differently. Waiting is not always wrong. **But it should be a decision,
> not a default.** Theirs was a default. They never ran the comparison, because
> nobody ever told them there was one to run."

### 14:40 — Slide 034 — Conventional at 3%

**Conventional 97** — 3% down, 30-year fixed, at least one borrower typically a
first-time buyer (defined as: no ownership interest in a primary residence in the
last three years — which surprises people who owned something a decade ago).

**HomeReady** (Fannie Mae) and **Home Possible** (Freddie Mac) — 3% down, income
limits by area, and the meaningful part: **reduced mortgage insurance coverage and
better pricing** than standard conventional at the same LTV. Also allow flexible
income sources — boarder income, non-occupant co-borrowers — in ways standard
conventional doesn't.

> "If you're income-eligible, these are frequently the best product available to a
> first-time buyer, and they are chronically under-mentioned. Homebuyer education
> is generally required. It's an online course. Do it early — it takes a couple of
> hours and it's a condition that holds up closings when people leave it to the
> end."

**The conventional advantage that matters most:** the mortgage insurance is
**temporary and removable.** Hold that thought for slide 039.

### 15:15 — Slide 035 — FHA

**3.5% down with a 580+ score.** Government-insured, more forgiving on credit
history, collections, and past derogatory events. Higher DTI tolerance in many
scenarios. It is a genuinely great program and it has helped an enormous number of
people become owners.

**The tradeoff nobody mentions at the sales table:**

- **Upfront MIP** — a percentage of the loan financed into the balance
- **Annual MIP** — paid monthly, and here's the part: **on most FHA loans with
  less than 10% down, MIP lasts the life of the loan.** Not until 20% equity.
  The life of the loan. Put 10% or more down and it drops off after 11 years.

> "So the honest framing: FHA's monthly cost is often competitive, sometimes
> better than conventional — especially if your credit is in the 600s, where
> conventional pricing gets expensive fast. But conventional's mortgage insurance
> has an exit and FHA's usually doesn't."

> "Which means for a lot of FHA buyers the plan isn't 'FHA forever.' It's **'FHA
> to get in, refinance out when it makes sense.'** That's a completely legitimate
> strategy. It's just one you should choose on purpose, knowing the exit is a
> future refinance that depends on future rates. Nobody can promise you that
> refinance. Anyone who does is selling."

### 15:50 — Slide 036 — Story 4 + VA

> "A few years back I was on a first call with a guy — good job, decent credit,
> scraping to get to 3.5% for an FHA loan. Halfway through, totally offhand, he
> mentioned he'd 'done four years in the Army after high school.'"

> "I stopped the call. I said — you know you can buy this house with nothing down,
> right? And no monthly mortgage insurance at all?"

> "He didn't. He was 34. Nobody had ever asked him."

**VA loans:**
- **0% down**, no monthly mortgage insurance — the two biggest costs, gone
- Competitive rates, and generally more forgiving underwriting
- Qualifies on **residual income** — cash left after all obligations, by family
  size and region — which is a more humane test than a pure ratio
- A **funding fee** applies (percentage varies by down payment and whether it's a
  first use), financeable into the loan — and **waived** for veterans receiving
  compensation for a service-connected disability
- **Reusable.** It is not once-per-lifetime. Entitlement restores.

> "If you served — active duty, National Guard, Reserves, or you're a surviving
> spouse — get your Certificate of Eligibility. And if you're sitting there
> thinking 'it was only a few years, it probably doesn't count' — **ask anyway.**
> That's the whole lesson of that story. He almost paid tens of thousands of
> dollars for the privilege of not asking a question."

### 16:20 — Slide 037 — USDA `[CUT-2]`

**0% down**, for properties in USDA-eligible areas with household income under the
area limit.

> "Two things people get wrong. First, 'rural' does not mean a farm. A lot of
> Colorado towns that feel like normal towns are eligible. Second — the boundary
> is a **map**, and the map is specific, and it does get updated. Check the
> address, not the vibe."

Tradeoffs: an upfront guarantee fee and an annual fee (lower than FHA's MIP in
many cases), household income limits that count everyone in the house, and a
property that has to meet condition standards.

### 16:45 — Slide 038 — Colorado: CHFA & down payment assistance `[EXP-3]`

> "This is the part you cannot find on a national website, and it's why I made a
> Colorado-specific slide."

**CHFA** — the Colorado Housing and Finance Authority — offers first-mortgage
programs paired with **down payment assistance**, delivered as either a grant or a
second mortgage, layered on top of conventional/FHA/VA/USDA first loans. Income and
purchase-price limits apply by county. Homebuyer education is required.

**Four layers to check, in this order:**
1. **State** — CHFA
2. **Metro / city** — several Front Range municipalities run their own programs
3. **County** — varies widely
4. **Employer / profession** — teachers, first responders, healthcare, some large
   employers; also union and credit-union programs

> "Here's my obligation to you: **program terms, income limits, and available
> funding change, and some of these run out of money mid-year.** Anything specific
> I told you today could be stale by the time you act on it. So the artifact isn't
> a list of programs — it's the instruction: *ask your lender to run the DPA
> layers for your county and your income, on the day you're actually shopping.*
> A broker can check across all of them. Ask by name."

*(Verified terms live in `research/chfa.md`, re-checked before each delivery.)*

### 17:20 — Slide 039 — Mortgage insurance & the decision tree

**Four kinds of mortgage insurance, and they are not interchangeable:**

| Type | Where | Removable? |
|---|---|---|
| **BPMI** (borrower-paid monthly) | Conventional | **Yes** — the important one |
| **LPMI** (lender-paid, baked into a higher rate) | Conventional | **No** — only via refinance |
| **FHA MIP** | FHA | Usually **no** at <10% down — life of loan |
| **VA funding fee / USDA guarantee fee** | VA / USDA | One-time upfront + USDA annual |

**How to kill BPMI — the three routes:**
1. **Request removal at 80% LTV** — based on the original value, once you've paid
   down to 80%. You have to ask. Nobody calls you.
2. **Automatic termination at 78% LTV** — by the original amortization schedule,
   primary residence, payments current. Federal law (Homeowners Protection Act).
3. **Appreciation-based removal** — new appraisal showing you've reached the
   equity threshold. **Servicer and investor rules vary**, including seasoning
   requirements. Call your servicer and ask for their specific policy in writing.

> "On Maya and Dev's loan: 80% of $450,000 is $360,000. They start at $427,500.
> They need $67,500 of paydown — or an appraisal that says the house went up.
> That $125 a month is not forever. It's a phase. Knowing that changes the whole
> emotional weight of it."

**ARTIFACT #3 — The Decision Tree (5 questions):**
1. Did you or your spouse serve? → **VA**, full stop, check it first
2. Is the property in a USDA-eligible area and are you under the income limit? → **USDA**
3. Is your credit under about 660, or do you have recent derogatory history? → **FHA** is likely
4. Are you under the HomeReady/Home Possible income limit for your area? → **that**, likely best
5. None of the above? → **Conventional 3–5% down**, and compare against FHA on total cost

> "Then, whatever the tree says: **layer down payment assistance on top.** DPA is
> not a program you pick instead. It's a floor you stack under the one you picked."

## Compliance — highest-risk module

- Every program figure is a **general guideline, not an approval or a quote**.
- Down payment minimums are program minimums; **lender overlays, credit, and AUS
  findings govern actual eligibility.**
- FHA MIP duration, VA funding fee percentages, and USDA fees change by policy —
  all carry `VERIFY` flags in `research/`.
- CHFA/DPA availability must be re-verified on the delivery date. The slide is
  intentionally framed as *how to check* rather than *what is true today*.
- MI removal: HPA applies to primary residences; appreciation-based removal is
  servicer/investor discretion. Do not state it as an entitlement.
