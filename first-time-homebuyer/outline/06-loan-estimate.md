# Module 6 — The Loan Estimate, Closing Costs & Prepaids

**Time:** 24:30 – 30:00 (5:30) · **Slides:** 052–060
**Job:** Hand them the ability to catch overcharging themselves. This is the
module that most directly serves "I'm being taken advantage of and I can't tell."
**Artifact:** The 60-Second Two-Lender Comparison
**Exercise:** Spot the Difference (057)

---

## Why this is the most valuable module in the webinar

Everything else teaches understanding. This teaches **verification.** After this
module the audience can independently check any lender's work, including mine.
That is the highest-trust thing a loan officer can do on camera, and it's the
reason the no-strings CTA at the end is credible rather than cute.

## Beat sheet

### 24:35 — Slide 053 — Story 5: Same rate, $6,400 apart

> "A client sent me two Loan Estimates and asked which was better. Both 30-year
> fixed. Both the same loan amount. **Both exactly the same interest rate.**"

> "She'd already decided it was a coin flip. Same rate, right?"

> "Section A on the first one — that's origination charges, the lender's own fees
> — was about $1,300. Section A on the second was about **$7,700**. Same rate.
> **Six thousand four hundred dollars apart**, sitting on page two, in a box
> neither of them had drawn her attention to."

> "She wasn't being defrauded. Every number was disclosed correctly, on the
> government form, exactly as required. The disclosure worked perfectly. **The
> disclosure only works if somebody reads page two.**"

> "In the next four minutes you're going to learn to read page two. It is the
> highest-paid four minutes of this webinar."

### 25:15 — Slide 054 — What this document is

**The Loan Estimate.** Standardized, three pages, required by federal rule (the
TRID rules that took effect in 2015). Every lender in America must give you the
same form, in the same order, with the same boxes.

> "That's the gift. Before this existed, every lender had their own layout, which
> made comparison basically impossible. Now they're identical, which means
> comparison is trivial — *if you know where to look.*"

**Two things you're owed:**
1. You must receive it **within 3 business days** of a completed application.
2. Certain costs are subject to **tolerance limits** — they can't just go up. Some
   can't increase at all without a valid changed circumstance; others are limited
   in aggregate. If they do, the lender generally has to cure the difference.

> "And the sentence that matters most: **asking for a Loan Estimate does not
> obligate you to anything.** It's not a commitment. It's not a hard sell.
> Get three. Genuinely, get three."

### 25:45 — Slide 055 — Page 1: the five boxes

1. **Loan Terms** — amount, rate, monthly P&I, and a set of yes/no columns:
   *can this increase after closing?* Prepayment penalty? Balloon payment?
   > "Those yes/no answers should all be the boring answer on a standard
   > 30-year fixed. If any of them surprise you, stop and ask why."
2. **Projected Payments** — the payment over time, including MI, and when MI drops
3. **Estimated Taxes, Insurance & Assessments** — the escrow line
4. **Costs at Closing** — two numbers: *Estimated Closing Costs* and
   *Estimated Cash to Close.* **These are different numbers.** (Slide 059.)
5. The rate-lock statement — is this rate locked, and until when

> "If you compare two Loan Estimates that were issued on different days, you're
> not comparing lenders — you're comparing days. Same day. Ask for it."

### 26:25 — Slide 056 — Page 2: the letters `[EXP-2]`

| Section | What it is | Can you shop it? |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Origination Charges** | The lender's own fees + points | **No — but you can shop lenders. This is the box.** |
| **B. Services You Cannot Shop For** | Appraisal, credit report, flood cert | No |
| **C. Services You CAN Shop For** | Title, settlement, survey, pest | **Yes — and almost nobody does** |
| **E. Taxes & Government Fees** | Recording, transfer taxes | No |
| **F. Prepaids** | Prepaid interest, first year's insurance | Not really |
| **G. Initial Escrow Deposit** | The cushion your servicer holds | No |
| **H. Other** | Owner's title policy, home warranty | Sometimes |

> "**A and C. That's it.** Section A is where lenders differ from each other.
> Section C is where you have leverage nobody uses — the lender hands you a
> Written List of Providers, and you are allowed to go get your own. Title fees
> can vary by four figures between companies in the same city."

> "D through G are mostly not the lender's doing. Your property taxes are your
> property taxes. Nobody's competing on those."

### 27:55 — Slide 058 — Prepaids and escrows are not closing costs

> "These get lumped together and they're completely different animals."

**Closing costs** — money you spend to *get the loan.* Origination, appraisal,
title, recording. Gone. Bought a service.

**Prepaids and escrows** — money that is **still yours**, paid early:
- **Prepaid interest** — interest from your closing date to the end of that month.
  Close on the 28th and it's a few days. Close on the 3rd and it's nearly a month.
- **First year's homeowners insurance** — paid in full at closing
- **Escrow reserves** — a few months of taxes and insurance so the account has a
  cushion

> "That's not a fee. That's your own tax and insurance money, prefunded. You were
> always going to pay it. **You're not losing it — you're front-loading it.**"

> "Real tactic: **closing later in the month reduces prepaid interest.** On this
> loan it's about $76 a day. Close on the 28th instead of the 3rd and you've moved
> roughly $1,900 of cash-to-close. It doesn't save you money in total — you owe
> the interest either way — but if cash at the table is your binding constraint,
> it's real. Almost nobody tells first-time buyers this."

### 28:30 — Slide 059 — Story 6: $5,400 vs. $31,760

> "A buyer told me, very confidently, that his closing costs were $5,400. He was
> right. It was on page one. He'd read it."

> "He showed up at the title company with a cashier's check and was **four thousand
> one hundred dollars short.** In the parking lot. Calling his brother."

Maya & Dev's real cash to close:

| | |
|---|---|
| Down payment | $22,500 |
| Closing costs | $5,400 |
| Prepaid interest (~15 days) | $1,142 |
| Homeowners insurance, 12 months | $1,800 |
| Escrow reserves | $918 |
| **Total cash to close** | **$31,760** |
| Less earnest money already paid | −$5,000 |
| **Due at the table** | **$26,760** |

> "Closing costs: $5,400. Cash to close: $31,760. **Those are not the same word,
> and they are twenty-six thousand dollars apart.**"

> "The number you plan your life around is **Cash to Close**, page one, bottom
> right. Not Closing Costs. If you remember one physical location on one piece of
> paper from today, make it that one."

### 29:10 — Slide 060 — ARTIFACT #5 + AMA BREAK 2

**Compare two lenders in 60 seconds:**
1. Both Loan Estimates issued the **same day** (rates move — otherwise you're
   comparing weather)
2. Same **loan amount**, same **program**, same **lock period**
3. Compare **Section A only** — that's the lender
4. Note the **points** on page 1 — a lower rate bought with points is not a lower rate
5. Check **Cash to Close**, page 1 — the real out-of-pocket
6. Ignore B, E, F, G — those aren't the lender competing

> "Sixty seconds. And here's the part that should tell you something about this
> industry: **that's the entire skill.** It took four minutes to teach. It has
> been kept from you for no reason other than that nobody's incentive was to
> teach it."

**AMA Break 2 (45s)** — seeded: *"Does getting multiple Loan Estimates hurt my
credit?"* (Answer: no — that's the 45-day window, coming up in 90 seconds.)
