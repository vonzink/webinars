## Why this module has to be scrupulously fair

The easy version of this module is "builders are trying to trap you." That version
is both wrong and self-serving — I'm an independent broker, so "don't use the
builder's lender" is *literally my business interest.* If I make that argument
without naming my own stake, I've become the thing this webinar was built against.

So: state the bias out loud, then give the honest analysis, including the
conditions under which the builder's lender is the correct choice. That's the
whole module.

## Beat sheet

### 33:35 — Slide 066 — The honest comparison

| | New construction | Existing home |
|---|---|---|
| Price | Often higher per sq ft | Often more negotiable |
| Negotiation | Builders resist price cuts (comps protection) but give **incentives** | Price is genuinely negotiable |
| Condition | New everything, builder warranty | Known quantity, inspectable, real history |
| Timeline | Months. Delays are normal. | 30–45 days |
| Rate risk | **Long lock or float — a real exposure** | Short, predictable |
| Hidden costs | Landscaping, fencing, blinds, appliances, lot premiums | Deferred maintenance you inherit |
| Property taxes | **Often assessed on land only at first, then jump** | Established and predictable |
| Negotiating power | Lower — they have many buyers | Higher — the seller has one house |
| HOA | Usually, and often builder-controlled at first | Varies |

> "Two things people consistently get wrong. First, **builders would usually
> rather give you $20,000 in incentives than take $20,000 off the price** — a
> price cut sets a comp that lowers every other house in the subdivision. So don't
> negotiate price. Negotiate *everything else*, and negotiate hard, because there
> is far more room there than people use."

> "Second, the tax one bites hard. Your first-year property tax bill on new
> construction may be assessed on an **unimproved lot.** Then the house gets
> assessed and your escrow payment jumps — sometimes several hundred dollars —
> and you get a bill for the shortfall. Ask, in writing, what the tax basis will
> be at full assessment. Budget from that number, not the first one."

### 34:20 — Slide 067 — Story 8, the incentive math, and the 12 questions

**STORY 8 — The Builder's $15,000**

> "Client's under contract on a new build. The builder offers **$15,000 toward
> closing costs — if he uses their preferred lender.** He calls me and says, look,
> I like you, but you can't beat fifteen thousand dollars. And honestly? He might
> have been right. So we ran it."

> "The builder's lender: $15,000 credit, rate a quarter point higher than what he
> could get elsewhere, and about $3,000 more in Section A."

> "On his loan, a quarter point was roughly $70 a month. Over the seven years he
> expected to stay: about $5,900. Plus $3,000 more in fees. So the $15,000 credit
> was really worth about **$6,100** against the alternative."

> "**And it still won.** Six thousand dollars is six thousand dollars. He used
> their lender and I told him to."

> "I'm telling you that story on purpose, because I want you to hear it from
> someone with the opposite incentive: **sometimes the builder's lender is the
> right answer.** Sometimes the incentive is bigger than the spread. What is
> *never* right is taking the incentive without running the comparison — and the
> reason builders can offer these at all is that hardly anyone runs it."

> "So the move is simple. **Take the builder's Loan Estimate. Get one outside
> estimate. Compare Section A, and convert the rate difference into dollars over
> how long you'll actually keep the loan.** Same skill you learned six minutes
> ago. That's it. And a builder can't stop you from shopping — if anyone implies
> you're required to use their lender to buy the house, that's worth asking
> pointed questions about."

**ARTIFACT #7 — 12 QUESTIONS TO ASK A BUILDER**
*(full version in the download kit — read 5 aloud, they get all 12)*

1. What exactly is included, and what's an upgrade? Can I have that in writing?
2. What will my property taxes be at **full assessment**, not first-year?
3. What's the HOA today, who controls it, and when does control transfer to owners?
4. What's the lot premium, and what am I getting for it?
5. Are landscaping, fencing, window coverings, and appliances included?
6. What's the incentive if I **don't** use your preferred lender?
7. Can I take a **price reduction** instead of the incentive?
8. What's your delay history on this phase, and what happens to my rate lock?
9. Can I have an **independent inspection** before drywall and again before closing?
10. What is the warranty, exactly — 1 year, 2 years, 10 years structural — and who honors it?
11. What is planned on the empty land behind this lot?
12. What happens to my earnest money if the build is delayed past my lock or my lease ends?

> "Question 6 is the one they don't expect. Question 7 is the one that occasionally
> saves people the most money. And question 9 — **get an independent inspection on
> a brand new house.** People skip it because it's new. New houses are built by
> humans on deadlines. Inspect it."

  a reason — do not cut it.

  arrangements are legal and require disclosure; the point is comparison, not
  accusation.
