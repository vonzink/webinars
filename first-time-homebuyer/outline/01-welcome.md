# Module 1 — Cold Open & Promise

**Time:** 0:00 – 2:30 (2:30) · **Slides:** 001–007
**Job:** Earn the next 38 minutes in the first 20 seconds. Establish that this is
a class, not a pitch — and make that promise falsifiable.

---

## Why this module is shaped this way

Webinar attrition is steepest in minutes 0–3. The instinct is to open with
credentials. That spends the audience's attention on the speaker at exactly the
moment they're deciding whether to stay. So: claim first, credentials second, and
keep the credentials to 25 seconds.

The single most important beat is **slide 005** — naming what I will *not* do.
The audience walked in braced for a sales pitch. Removing the brace is worth more
than any amount of rapport-building.

---

## Beat sheet

### 0:00 — Slide 001 — The hook

> "You are about to make the largest purchase of your life using information you
> got from strangers on the internet, a coworker who bought in 2019, and whatever
> a search engine put in front of you. That's not your fault. Nobody teaches this.
> For the next 40 minutes, somebody's going to."

Pause. Don't fill it.

### 0:20 — Slide 002 — Who I am (25 seconds, hard stop)

> "Zach Zink. I'm a mortgage loan originator with Mountain State Financial Group,
> here in Colorado. I'm an independent broker, which means I shop lenders instead
> of working for one. I also build mortgage software — calculators, tools — and
> you're going to see three of them today, because they explain this better than
> I can with words."

> "Here's my actual bias, stated up front: I think an educated borrower is a
> better client. Not a more profitable one on any single deal — a better one.
> That's the whole business model. That's why this is free and why nothing in the
> next 40 minutes is going to feel like a pitch."

### 0:45 — Slide 003 — The problem

> "The mortgage industry has a habit. It explains just enough to make you feel
> like you need us, and stops right before the part that would let you check our
> work. Every one of you has had a version of this conversation: someone throws
> six acronyms at you, you nod, and then you go home and Google them alone."

> "Two things are true. Mortgages are genuinely complicated. And they are also
> deliberately over-complicated. Today we're only dealing with the first kind."

### 1:05 — Slide 004 — The promise

> "By minute 40, you will know **five numbers, three documents, and one decision.**"

- **Five numbers:** your comfortable payment · your DTI · your real cash to close ·
  your rate's breakeven · your mortgage credit score
- **Three documents:** the pre-approval, the Loan Estimate, the Closing Disclosure
- **One decision:** whether you're ready — and it is genuinely fine if the answer
  is *not yet*.

> "And I want to say that last part clearly, because you will not hear it from
> many people in my job: **for some of you, the right answer today is to keep
> renting.** If you leave here having decided that on purpose instead of by
> accident, this worked."

### 1:30 — Slide 005 — What I will *not* do

> "So you can relax and actually listen, here's what's not happening today."

1. I will not quote you a rate. Rates change hourly and anyone quoting one to a
   room has quoted a fiction.
2. I will not tell you rates are about to go up. Nobody knows. Anyone who says
   they do is selling urgency.
3. I will not use a countdown, a deadline, or a "this offer expires."
4. I will not ask for your contact information to unlock anything. Every download
   today is free with no form.
5. I will not pretend buying is always right.

> "If I break one of those, call it out in the chat. Seriously."

### 1:50 — Slide 006 — POLL 1

**"Where are you right now?"**
- Just curious / thinking about it someday
- Actively saving, no timeline yet
- Looking at homes, haven't talked to a lender
- Working with an agent or lender already
- Under contract right now

Read results aloud. Calibrate live:
- Mostly first two → lean into affordability and programs
- Mostly last two → lean into Loan Estimate and the deal-killers
- Anyone under contract → "Stay to the end, minute 31 is for you." *(the do-not-do list)*

### 2:15 — Slide 007 — The map

> "Ten stops. Here's the whole trip so you know where we are the entire time.
> Downloads and homework at minute 38 — you don't have to take notes, but the
> people who do will get more out of it."

## Compliance

- Slide 001: Equal Housing Lender mark + NMLS IDs, small, bottom.
- Slide 005 is itself a compliance asset — it commits on the record to no trigger
  terms and no urgency. Keep it verbatim.

## Failure modes

- **Running long here is fatal.** Hard cap 2:30. If Poll 1 results are slow, read
  what's in at 20 seconds and move.
- If chat immediately floods with "what are rates today" — the scripted answer is
  in `speaker_notes/delivery-guide.md`. Short version: *"I'm not going to answer
  that on camera, and at minute 20 I'll show you why that question is the wrong
  one anyway."*
