# Module 2 — The Process, End to End

**Time:** 2:30 – 7:00 (4:30) · **Slides:** 008–016
**Job:** Kill the fear of the unknown. Replace "I don't know what happens" with a
map they could draw from memory.
**Artifact:** The 9-Stage Map (printable, in the download kit)

---

## Why this goes first

Every other module assumes a frame the audience doesn't have yet. You cannot
teach someone what a Loan Estimate is if they don't know when it arrives or why.
The map is the container everything else gets filed into.

Secondary job: correct the single most expensive sequencing error in the whole
process — **shopping before financing.**

---

## Beat sheet

### 2:35 — Slide 009 — Story 1: The Reyes Offer

> "Marcus and Dani spent five months looking. Every weekend. They knew the school
> boundaries, they knew which streets got afternoon sun, they had opinions about
> countertops that I did not understand."

> "They walked into a house on a Saturday and both of them just — stopped. It was
> the one. They called their agent from the driveway. Agent says, great, let's
> write it up — send me your pre-approval letter."

> "They didn't have one. They'd been meaning to. It was on the list."

> "It took them six days to get one. The house went under contract in **six
> hours**, to somebody who'd done the boring part first."

> "Here's what I want you to take from that. They didn't lose that house because
> they had less money. They had *more* money than the buyer who got it. They lost
> it because they did step four before step one."

### 3:20 — Slide 010 — The 9 stages

1. **Financial readiness** — credit pulled, income and assets reviewed
2. **Pre-approval** — a real one (see slide 012)
3. **House hunting** — with a known budget and a letter in hand
4. **Offer & contract** — earnest money, deadlines, contingencies
5. **Loan application** — the formal one, within 3 days you get a Loan Estimate
6. **Processing** — documents, verifications, the appraisal ordered
7. **Underwriting** — the decision, then conditions
8. **Clear to close** — Closing Disclosure 3 business days before signing
9. **Closing** — sign, fund, record, keys

> "Notice where 'look at houses' is. Third. Not first. The whole industry lets you
> believe it's first because looking at houses is fun and getting pre-approved
> feels like a test."

### 4:00 — Slide 011 — Stage 1 is a conversation, not an application

> "The first call with a lender is not an application. Nothing is committed.
> Nobody's judging you. It's twenty minutes of: here's what I make, here's what I
> owe, here's what I've saved — what does that make possible?"

> "And I'll tell you the thing that keeps people from making that call: they're
> afraid of hearing no. So let me take that away. **'No' is almost never the
> answer. 'Not yet, and here's exactly what changes it' is the answer.** In my
> experience the average 'not yet' is between three and nine months of specific,
> boring, do-able steps. The only way that becomes three *years* is if you never
> made the call."

### 4:30 — Slide 012 — Three things people call "pre-approval"

| | Prequalification | Pre-approval | Underwritten / TBD approval |
|---|---|---|---|
| Credit pulled? | Sometimes | Yes | Yes |
| Income/assets documented? | No — stated | Yes — collected | Yes — verified |
| Reviewed by an underwriter? | No | Usually automated | **Yes, a human** |
| How long | Minutes | 1–2 days | 3–7 days |
| What a seller thinks | Very little | It's real | Nearly as strong as cash |

> "In a competitive situation, the third column is a weapon. It means the only
> thing left to approve is the house — not you. If you're going to be competing
> for anything, ask your lender for it by name: **'Can you get me underwritten
> up front?'** Not everybody offers it. Now you know to ask."

### 5:15 — Slide 013 — Who's actually in the room

Seven people, and who they work for:

| Who | Works for | Paid by | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Your agent | **You** (if you signed a buyer agreement — read it) | Commission, per your agreement | Read how they're compensated. Ask directly. |
| Listing agent | **The seller** | Seller | They are lovely. They are not yours. |
| Loan officer | You (broker) or the lender (retail) | Commission/salary | Ask which one. It changes the incentives. |
| Processor | The lender | Salary | Your document conduit. Be nice to them. |
| Underwriter | The lender/investor | Salary | Never speaks to you. Decides everything. |
| Appraiser | Independent, ordered through a firewall | Fee, paid by you | Neither side can lean on them. That's the point. |
| Title / escrow | Neutral | Fee | Handles the money and the deed. |

> "The most useful sentence in this entire webinar might be: **'Who pays you, and
> how?'** Ask everyone. Nobody will be offended. The ones who are, are telling you
> something."

### 5:50 — Slide 014 — The real calendar

30–45 days from contract to keys, typical. Laid out as an actual month:

- Day 0 — under contract, earnest money deposited
- Day 1–3 — formal application, **Loan Estimate must arrive within 3 business days**
- Day 3–10 — inspection window (deadlines are contractual, not suggestions)
- Day 5–15 — appraisal ordered and completed
- Day 10–25 — underwriting, then **conditions** (this is the anxious part)
- Day 25–30 — clear to close
- Day 27–30 — **Closing Disclosure at least 3 business days before signing**
- Day 30 — sign, fund, record

> "The part nobody warns you about is the middle. Days 10 through 25 you will get
> emails asking for things you already sent. It feels like they think you're
> lying. They don't. Underwriting is a checklist, and the checklist doesn't know
> what you already did. Send it again, same day, and it's a non-event."

### 6:25 — Slide 015 — The 5 deal-killers `[CUT-5: reduce to 3]`

1. **New debt** — the car, the furniture, the credit card. *(Mistake #4 — hold that.)*
2. **Job change** — especially W-2 → self-employed, or salary → commission
3. **Large unexplained deposits** — every dollar has to have a story
4. **Appraisal comes in low** — the gap has to come from somewhere
5. **Missed contract deadlines** — inspection and financing dates are real dates

### 6:50 — Slide 016 — Artifact #1

> "That map is a one-page PDF in your download kit. Print it. Put it on the fridge.
> When you're on day 14 and you feel like nothing is happening — it's because
> you're in underwriting, which looks exactly like nothing happening."
