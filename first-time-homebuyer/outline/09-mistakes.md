# Module 9 — The 9 Most Expensive First-Time Buyer Mistakes

**Time:** 36:00 – 38:30 (2:30) · **Slides:** 068–069
**Job:** Retrieval practice, not first exposure. Every mistake here was *seeded*
earlier in the webinar at the moment it became relevant. This module is the
recall test, which is what actually drives retention.

---

## Design note — why this is a recap and not new content

v3's critique flagged that putting the most shareable content at minute 36 wastes
it, because minute 36 is the fatigue zone. The fix was to seed each mistake at its
natural moment ("that's mistake #4 — hold onto it") and use this block as
**retrieval practice.** Spaced retrieval is the single best-evidenced mechanism in
learning science, and it costs 2:30.

Delivery matters here: **fast.** ~15 seconds each. Energy up. If you slow down and
re-explain, you've turned a retention exercise back into a lecture and you'll run
over.

## Beat sheet

### 36:00 — Slide 068 — Mistakes 1–5

> "Nine mistakes. You've already met all of them today — I'm just naming them so
> they stick. Fast."

**1. Looking at houses before talking to a lender.**
> "Marcus and Dani. Six hours."

**2. Shopping the payment instead of the total cost.**
> "The lowest rate is not the cheapest loan. Compute the breakeven."

**3. Only talking to one lender.**
> "Same rate, six thousand four hundred dollars apart. It costs you nothing in
> credit score to get three. Nothing."

**4. Opening new credit between application and closing.**
> "The sofa. Nine days. Museum exhibit — look, don't touch."

**5. Confusing closing costs with cash to close.**
> "$5,400 versus $31,760. Page one, bottom right. Cash to Close."

### 37:15 — Slide 069 — Mistakes 6–9 + the most expensive one

**6. Waiting years to reach 20% down.**
> "Tom and Ashley. Six years avoiding a temporary fee. Run the comparison — then
> decide. Just don't let it be a default."

**7. Buying at the very top of your approval.**
> "The bank asks whether you'll repay. It never asks whether you'll be okay."

**8. Taking the builder incentive without comparing.**
> "Sometimes it wins. You just have to actually check. It took him ten minutes."

**9. Never asking who gets paid, and how.**
> "Ask everybody. Nobody good is offended by that question."

**AND THE MOST EXPENSIVE ONE — say it slowly:**

> "**Not asking questions because you're afraid of looking like you don't know
> what you're doing.**"

*(Pause.)*

> "Every single story I told you today started there. Marcus and Dani didn't ask
> what the first step was. The guy who served four years never asked if it
> counted — and almost paid tens of thousands of dollars for the silence. The
> woman with two Loan Estimates didn't ask what Section A was. The sofa guy didn't
> ask if he could buy furniture."

> "None of those people were uninformed because they were careless. They were
> uninformed because **asking felt embarrassing** — and this industry has been
> pretty comfortable with that, because a client who doesn't ask questions is a
> faster close."

> "So here's the last thing I'll say about mistakes. **You are allowed to not know
> this.** You've done it zero times. The people across the table have done it
> thousands. That asymmetry isn't a character flaw, it's just arithmetic, and the
> only thing that closes it is asking. Every question you were embarrassed to ask
> today, somebody else in this room typed into the chat."

## Delivery discipline

- **Hard cap 2:30.** This is the most compressible module in the deck *by pace*,
  but do not cut items — cut words.
- Do not take questions during this block. Hold to Q&A. Say so if needed.
- Energy up on 1–9, then **drop the pace entirely** for the last one. The tempo
  change is what makes it land.
- Slide 069's closing beat is the emotional peak of the webinar. Do not step on
  it with a transition. Let the silence run two full seconds.
