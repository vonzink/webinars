## Beat sheet

### 30:05 — Slide 062 — Your mortgage score is a different score

> "Show of hands in the chat — who knows their credit score?"

*(Wait. Lots of yes.)*

> "Okay. Almost none of you know your mortgage credit score."

**Three things nobody tells you:**

1. **Different scoring model.** The score in your banking app is usually a
   consumer-education model. Mortgage lending has historically used older,
   mortgage-specific FICO versions. They can differ meaningfully — sometimes by
   20+ points in either direction.
2. **Three bureaus, and the middle one is used.** Equifax, Experian, TransUnion —
   they hold different data. The lender pulls all three and uses your **middle**
   score. Not the average. The middle.
3. **Two borrowers?** Generally the **lower** of the two middle scores drives
   pricing.

> "So Maya's a 742 and Dev's a 758 — they're priced off **742.** Which leads to
> something people find uncomfortable: sometimes the couple qualifies for a better
> loan with **one** borrower on it. Not always — you lose the second income too.
> But it's a real calculation and your lender should run both ways. Ask for it."

> "Also — the score model is changing. There's been an ongoing industry
> transition in which mortgage scoring models are updated. Ask your lender what
> they're pulling **today**, because the answer has been moving."
> *(`VERIFY` before each delivery — `research/credit.md`.)*

### 30:50 — Slide 063 — What actually moves the number

| Factor | Weight | How fast it moves |
|---|---|---|
| Payment history | ~35% | Slow. One 30-day late hurts for years. |
| Amounts owed / utilization | ~30% | **Fast — 30 to 60 days** |
| Length of history | ~15% | Only time |
| New credit / inquiries | ~10% | Fast in, slow out |
| Credit mix | ~10% | Slow |

> "Utilization is the lever. Thirty percent of your score, and it can move in one
> billing cycle. That's the whole game if you're trying to improve a score before
> you buy."

**The three fastest legitimate moves:**
1. **Pay balances down below ~30% of each card's limit — ideally under 10%.**
   *Per card, not just overall.* One maxed card among five drags you.
2. **Do not close old cards.** Closing shortens history and reduces available
   credit — utilization jumps. That "cleaning up my credit" instinct backfires.
3. **Dispute genuine errors.** Errors are more common than people assume. But
   note: an *active dispute flag* on an account can itself stall underwriting.
   Fix it early, not during your loan.

**POLL 4** (quick): *"True or false: closing a credit card you don't use helps
your score."* → Overwhelmingly "true." It's **false.** Thirty seconds, big payoff.

> "And the thing nobody says: **you do not need a 780.** Pricing moves in tiers.
> Going from 735 to 745 can matter. Going from 780 to 800 usually does nothing.
> Chasing a perfect score is a way to delay buying for no benefit. Find out which
> tier you're in and whether you're close to the next one — that's a five-minute
> question for a lender."

### 31:40 — Slide 064 — The 45-day window, the list, and the sofa

**FIRST — the fear that stops people from shopping:**

> "Mortgage inquiries within a shopping window — commonly described as **14 to 45
> days depending on the scoring model** — are treated as **one inquiry** for
> scoring purposes. The system was deliberately designed so you could shop."

> "Let me be blunt about who benefits from you not knowing that. **Getting three
> Loan Estimates costs you essentially nothing in score.** Not getting them cost
> that woman on slide 53 six thousand four hundred dollars."

**EXERCISE 3 (30 seconds):** *"How many credit applications have you opened in the
last 12 months? Count them. Card, car, store financing, phone plan, buy-now-pay-
later. Number in the chat."*

**SECOND — Story 7: The $9,000 sofa**

> "Nine days before closing, a client bought furniture. Nine thousand dollars,
> zero percent for 24 months, at the store, because it was zero percent and he was
> excited — and honestly, who wouldn't be."

> "Lenders re-pull credit before closing. Every time. The new debt hit, his ratios
> moved, and his approval — which was already issued — came apart. He did not
> close on time."

> "He didn't do anything reckless. He bought a couch for a house he'd already been
> approved for. **Nobody told him the approval was still alive.**"

**ARTIFACT #6 — THE DO-NOT-DO LIST**
From application to keys in hand:

1. Don't open **any** new credit — cards, cars, furniture, phones, BNPL
2. Don't close existing accounts
3. Don't make large purchases on existing cards
4. Don't change jobs — and *especially* not W-2 → self-employed
5. Don't move money between accounts without a paper trail
6. Don't accept a cash gift without telling your lender first — gift funds have a
   documentation process, and undocumented deposits become a problem
7. Don't co-sign anything for anyone
8. Don't miss a single payment on anything

> "Rule that covers all eight: **from application to keys, your financial life is
> a museum exhibit. Look, don't touch.** If something has to change — a job offer,
> an inheritance, a car that died — that's fine, life happens. **Call your loan
> officer first.** Almost all of these are survivable with warning and fatal as a
> surprise."

## Compliance

- Score factor weights are the commonly published general model — describe as
  approximate.
- **Never state a specific score gets a specific rate.** Pricing tiers described
  qualitatively only.
- The 14–45 day shopping window varies by scoring model — state it as a range,
  never as a guarantee.
- Mortgage scoring model transition is an evolving policy item — `VERIFY` before
  each delivery.
- No credit repair advice, and no suggestion that legitimate negative information
  can be removed.
