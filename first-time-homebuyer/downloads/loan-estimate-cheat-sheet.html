Loan Estimate Cheat Sheet

Every lender in America must give you the same three-page form. Here's how to read it in 60 seconds.
From Page Two — What Nobody Explains About Buying Your First Home

Two Loan Estimates. Same term, same loan amount, same interest rate. Section A on one was about $1,300. Section A on the other was about $7,700. $6,400 apart.

Nobody was defrauded. Every number was disclosed correctly on the government form. The disclosure only works if somebody reads page two.

Two Things You're Owed

  • The Loan Estimate must arrive within 3 business days of a completed application.
  • Certain costs are subject to tolerance limits — they can't simply increase. If they do improperly, the lender generally has to cure the difference.

Asking for a Loan Estimate does not obligate you to anything. It isn't a commitment and it isn't a hard sell. Get three.

Page 1 — Five Boxes

BoxWhat to look for
Loan TermsAmount, rate, monthly P&I — plus yes/no columns: can this increase after closing? Prepayment penalty? Balloon payment? On a standard 30-year fixed these should all be the boring answer. If any surprise you, stop and ask why.
Projected PaymentsYour payment over time, including mortgage insurance — and when it drops off.
Taxes, Insurance & AssessmentsThe escrow line. Remember: your payment changes when these change.
Costs at ClosingTwo different numbers. Estimated Closing Costs and Estimated Cash to Close. Cash to Close — bottom right — is the one you plan your life around.
Rate lockIs it locked, and until when.

Page 2 — Only Two Letters Matter

SectionWhat it isShoppable?
A. Origination ChargesThe lender's own fees + pointsNo — but shop lenders. THIS IS THE BOX.
B. Services you cannot shop forAppraisal, credit report, flood certNo
C. Services you CAN shop forTitle, settlement, survey, pestYES — and almost nobody does

G. Initial escrow depositThe servicer's cushionNo
H. OtherOwner's title policy, home warrantySometimes

Section A is the labor line on a repair estimate. Parts cost what parts cost — everyone pays roughly the same for the appraisal and the recording fee. Labor is where shops differ.

Compare Two Lenders In 60 Seconds

  1. Both Loan Estimates issued the same day. Rates move — otherwise you're comparing weather, not lenders.
  2. Same loan amount, same program, same lock period.
  3. Compare Section A only. That's the lender.
  4. Note the points on page 1. A lower rate bought with points is not a lower rate.
  5. Check Cash to Close on page 1. That's the real out-of-pocket.
  6. Ignore B, E, F, and G. That isn't the lender competing.

Two Things To Remember

Shopping doesn't hurt your credit. Mortgage inquiries inside a shopping window — commonly described as 14 to 45 days depending on the scoring model — count as one inquiry. The system was deliberately designed so you could shop.

Then compare your Closing Disclosure to this. You must receive the CD at least 3 business days before signing. Read it line by line against your Loan Estimate. That's exactly what those three days are for.

Questions Worth Asking Every Lender

  • "What's in your Section A?"
  • "Can I have a lender credit instead of paying points?" — if cash is tight, this is frequently the right trade.
  • "Do you offer a float-down if rates improve after I lock?" — ask before you lock.
  • "Can you get me underwritten up front?" — if you'll be competing for a house.
  • "Who pays you, and how?"
