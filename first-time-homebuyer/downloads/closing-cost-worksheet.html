Two Levers Most Buyers Never Use

1. Shop Section C. Your lender must give you a Written List of Providers for title and settlement — and you're allowed to use your own. These fees can vary by four figures between companies in the same city. Almost nobody shops it.

2. Move your closing date later in the month. Prepaid interest runs from your closing date to the end of that month. On a $427,500 loan at 6.500%, that's about $76 per day. Closing on the 28th instead of the 3rd moves roughly $1,900 of cash off the table.

It doesn't reduce what you owe — you pay that interest either way. But if cash at closing is your binding constraint, it's real money in the right moment.

Don't Forget These — They Hit In The Same 60 Days

  • Home inspection — paid out of pocket, before closing, non-refundable if you walk. That's the cost of information, and it's worth it.
  • Moving — DIY or movers
  • Immediate needs — window coverings, a lawnmower, the tool you don't own, new locks on day one
  • The first repair — the thing the inspection flagged as "monitor"
  • Utility deposits and setup

Don't spend your last dollar at the closing table. Your emergency fund is not part of the down payment. If buying the house requires spending it, you're buying too much house.
