Mortgage Glossary

Every term from the masterclass, in plain English. ★ = the ones that actually change decisions.
From Page Two — What Nobody Explains About Buying Your First Home

Your Payment

Principal — the part of your payment that actually buys the house.
Interest — rent on the money.
PITI — Principal, Interest, Taxes, Insurance. Add mortgage insurance and HOA for the real total.
Escrow (impound) account ★ — a subscription your lender runs for you. They collect 1/12 of your annual taxes and insurance monthly, hold it, and pay the bills when due. Not a fee — your own money.
Escrow analysis ★ — the annual recalculation. When taxes or insurance change, your payment changes. This is why "fixed rate" does not mean "fixed payment."
HOA dues — homeowners association fees. Count against you in underwriting exactly like a mortgage payment, and can be raised without your vote.
Amortization ★ — how each payment splits between interest and principal. Interest-heavy early, principal-heavy late.

Getting The Loan

Pre-qualification — a conversation. Stated numbers, little verification. Worth little to a seller.
Pre-approval — credit pulled, documents collected, usually run through automated underwriting.
Underwritten (TBD) approval ★ — a human underwriter has already reviewed you. Only the property is left. Ask for it by name if you'll be competing.
Underwriting — the lender's decision process. Verifies income, assets, credit, and property against program guidelines.
AUS — Automated Underwriting System. Fannie Mae's Desktop Underwriter (DU) or Freddie Mac's Loan Product Advisor (LPA). Issues findings the loan must be documented to.
Conditions — the list underwriting needs before final approval. Getting conditions is normal, not a rejection.
Clear to Close (CTC) — all conditions satisfied.
Overlay ★ — a lender's own rule stricter than the program requires. Why the same borrower is declined at one lender and approved at another.

The Money You Bring

Down payment — your cash contribution to the purchase price.
Closing costs — fees to get the loan and transfer the property. Spent.
Prepaids — paid in advance: prepaid interest, first-year homeowners insurance. Still your money, front-loaded.
Initial escrow deposit — a few months of taxes and insurance to fund the escrow cushion.
Cash to Close ★ — down payment + closing costs + prepaids + escrows − earnest money − credits. The number that matters. Page 1, bottom right.
Earnest money — a good-faith deposit with your offer, credited toward cash to close. Refundable within your contingency deadlines.
Seller concessions — the seller pays some of your closing costs. Limits vary by program and down payment.
Lender credit ★ — the lender pays some of your costs in exchange for a higher rate. Points in reverse. Frequently right when cash is tight. Nobody offers it — ask.
Gift funds — down payment money from an eligible donor. Requires a gift letter and paper trail. Tell your lender before it hits your account.

Rate & Cost

Interest rate — what your payment is calculated from.
APR ★ — the rate plus certain lender costs, spread across the full term. The rate is the sticker price; APR is closer to the out-the-door price. A comparison tool, not your payment rate.
Discount points — prepaid interest to permanently lower your rate. One point = 1% of the loan amount.
Breakeven ★ — cost of points ÷ monthly savings = months to recover. Points are a bet on how long you keep this exact loan. If you won't clearly pass the breakeven, don't.
Temporary buydown (2-1, 3-2-1) — prefunded money, usually from a seller or builder, lowering your effective rate for the first years. You qualify at the real rate.
Rate lock — the lender holds your rate for a set number of days. Extensions cost money.
Float-down — an option some lenders offer to take a lower rate if the market improves after you lock. Ask before you lock.
Par rate — the rate with no points paid and no lender credit given.

Ratios & Measurements

DTI ★ — Debt-to-Income. Total monthly obligations ÷ gross monthly income. A weight limit on a suitcase — it doesn't care what's in the bag.
Front-end ratio — housing payment alone ÷ gross income.
Back-end ratio ★ — housing + all other monthly debts ÷ gross income. The one that governs.
LTV — Loan-to-Value. Loan ÷ property value. 5% down = 95% LTV.
CLTV — combined LTV, including second mortgages such as down payment assistance seconds.
Residual income ★ — cash left after all obligations, by family size and region. VA's test instead of a hard DTI cap — arguably a better measure of affordability.
Reserves — liquid assets remaining after closing, measured in months of housing payments.

Mortgage Insurance

PMI / BPMI ★ — borrower-paid monthly mortgage insurance on conventional loans. Removable.
LPMI — lender-paid MI, built into a higher rate. Not removable except by refinancing.
FHA MIP ★ — FHA's mortgage insurance. Upfront + annual. On most FHA loans with under 10% down, it lasts the life of the loan.
VA funding fee — one-time, financeable, varies by down payment and prior use. Waived for veterans receiving compensation for a service-connected disability.
USDA guarantee fee — USDA's upfront and annual equivalent.
HPA ★ — Homeowners Protection Act. Request PMI removal at 80% LTV; automatic termination at 78% on the original schedule, primary residence, payments current.

Documents

Loan Estimate (LE) ★ — standardized 3-page disclosure, due within 3 business days of application. Section A on page 2 is where lenders differ.
Closing Disclosure (CD) ★ — the final numbers. You must receive it at least 3 business days before signing. Compare it to your LE.
Promissory note — your promise to repay. The debt itself.
Deed of trust / mortgage — the security instrument tying the debt to the property.
Certificate of Eligibility (COE) — proves VA entitlement.
Written List of Providers ★ — the Section C services you're allowed to shop for. You're handed it. Almost nobody uses it.

Property

Appraisal — an independent opinion of value, ordered through a firewall so neither side can influence it.
Appraisal gap — when value comes in below contract price. Somebody covers the difference or the deal renegotiates.
Comparables (comps) — recent nearby sales used to support value.
Contingency ★ — a contractual right to exit — inspection, appraisal, financing — within a specific deadline. The deadline is the protection.
Title insurance — protects against defects in ownership history. Lender's policy protects the lender; owner's policy protects you.

Programs

Conventional — not government-insured. Follows Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac guidelines.
Conforming loan — a conventional loan within the annual limit for the county.
Conventional 97 — 3% down conventional for first-time buyers.
HomeReady / Home Possible ★ — Fannie/Freddie 3%-down programs with income limits, reduced MI coverage, and better pricing. Frequently the best first-time buyer product and chronically under-mentioned.
FHA — government-insured, 3.5% down at 580+, more forgiving credit.
VA ★ — 0% down, no monthly MI, for eligible service members, veterans, and some surviving spouses. Reusable — not once per lifetime.
USDA — 0% down in eligible areas with household income limits.
CHFA — Colorado Housing and Finance Authority. First mortgages plus down payment assistance.
DPA ★ — Down Payment Assistance. Grants or second mortgages. State, metro, county, and employer layers. Stack it under whatever program you chose — it isn't an alternative to one.
First-time homebuyer ★ — most programs: no ownership interest in a primary residence in the last three years. You can have owned before.
