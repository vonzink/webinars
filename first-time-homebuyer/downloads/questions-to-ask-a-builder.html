Questions To Ask A Builder

Twelve questions that change the balance of information. Bring this to the model home.
From Page Two — What Nobody Explains About Buying Your First Home

The one thing to understand first. Builders would usually rather give you $20,000 in incentives than take $20,000 off the price — because a price cut sets a comparable sale that lowers every other home in the subdivision.

So don't negotiate price. Negotiate everything else — and negotiate hard. There is far more room there than most buyers use.

The 12 Questions

1
What exactly is included, and what's an upgrade? Can I have that in writing?The model home is almost never the base package.
2
What will my property taxes be at full assessment — not first year?Your first-year bill may be assessed on an unimproved lot. When the finished house is assessed, your escrow payment jumps and you get a bill for the shortfall — both at once. Budget from the full number.
3
What's the HOA today, who controls it, and when does control transfer to owners?Builder-controlled HOAs often raise dues after transfer. Dues count against you in underwriting exactly like a mortgage payment.
4
What's the lot premium, and what am I getting for it?Sometimes it's a real view. Sometimes it's a slightly larger rectangle.
5
Are landscaping, fencing, window coverings, and appliances included?These are frequently excluded and easily total five figures in month one.
6
What's the incentive if I don't use your preferred lender?The question they don't expect. Ask it early, plainly, and without apology.
7
Can I take a price reduction instead of the incentive?A price reduction is permanent. A temporary rate buydown is two years. Sometimes you can have either — but only if you ask.
8
What's your delay history on this phase, and what happens to my rate lock?Delays are normal. Lock extensions cost money. Ask who pays if the delay is theirs.
9
Can I get an independent inspection before drywall and again before closing?People skip this because the house is new. New houses are built by humans on deadlines. Inspect it. Twice.
10
What's the warranty, exactly — 1 year, 2 years, 10 years structural — and who honors it?"Who honors it" matters as much as the term. Builders change hands.
11
What is planned on the empty land behind this lot?That view may be a phase-three cul-de-sac.
12
What happens to my earnest money if the build is delayed past my rate lock or my lease ends?Ask before you sign, not after.

The Preferred Lender Incentive — Run The Math

Builder incentives are commonly conditioned on using their preferred or affiliated lender. This is legal and disclosed. The issue isn't legality — it's that hardly anyone runs the comparison, which is what makes the offer work for the builder.

Here's a real one:

 Amount
Builder credit for using their lender+$15,000
Rate 0.25% higher ≈ $70/month × 7 years (expected hold)−$5,880
Extra Section A at their lender−$3,000
NET VALUE OF THE INCENTIVE$6,120

The builder's lender won — and he was told to take it. Six thousand dollars is six thousand dollars.

Sometimes the builder's lender is the right answer. What's never right is taking the incentive without running the comparison. It takes ten minutes.

Run yours

 Yours
Builder credit offered+$__________
Rate difference × monthly cost × months you'll keep the loan−$__________
Difference in Section A−$__________
NET VALUE$__________

The procedure: get the builder's Loan Estimate. Get one outside Loan Estimate the same day. Compare Section A. Convert the rate difference into dollars over how long you'll actually keep the loan. Compare that total to the incentive.

A builder can't stop you from shopping. If anyone implies you're required to use their lender to buy the house, that's worth asking pointed questions about.
