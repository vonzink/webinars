**PRESENTER NOTES:** Don't walk all five. Ask the room which they've heard of, and open

🔴 **Every modal in this section carries:** *"General guidelines only. Actual
eligibility depends on full underwriting, credit, property, and lender overlays."*

### MODAL STRUCTURE — identical for all five
```
TYPICAL BORROWER · MINIMUM DOWN · MORTGAGE INSURANCE
CREDIT FLEXIBILITY · PROS · CONS · BEST USED WHEN
```

**MODAL — CONVENTIONAL**
- **Typical borrower:** solid credit, steady documented income
- **Minimum down:** 3% on first-time buyer programs; 5% standard
- **Mortgage insurance:** required under 20% down — **and removable**
- **Credit flexibility:** least forgiving; pricing degrades noticeably at lower scores
- **Pros:** MI comes off · best pricing at strong credit · widest property flexibility ·
  income-limited versions offer reduced MI and better pricing
- **Cons:** stricter credit · less tolerant of recent derogatory history
- **Best used when:** credit is good and you want mortgage insurance with an exit

**MODAL — FHA**
- **Typical borrower:** credit that's rebuilding, or a higher debt load
- **Minimum down:** 3.5%
- **Mortgage insurance:** upfront **and** monthly. On most loans under 10% down it lasts
  the **life of the loan**
- **Credit flexibility:** significantly more forgiving
- **Pros:** lower credit thresholds · higher DTI tolerance · shorter waiting periods
  after derogatory events · **assumable**
- **Cons:** MI usually doesn't come off · upfront premium added to the balance ·
  property condition standards
- **Best used when:** credit or debt load rules out conventional — often with a plan to
  refinance later *(which depends on future rates nobody can promise)*
- **No first-time buyer requirement.** Anyone eligible can use it

**MODAL — VA**
- **Typical borrower:** eligible service members, veterans, National Guard, Reserves,
  and certain surviving spouses
- **Minimum down:** **0%**
- **Mortgage insurance:** **none** — no monthly MI at all
- **Credit flexibility:** no VA-wide minimum score; lender overlays apply
- **Pros:** no down payment · no monthly MI · competitive pricing · qualifies on
  **residual income** rather than a hard ratio · **reusable — not once per lifetime** ·
  assumable
- **Cons:** a funding fee applies *(financeable, and waived for veterans receiving
  compensation for a service-connected disability)* · stricter property standards
- **Best used when:** you're eligible. Almost always check this first
- ⭐ **If anyone in the household served — including a spouse — ask. People assume a
  short enlistment "doesn't count." It usually does.**

**MODAL — USDA**
- **Typical borrower:** buying in an eligible area, under the household income limit
- **Minimum down:** **0%**
- **Mortgage insurance:** an upfront guarantee fee plus a smaller annual fee
- **Credit flexibility:** moderate
- **Pros:** no down payment · annual fee often lower than FHA's
- **Cons:** **geography decides** · household income limits count everyone in the home,
  not just borrowers · property condition standards
- **Best used when:** the address is eligible — **check the map, not the vibe.** Plenty
  of ordinary-feeling towns qualify

**MODAL — JUMBO**
- **Typical borrower:** loan amount above the conforming limit for the county
- **Minimum down:** varies by investor — generally higher
- **Mortgage insurance:** varies; often structured to avoid it
- **Credit flexibility:** the least — strongest credit and reserves expected
- **Pros:** finances higher-priced homes · flexible structures available
- **Cons:** stricter everything — reserves, documentation, DTI caps · **rules vary
  substantially by investor**
- **Best used when:** the price requires it. Shop it — jumbo terms differ far more
  between lenders than conforming terms do

## PROGRAMS SLIDE 2 — Cash to Close: Where It Comes From

**ONE CONCEPT:** the money doesn't all have to be yours.

**HEADLINE:** It doesn't all have to come from savings
**SUBHEAD:** Six sources. Click any one.

**LAYOUT:** Six clickable cards, icon + label, in a responsive grid.
`Savings` · `Gift Funds` · `Seller Credits` · `Down Payment Assistance` ·
`2-1 Buydowns` · `Agent Credits`

**VISUAL:** Six tributaries flowing into one channel labelled **Cash to Close** — the
banded-triangle language applied as flow bands.

**PRESENTER NOTES:**
> "Most first-time buyers assume every dollar has to come out of their own savings.
> It doesn't. There are six places this money can come from, and most buyers use more
> than one."

**MODAL — SAVINGS**
- Must be **seasoned** when the program requires it — sitting in your account, documented
- **No undocumented cash deposits.** Cash you deposited without a paper trail generally
  cannot be used
- Large deposits outside normal payroll must be **sourced and explained**
- Underwriting will ask about deposits that don't match your income pattern — that's
  routine, not suspicion
- 💡 Move money **before** you apply, not during

**MODAL — GIFT FUNDS**
- Allowed on most programs, with conditions
- **Gift letter required** — stating the amount, the donor, and that **it is not a loan**
- **Eligible donor relationships** vary by program — generally family; some programs
  allow employers, unions, or charitable and government entities
- **Source documentation** — where the donor's money came from
- **Transfer documentation** — the paper trail from donor's account to yours
- ⚠️ **Tell your lender before the money moves.** A gift handled correctly is routine;
  the same gift deposited quietly becomes a two-week problem

**MODAL — SELLER CREDITS**
- The seller contributes toward your closing costs
- **Concession limits apply** and vary by loan program and down payment
- Can pay allowable closing costs and prepaid items — **cannot** be used for your down
  payment
- Cannot exceed your actual costs — unused credit doesn't come back as cash
- **Negotiation strategy:** in a softer market this is often more available than buyers
  realise. Sometimes easier to obtain than a price reduction — ask your agent to ask

**MODAL — 2-1 BUYDOWNS**
- **Temporary** payment reduction — the rate acts lower in year one, less so in year two,
  then reaches the real rate in year three
- The difference is **prefunded into an escrow account** — usually paid by the **seller
  or builder** as a concession, not by you
- **Benefit:** real cash-flow relief in the two most expensive years of ownership
- ⚠️ **You qualify at the real rate, not the reduced one.** You will be paying the full
  payment in year three
- If you sell or refinance early, unused funds are generally credited toward your loan
- ⚠️ **If the plan depends on refinancing before year three, that plan depends on rates
  nobody controls**

**MODAL — AGENT CREDITS**
- A real estate agent may contribute part of their commission toward your costs
- **When allowed:** permitted by many programs, but it must be **disclosed** and appear
  on the closing documents
- **Limitations:** counts toward overall contribution limits · cannot be an undisclosed
  side arrangement · brokerage policy varies and some prohibit it entirely
- Ask early — it can't be added at the closing table

**MODAL — DOWN PAYMENT ASSISTANCE**
*(This one gets the longest treatment — it's the least understood.)*
- Grants or second mortgages that reduce the cash you need at closing
- **Four layers to check:** state · metro/city · county · **employer, union, or profession**

**PROS**
- Can dramatically reduce cash needed to close
- Sometimes forgivable after a period of occupancy
- Opens ownership to buyers who'd otherwise wait years

**CONS**
- **Income limits** and **purchase price limits** apply
- **Additional underwriting** and a second set of guidelines to satisfy
- **Often a higher interest rate** on the first mortgage — that's how the assistance is funded
- **More documentation** and longer timelines
- **Possible repayment requirements** — some must be repaid on sale or refinance
- ⚠️ **Assistance can weaken an offer.** Sellers in a competitive market may view a
  DPA-backed offer as slower or likelier to fall through. Sometimes true, often not — but
  perception affects which offer gets accepted

**WHAT TO ACTUALLY DO**
- Ask a lender to run all four layers for **your county and income**
- **Terms, limits, and funding availability change — some programs run out of money
  mid-year.** Verify on the day you're shopping, not months ahead
- Compare the assistance against the higher rate. Sometimes it clearly wins. Sometimes
  it doesn't. **Run it.**

**Optional chip → modal — "Prepaids and escrows aren't fees"**
- Prepaid interest, your first year of homeowners insurance, and a few months of taxes

- You were always going to pay it. It's front-loaded, not additional
- 💡 Closing later in the month reduces prepaid interest. It doesn't change what you owe
  overall — but if cash at the table is your constraint, it's a real lever

## PROCESS SLIDE 1 — Meet Your Home Buying Team

**ONE CONCEPT:** eight people, and they don't all work for you.

**HEADLINE:** Meet your home buying team
**SUBHEAD:** Click anyone.

**LAYOUT:** Eight clickable cards — avatar/icon + role. Responsive grid, 4 across
desktop / 2 tablet / 1 mobile.

`Loan Officer` · `Realtor` · `Seller` · `Processor` · `Underwriter` ·
`Title Company` · `Closer` · `Insurance Agent`

**MODAL STRUCTURE — identical for all eight:**
```
WHAT THEY DO · WHEN YOU'LL INTERACT · COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS
```

**LOAN OFFICER** — Structures your financing, shops lenders, guides you from
pre-approval to closing. *When:* start to finish, most often at the beginning and end.
*Misconception:* that we approve your loan. We don't — **the underwriter does.** We
package the file and advocate for it.

**REALTOR** — Finds homes, writes and negotiates your offer, manages contract deadlines.
*When:* constantly, from search through closing. *Misconception:* that the agent showing
you a house works for you. **The listing agent works for the seller.** Yours works for
you — read your buyer agreement and ask how they're paid.

**SELLER** — Owns the home. Their motivation shapes what's negotiable. *When:* almost
never directly — communication runs through agents. *Misconception:* that they want the
highest price above all. Sometimes timing, certainty, or a fast close matters more, and
that's where a well-prepared buyer wins.

**PROCESSOR** — Collects and organises your documentation, orders the appraisal and
title, prepares the file for underwriting. *When:* the middle stretch, and they'll email
you a lot. *Misconception:* that being asked for something twice means someone lost it.
Underwriting is a checklist and the checklist doesn't know what you already sent.
**Send it again, same day, and it's a non-event.**

**UNDERWRITER** — Makes the actual decision. Verifies income, assets, credit, and the
property against program guidelines. *When:* **never directly.** They don't speak to you.
*Misconception:* that they're looking for a reason to say no. They're documenting a
reason to say yes.

**TITLE COMPANY** — Researches ownership history, clears liens, issues title insurance,
holds and disburses funds. Neutral to both sides. *When:* mid-process and at closing.
*Misconception:* that they work for the lender. **They're neutral.** And you can often
shop for them — most people never do.

**CLOSER** — Runs the signing, walks you through the documents, notarises, and makes sure
funds move correctly. *When:* closing day. *Misconception:* that closing takes five
minutes. Budget an hour, read what you sign, and ask about anything unclear — that's
what they're there for.

**INSURANCE AGENT** — Places your homeowners policy, required before closing. *When:*
a few weeks out. *Misconception:* that it's a last-minute formality. **Start early.**
Premiums vary widely, some properties are harder to insure than expected, and a high
premium can affect qualifying — not just your budget.

**PRESENTER NOTES:**
> "The most useful question in this entire process is four words: **who pays you, and
> how?** Ask everybody. Nobody good is offended by it — and the ones who are have just
> told you something."

## PROCESS SLIDE 2 — The Loan Process

**ONE CONCEPT:** eight steps, and you know where you are at all times.

**HEADLINE:** From first call to keys
**SUBHEAD:** Click any step.

**LAYOUT:** A **horizontal banded timeline** — the deck's signature language. Eight
connected steps, each a clickable node. Progress bands deepen from lime at the start to
deep teal at the end, mirroring the logo.

`Pre-Approval` → `Application` → `Processing` → `Underwriting` →
`Conditional Approval` → `Clear to Close` → `Closed` → `Funded`

*(Match the step naming on the MSFG website exactly — the screenshot is the reference.)*

**MODAL STRUCTURE — identical for all eight:**
```
WHAT HAPPENS · TYPICAL TIMELINE · WHO'S INVOLVED · WHAT TO EXPECT
```

**PRE-APPROVAL** — Credit reviewed, income and assets examined, you learn what's
possible. *Timeline:* 1–2 days. *Who:* you and your loan officer. *Expect:* a
conversation, not an exam. **Nothing is committed.** "No" is almost never the answer —
"not yet, and here's what changes it" is.

**APPLICATION** — The formal application once you're under contract. *Timeline:* same
day. *Who:* you, loan officer. *Expect:* a **Loan Estimate within three business days.**
Read page two.

**PROCESSING** — Documents collected and verified, appraisal and title ordered.
*Timeline:* 1–2 weeks. *Who:* processor, appraiser, title. *Expect:* document requests,
some of which will feel repetitive. Respond same-day and this stage stays short.

**UNDERWRITING** — The lender's decision. *Timeline:* several days to a week. *Who:*
the underwriter, whom you'll never meet. *Expect:* **this is the quiet, anxious part.**
It looks like nothing is happening. Something is.

**CONDITIONAL APPROVAL** — Approved, subject to a list of items. *Timeline:* a few days
to clear. *Who:* you, loan officer, processor. *Expect:* **conditions are normal.**
Getting a list is not a rejection — it's how nearly every loan is approved.

**CLEAR TO CLOSE** — Every condition satisfied. Closing gets scheduled. *Timeline:*
typically a few days before closing. *Expect:* your **Closing Disclosure at least three
business days before signing.** Compare it to your Loan Estimate line by line — that's
what the three days are for.

**CLOSED** — You sign. *Timeline:* about an hour. *Who:* you, the closer, sometimes your
agent. *Expect:* a lot of signatures. Read them. Ask questions.

**FUNDED** — The loan funds, the deed records, the home is yours. *Timeline:* same day
or next. *Expect:* **keys.** Depending on timing and local practice, recording may
happen the following business day.

**PRESENTER NOTES:**
> "Print this. Put it on the fridge. Ninety percent of the anxiety in this process is
> not knowing which step you're on — and the hardest stretch is underwriting, because
> it's the one that looks exactly like nothing happening."

**TIME:** 3:00 + popouts

## MISTAKES SLIDE 2 — Chasing the Lowest Rate

**ONE CONCEPT:** people lose money trying to save money on rate.
**Reuses the breakeven diagram from Myth Slide 2 — build once, reuse.** Same comparison
numbers, shown as a callback. The audience has seen this crossing before; the second
appearance should feel like recognition, not new information.

**HEADLINE:** How people lose money chasing rates

**LAYOUT:** The breakeven crossing diagram, callback style, with four points beside it:

- **Lowest rate vs. lowest cost** — a low rate bought with points isn't a low rate
- **Breakeven** — cost of the points ÷ the monthly saving. Won't pass it? Don't pay it
- **Points** — a bet on how long you keep **this exact loan**, not this house
- **Refinancing later** — ends the loan early, and **restarts amortization**

**Bottom, lime-barred:**
> Equity is built by payments and time — not by the third decimal place of your rate.

**PRESENTER NOTES:**
> "The mistake isn't paying points. Points are sometimes exactly right. The mistake is
> paying them without asking the one question that decides it: **how long will I keep
> this loan?** Not this house — this loan. Refinancing ends it even if you never move.
> Most first-time buyers dramatically overestimate that number, and 'I don't know' is
> itself an argument against paying."

## MISTAKES SLIDE 3 — Small Assumptions

**ONE CONCEPT:** the expensive mistakes don't feel like mistakes.

**HEADLINE:** The most expensive mistakes start as small assumptions
**SUBHEAD:** Click any one.

**LAYOUT:** Six clickable cards, each stating an assumption in quotation marks, in a
lighter type weight — they should read like thoughts, not headings.

1. *"I'll get pre-approved once I find a house."*
2. *"They're all about the same, so I'll just use one lender."*
3. *"Closing costs are what I need at closing."*
4. *"I'm approved, so I'm done."*
5. *"I'll wait until I have twenty percent."*
6. *"The builder's incentive is free money."*

**MODAL STRUCTURE:** `THE ASSUMPTION · WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENS · WHAT TO DO INSTEAD`

**1 — Pre-approval last.** *What happens:* the house you want goes under contract while
you're getting a letter. Sellers don't wait, and in a competitive situation an offer
without one often isn't presented seriously. *Instead:* pre-approval **first**. It's a
conversation, it's free, and nothing is committed.

**2 — One lender.** *What happens:* lender fees vary far more than people expect — two
loans at the identical rate can differ by thousands in origination charges. *Instead:*
get more than one Loan Estimate, **issued the same day**, and compare origination
charges. Mortgage inquiries inside a shopping window count as one — **it costs you
essentially nothing in score.**

**3 — Closing costs = cash to close.** *What happens:* you arrive at the table short,
because cash to close also includes the down payment, prepaid items, escrows, and
reserves. *Instead:* plan around **Cash to Close** on page one. Not closing costs.

**4 — Approved means done.** *What happens:* lenders **re-pull credit before closing.**
New debt taken on after approval can break the file days before you sign. *Instead:*
change nothing until you have keys. If something must change, call first.

**5 — Waiting for 20%.** *What happens:* the target is a percentage of a moving number,
so it rises while you save — and you pay rent the whole time. *Instead:* run the
comparison. Waiting is sometimes right. **It should be a decision, not a default.**

**6 — The builder's incentive.** *What happens:* the incentive may be partly funded by a
higher rate, higher fees, or a price set to pay for it. *Instead:* take the builder's
Loan Estimate, get one from outside, compare origination charges, and convert the rate
difference into real money over how long you'll keep the loan. **Sometimes the builder
genuinely wins — you just have to actually check.**

**PRESENTER NOTES:**
> "None of these feel like mistakes when you make them. Every one of them feels
> reasonable. That's what makes them expensive."
>
> *(Then, slowly:)* "And the one underneath all six — **not asking a question because
> you're afraid of looking like you don't know what you're doing.** You've done this
> zero times. Everyone across the table has done it thousands. That's not a flaw,
> that's arithmetic. **You are allowed to not know this.**"

# SECTION — QUESTIONS

## QUESTIONS SLIDE — Most Common Questions

**ONE CONCEPT:** the five things everyone wants to ask and doesn't.

**HEADLINE:** Most common questions
**SUBHEAD:** Click to expand.

**LAYOUT:** Five stacked accordion rows or clickable cards. Generous vertical rhythm.
Answers expand inline (HTML) / separate slides (PPT).

**1 · How much money do I actually need?**
Less than most people think. Conventional starts at 3% down, FHA at 3.5%, and VA and
USDA can be zero. But budget beyond the down payment — closing costs, prepaid items, and
an emergency fund you do **not** spend on the house. **If buying requires spending your
emergency fund, you're buying too much house.**

**2 · What credit score do I need?**
There's no single number — it depends on the program, and lender overlays vary. FHA and
VA reach meaningfully lower than conventional. Pricing moves in **tiers**, so being near
a tier edge matters more than being "perfect." **You are probably closer than you think.
Find out — it's a five-minute question.**

**3 · How long does the whole thing take?**
Contract to keys is typically 30–45 days. The longer part is the house hunt. From a
serious start, most people are two to six months out — and the single biggest variable
is **how fast you return documents.**

**4 · Should I lock my rate?**
Generally once you're under contract, for a period that covers your closing date with
room. Longer locks usually cost more, and extensions aren't free. **Ask about a
float-down before you lock** — it's free to ask, and it has real value if rates improve.

**5 · What happens after pre-approval?**
You shop with a real budget and a letter in hand. When you find the home, your offer
goes in, and pre-approval becomes a formal application — Loan Estimate within three
business days, then processing, underwriting, conditions, clear to close, and signing.
**Your pre-approval isn't permanent** — it expires, and it can change if your finances do.

**PRESENTER NOTES:** Open the ones the room asks for. Don't walk all five. Then open the
floor — **the questions people ask out loud are better than the ones I pre-wrote.**
