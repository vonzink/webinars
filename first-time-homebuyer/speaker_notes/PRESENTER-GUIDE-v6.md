# Presenter Guide — v6

**~38 minutes taught + open Q&A · 23 main slides · 53 popouts**
Default presenter: **Seth Angell**

> **This is not a word-for-word script, and that's deliberate.** Leadership asked for
> conversational delivery. A read script fights that. What follows is: talking points
> per slide, the handful of lines worth landing verbatim, which popouts to open, and
> the timing.
>
> **You will not open all 53 popouts. Nobody should.** Open the ones the room asks for.
> The rest exist so the answer is there when somebody wants it.

---

## THE THREE RULES OF DELIVERY

1. **Don't read the slide.** The audience reads faster than you talk. Say the thing the
   slide *doesn't* say.
2. **Ask before you explain.** "Which of these did you believe?" beats walking five cards.
3. **When you don't know — say so, and say who does.** Legal, tax, and contract questions
   go to a real estate attorney, a CPA, or their agent. Saying that out loud makes every
   other answer you give more credible.

# SLIDE 1 — OPENING · 0:45

Name, role, company. Twenty seconds, not two minutes.

**Land verbatim — this is the trust-setter:**
> "Two things I'm not going to do today. I won't quote you a rate — rates change hourly
> and anyone quoting one to a room of strangers has quoted a fiction. And I won't tell
> you where rates are going, because nobody knows, and anyone who says they do is
> selling you urgency."

Then: *"Everything today is education. If you leave and never call me, that's a fine
outcome."*

---

# BUYING MYTHS

## Myths 1 — The five cards · 1:30 + popouts

**Do not read the cards.** Ask: *"Which of these have you been told?"* Open the two the
room reacts to. Three at most.

- **20% down** is the one that stops the most people. Open it almost every time.
- **"FHA is only for first-time buyers"** is fast and surprising — good second pick.
- **Perfect credit** — open it if the chat skews anxious.

**Land verbatim on the 20% modal:**
> "Twenty percent isn't required. It never was. It avoids mortgage insurance — that's
> real — and somewhere along the way 'avoids one specific fee' turned into 'required.'
> And on a conventional loan that insurance is **removable.** It's a phase, not a
> sentence."

## Myths 2 — Lowest rate vs lowest cost · 3:00

**The structure is a trap, and the trap is the teaching.** Show the two loan cards.
**Stop. Ask the room which is better.** Wait for answers. Most will say B.

*Then* reveal the breakeven diagram.

**Land verbatim:**
> "Almost everyone says B — lower rate, lower payment, both things you've been trained
> to shop for. And B might be right. But you can't tell yet, because nobody's told you
> the one thing that decides it."
>
> "B's lower rate was **bought.** Points, paid in cash at closing. About five years to
> earn it back. Keep the loan longer and B wins. Sell or refinance sooner and you lit
> that money on fire for a better-looking number on a piece of paper."
>
> "And it's the **loan**, not the house. Refinancing ends the loan even if you never
> move — and most first-time buyers badly overestimate how long they'll keep one."

Open the **Lender Credits** chip if anyone asks about cash-tight buying. It's the
least-known lever in the whole deck.

# BUDGET

## Budget 1 — Rent vs buying · 2:30

The flat line beside the stepping line does the work. Let the animation run.

**Land verbatim:**
> "Two things move at once. Rent tends to go up, and home values tend to go up over
> time. So waiting isn't neutral — it usually costs something."

**Then immediately take the pressure off, or this reads as a pitch:**
> "But hear the other half. **Almost nobody's first house is their last house.** You are
> not choosing where you'll die. You're choosing where you start."

⚠️ Say out loud that the flat line is **principal and interest only** — taxes and
insurance still move. If you skip that, Budget 2 contradicts you three minutes later.

## Budget 2 — How a mortgage payment works · 3:00

The banded stack builds. Then the two columns.

**Land verbatim — the single most useful sentence in the section:**
> "**Fixed rate does not mean fixed payment.** Your rate is fixed. Your taxes and
> insurance are not — and your lender collects those monthly and pays them for you.
> When they go up, your payment goes up. Nobody's taking advantage of you. But it
> surprises people every single year."

Open **Escrow** almost always — it's the #1 source of post-closing confusion.
Open **Amortization** if the room is engaged; the refinance-resets-the-clock point is
genuinely new to most people.

## Budget 3 — Budgeting · 2:30

The waterfall cascades. Disposable income lands last and smallest.

**Land verbatim:**
> "A lender's job is to determine whether you'll repay. That's the only question the
> guidelines ask. It is **not** 'will you be okay.' Nobody in your transaction is asking
> that one — so you have to."
>
> "The number you can qualify for and the number you can comfortably live with are
> usually not the same number. And the gap is bigger than most people expect."

Open the **builder incentive / negative equity** modal if anyone is looking at new
construction. Ask — usually somebody is.

# MOST COMMON LOAN PROGRAMS

## Programs 1 — Five programs · 3:00 + popouts

**Do not walk all five.** Ask which they've heard of.

⭐ **Always say the VA line out loud, even if nobody asks:**
> "If anyone in your household served — including a spouse — check VA. Zero down, no
> monthly mortgage insurance. And people constantly assume a short enlistment doesn't
> count. It usually does. **Ask anyway.**"

That one sentence is worth more to the right person than the rest of the webinar.

## Programs 2 — Where cash to close comes from · 3:30 + popouts

**Land verbatim:**
> "Most first-time buyers assume every dollar has to come out of their own savings. It
> doesn't. There are six places this money can come from, and most buyers use more than
> one."

Open **Gift Funds** and **Down Payment Assistance** by default — highest-value, most
misunderstood. Open **Seller Credits** if the market is soft.

**On gift funds, land this:**
> "Tell your lender **before** the money moves. A gift handled correctly is routine. The
> same gift deposited quietly becomes a two-week problem."

**On DPA — say the honest part:**
> "Assistance can also weaken your offer. Some sellers see it as slower or likelier to
> fall apart. Sometimes that's fair, often it isn't — but perception affects which offer
> gets accepted, and you should know that going in."

## Programs 3 — Cash to close breakdown · 2:30

The second bar keeps going. That length difference is the whole slide.

**Land verbatim:**
> "Two lines appear on your Loan Estimate and people plan their life around the wrong
> one. **Closing costs** is what you pay to get the loan. **Cash to close** is
> everything — down payment, closing costs, prepaid items, escrows, reserves — minus
> credits. It's a much bigger number, it's on page one, and it's the one that decides
> whether you can actually get to the table."

# THE PROCESS

## Process 1 — Meet your team · 3:00 + popouts

Open **Underwriter** and **Processor** — they resolve the most anxiety.

**Land verbatim:**
> "The most useful question in this entire process is four words: **who pays you, and
> how?** Ask everybody. Nobody good is offended by it — and the ones who are have just
> told you something."

**On the processor modal:**
> "You will be asked for something you already sent. It doesn't mean anyone lost it.
> Underwriting is a checklist and the checklist doesn't know what you already did. Send
> it again, same day, and it's a non-event."

## Process 2 — The loan process · 3:00 + popouts

Walk the timeline once, quickly. Open **Underwriting** and **Conditional Approval**.

**Land verbatim:**
> "Ninety percent of the anxiety in this process is not knowing which step you're on.
> And the hardest stretch is underwriting — because it's the one that looks exactly like
> nothing happening."

> "**Conditions are normal.** Getting a list is not a rejection. It's how nearly every
> loan in America gets approved."

# MISTAKES TO AVOID

## Mistakes 1A — The Don'ts · 2:00

Eleven items. Don't read all eleven — let them animate and pull out the surprising ones.

**Land verbatim:**
> "One rule covers all eleven. **From application to keys, your financial life is a
> museum exhibit. Look, don't touch.**"

**Then the two nobody expects:**
> "Don't pay off collections unless your lender tells you to. And don't dispute anything
> on your credit report. Both of those feel like cleaning up. Both can stall your
> closing."

**Then the escape valve — say it, it matters:**
> "If something has to change — a job offer, an inheritance, a car that died — that's
> fine. Life happens. **Call your loan officer first.** Almost all of these are
> survivable with warning and fatal as a surprise."

## Mistakes 1B — The Do's · 1:30

Five items. The contrast with eleven is intentional — don't apologise for it.

**Land verbatim:**
> "Only five, and the last one does the most work. The biggest predictor of a smooth
> closing isn't your credit score or your down payment. **It's how fast you send
> documents back.** Same-day beats perfect."

## Mistakes 2 — Chasing the lowest rate · 2:00

Callback to the breakeven diagram. This should feel like recognition, not new material.

**Land verbatim:**
> "The mistake isn't paying points — points are sometimes exactly right. The mistake is
> paying them without asking the question that decides it: **how long will I keep this
> loan?** And if your honest answer is 'I don't know,' that's not a failure. It's
> information. And it argues against paying."

## Mistakes 3 — Small assumptions · 2:30 + popouts

Six cards, written as thoughts. Open two or three.

**Land verbatim — this is the emotional peak of the webinar. Slow down.**
> "None of these feel like mistakes when you make them. Every one of them feels
> reasonable. That's what makes them expensive."

*(pause)*

> "And there's one underneath all six. **Not asking a question because you're afraid of
> looking like you don't know what you're doing.**"

*(pause — two full seconds)*

> "You've done this zero times. Everyone across the table has done it thousands. That
> isn't a flaw, that's arithmetic. **You are allowed to not know this.**"

*(beat, then move — don't step on it with a transition)*

## FAILURE MODES

**Chat is dead.** Normal for the first five minutes. Ask a one-word question — *"Type
YES if you've ever looked at Zillow at midnight."* Low-cost participation unlocks
higher-cost participation. Don't beg for engagement; it reads as insecure.

**Someone demands a rate quote.** Never quote on camera.
> "I won't quote a rate on camera, and here's why rather than just dodging. Your rate
> depends on at least seven things I don't know about you — credit, down payment,
> program, property type, occupancy, term, and points. Anyone who fires back a number
> without knowing five of those is quoting you a rate you may not qualify for. That's
> not a quote, that's bait. If you want a real one, that's a twenty-minute conversation
> and I'm happy to have it."

**Someone asks about a competitor.**
> "I won't grade another company on camera. Send me their Loan Estimate and I'll show
> you how to grade it yourself — that's more useful than my opinion anyway."

**Out of your lane** — legal, tax, contract interpretation:
> "That's genuinely outside what I should answer. You want a real answer from a
> [CPA / real estate attorney / your agent], not my approximation. Here's what I'd ask
> them."

**One person dominates.** Answer fully once. Answer briefly twice. Third time:
*"Your situation has enough specifics that it deserves a real conversation — email me
and I'll take it properly."* Warm, then move and don't return.

**Running long.** Open fewer popouts. That's the whole compression plan and it's why the
deck is built this way — every popout is optional by design.
