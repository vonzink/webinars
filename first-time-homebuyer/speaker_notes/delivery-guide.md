## Failure modes

### Chat is dead
Normal in the first 5 minutes. Don't panic-fill.

- Ask a question with a **one-word answer**. "Type YES if you've ever looked at
  Zillow at midnight." Low-cost participation unlocks higher-cost participation.
- Use the moderator: seeded questions per AMA break are in the module files.
- If it's still dead at minute 15, say it out loud, lightly: *"Chat's quiet, which
  is fine — but I'd rather answer your question than my own. Anything, however
  basic. Especially basic."* Naming it usually breaks it.
- **Do not** guilt the audience or repeatedly ask for engagement. It reads as
  insecure and it costs you the room.

### One person dominates
- Answer their first question fully — they may be asking what ten people are
  thinking.
- Second: answer briefly, then *"Let me grab a few others and come back to you."*
- Third: *"[Name], your situation has enough specifics that it deserves a real
  conversation — email me and I'll take it properly."* Warm, not dismissive. Then
  move and do not return.
- The moderator should be prioritizing by upvote, which handles most of this
  structurally.

### 🔴 Someone demands a live rate quote
**Compliance trap. Never quote a rate on camera.** Scripted answer:

> "I'm not going to quote a rate on camera, and I want to tell you why rather than
> just dodging. Your rate depends on at least seven things I don't know about you —
> credit, down payment, program, property type, occupancy, term, and points. Anyone
> who fires back a number without knowing five of those is quoting you a rate you
> may not qualify for. That's not a quote, that's bait. If you want a real one,
> that's a twenty-minute conversation and I'm happy to have it."

This turns a compliance risk into a credibility moment. It's slide 042's argument,
delivered on demand.

### Someone asks about a competitor
> "I won't grade another company on camera. But send me their Loan Estimate and I'll
> show you how to grade it yourself. That's more useful than my opinion anyway."

### The question is outside your lane
Legal, tax, contract interpretation, investment advice.

> "That's genuinely outside what I should be answering — it's a [CPA / real estate
> attorney / your agent] question, and you want a real answer, not my
> approximation. Here's who I'd ask and what I'd ask them."

**Saying this is a trust asset, not a weakness.** It proves the rest of your answers
are inside your lane.

### A demo fails
**Bail in under 5 seconds.** Do not troubleshoot on camera. Bridge lines are in each
`resources/*-demo.md`. Advance to the fallback slide and make the same argument.
The audience will not know the difference — but they will absolutely notice you
fighting with a browser.

### You realize you got something wrong
Correct it plainly, immediately, once. *"I misspoke a minute ago — it's X, not Y."*
Then keep going. Don't over-apologize; a clean correction increases credibility.
Rumination destroys it.

### Someone is hostile or clearly not the audience
Answer the substance once, neutrally, in one sentence. Don't take the bait, don't
be defensive, don't be clever at their expense. Move. The moderator can hide
further messages if needed.

### You're losing the room (visible drop-off, chat dying at once)
Something got too abstract. Get to a story or a number immediately — skip ahead to
the nearest one. The stories are the recovery mechanism.

## Pacing

| Beat | Pace |
|---|---|
| Cold open (001–003) | **Slow.** Earn attention before you spend it. |
| Process map (010) | Brisk — it's orientation, not depth |
| Stories | **Slow.** Especially the turn. |
| Data slides | Steady. Say every number out loud. |
| Demos | Deliberate. The moves are the point. |
| Mistakes (068–069) | **Fast** — 15 seconds each. Then **stop completely** for the last one. |
| The close | Slow. Let the last line sit before opening Q&A. |

**The two scripted silences — both are load-bearing:**
- **Slide 019** — 15 seconds while they write down their Number. Let it be awkward.
- **Slide 051** — 2 seconds after "$545,000." Do not fill it.

## Things that are easy to forget

- **Say numbers out loud.** Half the audience is on the replay and some aren't
  looking at the screen.
- **Define every term the first time.** Under 10 words.
- **Say the email address aloud** at the close — replay viewers can't click chat.
- **Slide 067: state the conflict of interest.** It's scripted. Don't skip it
  because it feels awkward — the awkwardness is exactly what makes it work.
- **Hold Poll 3's results** before revealing slide 032. The reveal is the lesson.
- **Read the poll results aloud.** Not everyone can see them.
- **Log Poll 1 and Poll 5.** They're the measurement instrument.

## Rehearsal plan

**T-7:** Read the full script aloud once, unstopped. Find the sentences you stumble
on — rewrite them, don't practice them. Stumbling means it's written, not spoken.

**T-3:** Full run with a stopwatch, slides advancing. Log actual time at each of the
6 checkpoints. Fix anything over by 30 seconds *structurally* — don't plan to talk
faster.

**T-2:** Demos only. All three, five times each, including the failure bridge.
Practice bailing to fallback until it's smooth.

**T-1:** Full run at delivery pace with the real setup, real network, real screen
share. Then stop. Don't rehearse the day of — it flattens delivery.
