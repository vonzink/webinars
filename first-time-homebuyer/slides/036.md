# Slide 036

**Module:** 4 · **Time:** 15:50 · **Duration:** 30s · **Type:** Story + Data

## Purpose
Surface VA eligibility to people who don't know they have it — and deliver the
meta-lesson that carries the whole webinar: **ask anyway.** Story 4.

## Headline
**He was 34. Nobody had ever asked him.**

## On-slide content
**VA — 0% down · no monthly mortgage insurance**
- Qualifies on **residual income**, not a hard DTI cap
- Funding fee applies, financeable — **waived** with service-connected disability
  compensation
- **Reusable.** Not once per lifetime.
- Active duty · veterans · **National Guard and Reserve** · certain surviving spouses

> **Get your Certificate of Eligibility. Even if you think it doesn't count.**

*Program guidelines. Eligibility determined by VA.*

## Speaker notes
> "A few years back I'm on a first call with a guy. Good job, decent credit,
> scraping together three and a half percent for an FHA loan. Halfway through,
> totally offhand, he mentions he'd 'done four years in the Army after high
> school.'"

> "I stopped the call. I said — you know you can buy this house with nothing down?
> And no monthly mortgage insurance at all?"

*(Beat.)*

> "He didn't. **He was thirty-four. Nobody had ever asked him.**"

*(Beat.)*

> "VA loans. Zero down, no monthly mortgage insurance — the two biggest costs, gone.
> Competitive rates. And it qualifies on **residual income** — how much cash is
> actually left after everything, given your family size and where you live — which
> is honestly a more humane test than a pure ratio."

> "There's a funding fee, it's financeable, and it's **waived** for veterans
> receiving compensation for a service-connected disability. And it's **reusable** —
> it is not once per lifetime. That one's a myth."

> "So: active duty, veterans, **National Guard, Reserves**, certain surviving
> spouses. Get your Certificate of Eligibility."

> "And if you're sitting there thinking 'it was only a few years, it probably
> doesn't count' — **ask anyway.** That's the whole lesson of that story. He almost
> paid tens of thousands of dollars for the privilege of not asking a question."
