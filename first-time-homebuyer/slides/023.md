# Slide 023

**Module:** 3 · **Time:** 10:00 · **Duration:** 35s · **Type:** Build

## Purpose
Add the costs no calculator includes, and convert an abstract caveat into a
specific monthly number.

## Headline
**The five nobody puts in the calculator.**

## On-slide content
1. **Maintenance** — plan 1–2% of value per year *(and it won't be smooth)*
2. **Utility delta** — a house is not an apartment
3. **Furnishing** — the empty-house tax
4. **Commute change** — cheaper house, farther out
5. **Escalation** — taxes and insurance go up

**Planning figure: +$450/month → $3,633**
Renting at $2,150. **That's a $1,483 change.**

*Hypothetical illustration for education only.*

## Speaker notes
> "Five things. No calculator online includes them."

> "**Maintenance.** Plan one to two percent of the value per year. On this house
> that's three seventy-five to seven fifty a month. And it will not be smooth —
> nothing for two years and then a fourteen-thousand-dollar roof."

> "**Utilities.** A house is not an apartment. Budget another hundred to two."

> "**Furnishing** — the empty-house tax. It's real, it's three to ten thousand
> dollars, and it is the number one reason people put furniture on a credit card in
> month one. Remember that. It comes back at minute thirty-one."

> "**Commute.** Cheaper house, farther out, two hundred a month in gas and an hour
> of your life."

> "**Escalation.** Taxes and insurance go up. Insurance in Colorado has gone up
> meaningfully — wildfire and hail. Assume increases."

*(Final click.)*

> "So Maya and Dev's real number isn't thirty-one eighty-three. It's about
> **thirty-six thirty-three.** They're renting at twenty-one fifty. That's not a
> thousand-dollar change. **It's fourteen eighty-three.** That's the number that
> decides whether this is a good year or a bad one."

> "And one more thing: **the emergency fund is not part of the down payment.** If
> buying the house requires spending it, you're buying too much house."
