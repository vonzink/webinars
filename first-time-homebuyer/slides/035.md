# Slide 035

**Module:** 4 · **Time:** 15:15 · **Duration:** 35s · **Type:** Data

## Purpose
Present FHA fairly and deliver the MIP-duration fact that changes the decision —
without discouraging a program that helps enormous numbers of people.

## Headline
**FHA: great program. One tradeoff nobody mentions.**

## On-slide content
**3.5% down at 580+** · more forgiving on credit, collections, past derogatories ·
higher DTI tolerance in many cases

**The tradeoff:**
- Upfront MIP, financed into the loan
- Annual MIP, paid monthly
- **At less than 10% down, MIP generally lasts the LIFE OF THE LOAN**
- At 10%+ down, it generally drops after 11 years

> Conventional MI has an exit. FHA MI usually doesn't.

*Program guidelines. Subject to change. Lender overlays apply.*

## Graphic
Program card. Two bars side by side showing MI duration: conventional draining to
zero at the 80% mark; FHA running the full length of the bar. The visual asymmetry
is the point.

## Animation
Card, then the two duration bars. The FHA bar's failure to end is the beat.

## Speaker notes
> "FHA. Three and a half percent down at a 580 score. More forgiving on credit
> history, on collections, on past derogatory events. Higher DTI tolerance in a lot
> of scenarios. It is a genuinely great program and it has helped an enormous
> number of people become owners. I'm not talking you out of it."

*(Beat.)*

> "But here's the tradeoff nobody mentions at the sales table. There's an upfront
> mortgage insurance premium financed into the loan, and an annual one paid
> monthly. And **on most FHA loans with less than ten percent down, that annual
> premium lasts the life of the loan.** Not until you hit twenty percent equity.
> The life of the loan."

> "Put ten percent or more down and it drops off after eleven years."

*(Beat.)*

> "So the honest framing: FHA's monthly cost is often competitive, sometimes better
> than conventional — especially if your credit is in the 600s, where conventional
> pricing gets expensive fast. **But conventional's mortgage insurance has an exit
> and FHA's usually doesn't.**"

> "Which means for a lot of FHA buyers the plan isn't 'FHA forever.' It's **FHA to
> get in, refinance out when it makes sense.** That is a completely legitimate
> strategy. It's just one you should choose **on purpose**, knowing the exit is a
> future refinance that depends on future rates. **Nobody can promise you that
> refinance. Anyone who does is selling.**"

## Transition
> "Now — the most underused benefit in America, and a story about a guy nobody ever
> asked."
