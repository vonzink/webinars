# Slide 025

**Module:** 3 · **Time:** 11:05 · **Duration:** 25s · **Type:** Data · `[EXP-1]`

## Purpose
Show the canonical example's actual ratios — and then deliver the module's central
argument: the ratio would happily approve them for more.

## Headline
**They could buy $60,000 more. The ratio has no opinion about their life.**

## On-slide content
Maya & Dev — $9,500/mo gross · $780/mo other debt

- **Front-end** (housing only) — $3,183 ÷ $9,500 = **33.5%**
- **Back-end** (housing + debt) — $3,963 ÷ $9,500 = **41.7%**

Conventional commonly to ~45% · can stretch toward ~50% with strong AUS findings
VA uses **residual income** instead of a hard cap

*Hypothetical illustration for education only. DTI limits vary by program and are
determined by automated underwriting findings.*

## Graphic
Two gauges: 33.5% and 41.7%. The 45% and 50% redlines marked. A ghosted third
gauge shows where 45% would put them, with "+$60,000 of house" beside it.

## Animation
Gauges fill. The ghost gauge appears last, on click.

## Speaker notes
> "Maya and Dev make ninety-five hundred a month gross. They've got seven eighty in
> other debt — car, student loan, credit card minimums."

> "**Front-end** — housing alone over income — **thirty-three and a half percent.**
> **Back-end** — housing plus everything — **forty-one point seven.**"

> "Back-end is the one that matters. Conventional loans commonly go to about
> forty-five percent, and with strong automated-underwriting findings can stretch
> toward fifty. FHA can go higher with compensating factors. VA doesn't use a hard
> cap the same way — it uses **residual income**, which is honestly a smarter test:
> how much cash is left after everything, given your family size and where you live."

*(Beat. Then the click.)*

> "But watch this, because this is the whole point of the module. At forty-one
> seven, they're comfortably approvable. **They could go to forty-five and buy about
> sixty thousand dollars more house.** Their DTI would still say yes."

> "Their life would say something else. **The ratio has no opinion about their
> life.**"
