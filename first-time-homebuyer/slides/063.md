# Slide 063

**Module:** 7 · **Time:** 30:50 · **Duration:** 50s · **Type:** Data + Poll

## Purpose
Point the audience at the one factor that can move fast, correct the close-your-card
myth, and remove the "I need perfect credit" blocker.

## Headline
**Utilization is 30% of your score. It moves in 30 days.**

## On-slide content

| Factor | ~Weight | How fast |
|---|---|---|
| Payment history | ~35% | Slow — a 30-day late lingers for years |
| **Amounts owed / utilization** | **~30%** | **Fast — 30 to 60 days** |
| Length of history | ~15% | Time only |
| New credit | ~10% | Fast in, slow out |
| Credit mix | ~10% | Slow |

**The three fastest legitimate moves:**
1. Pay each card **under 30%** of its limit — ideally under 10%. *Per card.*
2. **Do not close old cards.**
3. Dispute genuine errors — **but early**, not during your loan.

> **You don't need a 780.** Pricing moves in tiers. 735→745 can matter. 780→800
> usually does nothing.

## Animation
Donut. Poll overlay. Then the three moves.

## Speaker notes
> "Five factors. Approximate weights."

*(Walk it fast.)*

> "**Utilization is the lever.** Thirty percent of your score, and it can move in one
> billing cycle. That's the whole game if you're trying to improve before you buy."

**POLL 4 (15 seconds):**
> "Quick one. **True or false — closing a credit card you don't use helps your
> score.**"

*(Results come in heavily "true.")*

> "**False.** And this one gets almost everybody, so don't feel bad. Closing an
> account shortens your average history and removes available credit — which raises
> your utilization. The instinct to 'clean up my credit' before applying is the
> right instinct pointed at exactly the wrong thing."

> "So: **pay balances down — under thirty percent per card, ideally under ten. Per
> card, not just overall** — one maxed card among five drags you. **Don't close
> anything.** And dispute real errors, but do it **early** — an active dispute flag
> can stall underwriting."

*(Beat.)*

> "And the thing nobody says: **you do not need a 780.** Pricing moves in tiers. 735
> to 745 can matter. 780 to 800 usually does nothing. Chasing a perfect score is a
> way to delay buying for no benefit. Find out which tier you're in and whether
> you're close to the next one — that's a five-minute question."
