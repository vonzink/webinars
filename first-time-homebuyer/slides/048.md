# Slide 048

**Module:** 5 · **Time:** 22:05 · **Duration:** 30s · **Type:** Statement

## Purpose
Define points, and introduce lender credits — the reverse trade that is frequently
right for a cash-tight first-time buyer and is almost never offered unprompted.

## Headline
**A point is prepaid interest. It's a bet.**

## On-slide content
**One point = 1% of the loan amount**, paid at closing, to permanently lower your
rate.
On $427,500 → **one point = $4,275**

*It's an annual gym membership instead of paying monthly. Cheaper per month. A
waste if you move in March.*

**And the same trade runs backwards:**
> **Lender credit** — take a *higher* rate, the lender pays some of your closing
> costs. **Nobody offers this. Ask for it.**

## Graphic
Left: a cash stack flowing into a payment bar that shrinks. Right: the arrow
reversed — the payment bar grows, cash flows back to the buyer. Symmetry is the
point.

## Animation
Forward flow, then reverse on click.

## Speaker notes
> "One 'point' is one percent of the loan amount, paid at closing, to permanently
> lower your rate. On four twenty-seven five, one point is **four thousand two
> hundred seventy-five dollars.**"

> "It's prepaid interest. You're handing over cash today to reduce every payment
> for as long as you keep the loan. Like buying a **gym membership for the year
> instead of paying monthly** — cheaper per month, and a total waste if you move in
> March."

*(Click — reverse the flow.)*

> "Now the part almost nobody tells first-time buyers: **the same trade runs
> backwards.** It's called a **lender credit.** You take a *higher* rate, and the
> lender pays some of your closing costs."

> "And that is frequently the right move for a first-time buyer who is cash-tight —
> which is most first-time buyers. **Cash today can be worth more to you than a
> slightly lower payment.** Nobody offers this unprompted. **Ask for it.**"

## Transition
> "So how do you decide? One number."
