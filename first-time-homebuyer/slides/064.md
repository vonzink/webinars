# Slide 064

**Module:** 7 · **Time:** 31:40 · **Duration:** 110s · **Type:** Story + Artifact · **HERO**

## Purpose
Remove the fear that blocks shopping, then deliver the list that saves the most
actual money in the room. Story 7. Artifact #6. Seeds mistake #4.

## Headline
**Nine days before closing, he bought a couch.**

## On-slide content
**First — the 45-day window:**
Mortgage inquiries within a shopping window — commonly **14 to 45 days** depending
on the scoring model — count as **one inquiry.**
> **The system was deliberately designed so you could shop.**

**ARTIFACT #6 — THE DO-NOT-DO LIST** *(application → keys)*
1. No new credit — cards, cars, furniture, phones, buy-now-pay-later
2. Don't close existing accounts
3. No large purchases on existing cards
4. No job changes — especially W-2 → self-employed
5. No moving money without a paper trail
6. No accepting a cash gift without telling your lender **first**
7. Don't co-sign anything
8. Don't miss a payment on anything

> **From application to keys, your financial life is a museum exhibit.
> Look, don't touch.**

## Speaker notes
**First — the window.**

> "The fear that stops people from shopping: won't three lenders wreck my credit?"

> "**No.** Mortgage inquiries within a shopping window — commonly described as
> fourteen to forty-five days depending on the scoring model — are treated as **one
> inquiry.** The system was deliberately designed so you could shop."

> "Let me be blunt about who benefits from you not knowing that. **Getting three
> Loan Estimates costs you essentially nothing in score.** Not getting them cost
> that woman on the earlier slide six thousand four hundred dollars."

**EXERCISE 3 (30 seconds):**
> "Quick — how many credit applications have you opened in the last twelve months?
> Card, car, store financing, phone plan, buy-now-pay-later. Count them. Number in
> the chat."

**Then — Story 7.**

> "Nine days before closing, a client bought furniture. Nine thousand dollars, zero
> percent for twenty-four months, at the store, because it was zero percent and he
> was excited. And honestly — who wouldn't be? He was about to have a house with
> nothing in it."

> "Lenders re-pull credit before closing. **Every time.** The new debt hit, his
> ratios moved, and his approval — which was already issued — came apart. He did
> not close on time."

*(Beat. Genuine sympathy here, zero mockery.)*

> "He didn't do anything reckless. **He bought a couch for a house he'd already been
> approved for. Nobody told him the approval was still alive.**"

*(The list.)*

> "So — eight things, from application to keys."

*(Read all eight, fast.)*

> "One rule that covers all of them: **from application to keys, your financial life
> is a museum exhibit. Look, don't touch.**"

> "And the escape valve, because life happens: if something **has** to change — a
> job offer, an inheritance, a car that died — that's fine. **Call your loan officer
> first.** Almost every one of these is survivable with warning and fatal as a
> surprise. That's **mistake number four.**"

## Transition

> where you buy changes the math more than most first-time buyers realize."
