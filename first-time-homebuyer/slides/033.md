# Slide 033

**Module:** 4 · **Time:** 14:00 · **Duration:** 40s · **Type:** Story

## Purpose
Make the cost of the 20% myth concrete — while keeping the counterweight that makes
it honest rather than propaganda. Story 3. Seeds mistake #6.

## Headline
**They spent six years avoiding a temporary fee.**

## On-slide content
**6 years saving. The target moved every year.**
Eligible for 3% down the entire time.

## Graphic
Two parallel six-year timelines. Top: rent payments accumulating, no equity, and a
20% target line that visibly rises each year. Bottom: the same six years with a
mortgage — MI (Aspen Gold) present for a few years, then dropping off, equity
building underneath.

## Animation
Both timelines advance year by year, in sync. The rising target line is the visual
argument — it should be slightly infuriating to watch.

## Speaker notes
> "Tom and Ashley decided they'd do it right. Twenty percent, no mortgage insurance,
> no shortcuts. Heads down, and they saved."

> "It took six years. And they did it — they hit their number. I want to be clear
> that I admire the discipline, because it is real discipline."

*(Beat.)*

> "But here's what happened during those six years. Prices in their target area went
> up. So the twenty percent target went up with them — it's a percentage of a moving
> number. **Every year they saved, the finish line moved.** And they paid six more
> years of rent, which bought them exactly zero equity."

> "Meanwhile, they were eligible for a three-percent-down conventional loan the
> entire time. They'd have paid mortgage insurance — call it a bit over a hundred
> dollars a month — for a handful of years until they hit twenty percent equity
> through payments and appreciation. Then it comes off. Permanently."

> "**They spent six years avoiding a temporary fee.**"

*(Beat. Shift tone — this next part is not optional.)*

> "Now — the honest counterweight, because I told you I wouldn't sell you anything.
> **This is not a law of nature.** If they'd bought right before a downturn, this
> story reads differently. Waiting is not always wrong."

> "**But it should be a decision, not a default.** Theirs was a default. They never
> ran the comparison, because nobody ever told them there was one to run. That's
> **mistake number six.**"

## Transition
> "So let's go through the actual options. Four programs, and I'm going to tell you
> the downside of each one."
