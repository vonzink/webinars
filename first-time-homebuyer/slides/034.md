# Slide 034

**Module:** 4 · **Time:** 14:40 · **Duration:** 35s · **Type:** Data

## Purpose
Introduce the 3%-down conventional products and flag HomeReady/Home Possible as
chronically under-mentioned.

## Headline
**Conventional at 3% — and the one nobody tells you about.**

## On-slide content
**Conventional 97** — 3% down · 30-year fixed · first-time buyer
> *First-time buyer = no primary-residence ownership in the last **3 years.***
> You can have owned before.

**HomeReady** (Fannie) / **Home Possible** (Freddie) — 3% down
- Income limits by area
- **Reduced MI coverage and better pricing** at the same LTV
- Flexible income: boarder income, non-occupant co-borrowers
- Homebuyer education generally required

> **The conventional advantage: the mortgage insurance is removable.**

## Speaker notes
> "**Conventional 97.** Three percent down, thirty-year fixed, generally requires at
> least one first-time buyer."

> "And 'first-time buyer' does not mean what you think. Most programs define it as
> **no ownership interest in a primary residence in the last three years.** So if
> you owned something a decade ago, you're probably a first-time buyer again. That
> surprises people right out of assistance they're eligible for."

*(Click.)*

> "**HomeReady and Home Possible.** Fannie and Freddie. Three percent down, income
> limits by area — and here's the part that matters: **reduced mortgage insurance
> coverage and better pricing** at the same loan-to-value than standard
> conventional."

> "If you're income-eligible, these are frequently the best product available to a
> first-time buyer, and they are **chronically under-mentioned.** Ask about them by
> name."

> "Homebuyer education is generally required — it's an online course. Do it early.
> It takes a couple of hours and it's a condition that holds up closings when people
> leave it to the end."

*(Beat.)*

> "And the big one, which comes back in four minutes: **conventional mortgage
> insurance is removable.**"
