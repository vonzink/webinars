## Purpose
Prevent over-trust in APR before the demo — so the audience understands the demo's
payload rather than just watching numbers move.

## Headline
**Where APR lies to you.**

## On-slide content
1. **It assumes you keep the loan 30 years.** Most people don't. Upfront costs get
   spread across months you'll never use.
2. **ARMs** — APR has to guess at future adjustments using today's index.
3. **Lenders don't all include identical items**, and shoppable third-party costs
   muddy the comparison.

> **Use APR as a flag, not a verdict.** A big gap between rate and APR means big
> upfront costs. Go find them.

## Speaker notes
> "APR is the best single comparison number you have. It is also flawed in three
> specific ways, and you should know them."

> "**One — it assumes you keep the loan the entire term.** Thirty years. Most people
> don't come close. So APR spreads your upfront costs across three hundred sixty
> months you're never going to use."

> "**Two — adjustable-rate mortgages.** APR has to guess at future adjustments using
> today's index. It's a projection, not a fact."

> "**Three — lenders don't all include identical items**, and there are third-party
> costs you can shop for that muddy the comparison."

*(Beat.)*

> "So: **use APR as a flag, not a verdict.** A big gap between the rate and the APR
> means big upfront costs. That's what it's for — it tells you where to look."
