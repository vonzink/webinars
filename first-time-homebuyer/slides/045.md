# Slide 045

**Module:** 5 · **Time:** 20:25 · **Duration:** 35s · **Type:** Data · **HERO**

## Purpose
Set the trap. Get the audience to publicly commit to "Loan B" so the reveal at 047
and 049 has something to overturn.

## Headline
**Which one is better?**

## On-slide content
Same house. Same $427,500. Two lenders. Same day.

| | **Loan A** | **Loan B** |
|---|---|---|
| Rate | 6.750% | **6.250%** |
| Discount points | 0 | **2.00 = $8,550** |
| Other lender fees | $1,400 | $1,400 |
| **Monthly P&I** | $2,773 | **$2,632** |

*Hypothetical illustration for education only. Not a quote, offer, or commitment to
lend.*

## Graphic
**HERO.** Two loan cards side by side, equal visual weight. **Deliberately withhold
APR** — it doesn't appear until slide 047.

## Animation
Cards reveal together. The question hangs. No further build.

## Speaker notes
> "Same house. Same loan amount. Two lenders, same day."

> "**Loan A** — six point seven five, no points. **Loan B** — six point two five,
> but two points, which on this loan is **eighty-five hundred and fifty dollars**
> at closing."

> "Monthly payment: twenty-seven seventy-three versus **twenty-six thirty-two.**"

*(Beat.)*

> "Which one's better?"

*(Chat prompt: "Type A or B." **Genuinely wait.** Most will say B.)*

> "Almost everyone says B. B has the lower rate and the lower payment — both things
> you've been trained to shop for."

*(Beat.)*

> "And B **might** be better. But you cannot tell yet, because nobody has told you
> the only thing that decides it."

## Transition
> "Before I show you — one warning about APR itself."
