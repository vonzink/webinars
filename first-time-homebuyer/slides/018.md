# Slide 018

**Module:** 3 · **Time:** 7:05 · **Duration:** 45s · **Type:** Story

## Purpose
Establish that "approved" and "comfortable" are different numbers — and that
choosing the smaller one is a strength, not timidity. Story 2. Seeds mistake #7.

## Headline
**"I didn't buy a smaller house. I bought a smaller amount of fear."**

## On-slide content
$520,000 *(approved)* → **$390,000** *(bought)*

## Graphic
Two house silhouettes, sized proportionally. The smaller one glows in Alpenglow.
The larger sits in grey. Quote appears large across the bottom at the turn.

## Animation
Houses first. The quote lands on click, after the story.

## Speaker notes
> "Priya came to me pre-approved for five hundred twenty thousand. Real number,
> real letter, she qualified for every dollar of it."

> "She bought at three ninety."

> "And I want to be careful here, because I'm not telling you she was scared or
> that she settled. She ran the math and decided she'd rather have nine hundred
> dollars a month than a fourth bedroom."

> "Two years later her car needed a transmission, her company did a layoff round
> she survived but did not enjoy, and her sister needed help. Same spring. All
> three. She handled all of it and never missed a beat."

*(Beat. Then click.)*

> "She told me something I've repeated ever since. She said: **'I didn't buy a
> smaller house. I bought a smaller amount of fear.'**"

*(Beat.)*

> "Here's the uncomfortable part, and I'm including myself in this. **The mortgage
> industry approves you at the top of your range.** That's not a conspiracy — it's
> that our guidelines measure whether you'll *repay*, not whether you'll be
> *okay*. Those are different questions, and only one of them is being asked on
> your behalf."

> "That's **mistake number seven.** So you're going to ask the other one. Right now."

## Transition
> "Everybody — I need you to actually do something. Paper, phone, whatever you've
> got."
