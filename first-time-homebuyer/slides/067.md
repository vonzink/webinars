# Slide 067

**Module:** 8 · **Time:** 34:20 · **Duration:** 100s + AMA · **Type:** Story + Artifact · **HERO**

## Purpose
Demonstrate intellectual honesty by arguing against the presenter's own financial
interest — the single strongest trust move in the webinar. Story 8. Artifact #7.
Seeds mistake #8.

## Headline
**The builder's lender won. I told him to take it.**

## On-slide content
```
Builder credit                          $15,000
Rate 0.25% higher ≈ $70/mo × 7 years    −$5,880
Extra Section A                         −$3,000
─────────────────────────────────────────────────
NET VALUE OF THE INCENTIVE               $6,120   ← builder wins
```

**ARTIFACT #7 — 12 Questions to Ask a Builder** *(all 12 in the download kit)*
**6.** What's the incentive if I **don't** use your preferred lender?
**7.** Can I take a **price reduction** instead of the incentive?
**9.** Can I get an **independent inspection** before drywall and before closing?

*Hypothetical illustration for education only.*

## Speaker notes
> "Client's under contract on a new build. Builder offers **fifteen thousand dollars
> toward closing costs — if he uses their preferred lender.**"

> "He calls me and says, look, I like you, but you can't beat fifteen thousand
> dollars. And honestly? He might have been right. So we ran it."

> "Their lender: fifteen thousand credit, rate a quarter point higher than what he
> could get elsewhere, and about three thousand more in Section A."

> "On his loan, a quarter point was roughly seventy dollars a month. Over the seven
> years he expected to stay — about fifty-nine hundred. Plus three thousand more in
> fees. So the fifteen-thousand-dollar credit was really worth about **six thousand
> one hundred** against the alternative."

*(Beat. Then say it plainly.)*

> "**And it still won.** Six thousand dollars is six thousand dollars. He used their
> lender. **And I told him to.**"

*(Beat. This next paragraph is the whole module. Do not skip it.)*

> "I'm telling you that story on purpose, because I want you to hear it from someone
> with the opposite incentive. **'Don't use the builder's lender' is literally my
> business interest.** So hear it from me specifically: **sometimes the builder's
> lender is the right answer.** Sometimes the incentive is bigger than the spread."

> "What is **never** right is taking the incentive without running the comparison.
> And the reason builders can offer these at all is that hardly anyone runs it."

> "So the move is simple. **Take the builder's Loan Estimate. Get one outside
> estimate. Compare Section A, and convert the rate difference into dollars over how
> long you'll actually keep the loan.** Same skill you learned six minutes ago. Ten
> minutes of arithmetic. That's **mistake number eight.**"

> "And a builder can't stop you from shopping. If anyone implies you're *required*
> to use their lender to buy the house, that's worth asking pointed questions
> about."

*(The questions — read 5, 6, 7, and 9 aloud only.)*

> "Question six is the one they don't expect. Question seven occasionally saves the
> most money. And question nine — **get an independent inspection on a brand new
> house.** People skip it because it's new. New houses are built by humans on
> deadlines. Inspect it. Twice."

**AMA BREAK 3 — 45 seconds.**
Seeded: *"Can I use my own agent on a new build?"* → **Yes — but register them on
your first visit**, before you tour alone, or you may lose the ability to.
