# Slide 039

**Module:** 4 · **Time:** 17:20 · **Duration:** 40s · **Type:** Build

## Purpose
Reframe mortgage insurance from a permanent penalty into a removable phase, and
deliver Artifact #3.

## Headline
**Mortgage insurance is a phase, not a sentence.**

## On-slide content

| Type | Where | Removable? |
|---|---|---|
| **BPMI** — borrower-paid monthly | Conventional | **Yes** |
| **LPMI** — lender-paid, higher rate | Conventional | No — only by refinancing |
| **FHA MIP** | FHA | Usually **no** at <10% down |
| **VA funding fee / USDA guarantee fee** | VA / USDA | One-time (+ USDA annual) |

**Killing BPMI — three routes:**
1. **Request at 80% LTV** — you have to ask. Nobody calls you.
2. **Automatic at 78% LTV** — original schedule, primary residence, current
   payments *(Homeowners Protection Act)*
3. **Appreciation-based** — new appraisal. **Servicer/investor rules vary.**

**ARTIFACT #3 — the 5-question decision tree**

## Speaker notes
> "Four kinds of mortgage insurance, and they're not interchangeable."

*(Walk the table quickly.)*

> "The one that matters: **BPMI on conventional is removable.** Three routes."

> "**One — request removal at eighty percent loan-to-value.** Based on the original
> value, once you've paid down to eighty. **You have to ask. Nobody calls you.**"

> "**Two — automatic termination at seventy-eight percent**, by the original
> amortization schedule, primary residence, payments current. That's federal law."

> "**Three — appreciation-based**, with a new appraisal. And here I have to hedge:
> **servicer and investor rules vary**, including seasoning requirements. It's not
> an entitlement. Call your servicer and ask for their policy in writing."

> "On Maya and Dev's loan: eighty percent of four-fifty is three hundred sixty
> thousand. They start at four twenty-seven five. They need sixty-seven thousand
> five hundred of paydown — or an appraisal that says the house went up."

> "**That hundred twenty-five a month is not forever. It's a phase.** Knowing that
> changes the whole emotional weight of it."

*(Decision tree.)*

> "Five questions. Did you or your spouse serve? VA, full stop, check it first. Is
> the property USDA-eligible and are you under the income limit? USDA. Is your
> credit under about six-sixty, or do you have recent derogatory history? FHA is
> likely. Are you under the HomeReady income limit? That's probably your best one.
> None of the above? Conventional three to five percent down, and compare it against
> FHA on total cost."

> "**Then, whatever the tree says — layer down payment assistance on top.** DPA is
> not a program you pick instead. It's a floor you stack under the one you picked."

investor rules vary" is required language. HPA applies to primary residences.
