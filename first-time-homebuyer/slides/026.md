# Slide 026

**Module:** 3 · **Time:** 11:30 · **Duration:** 30s · **Type:** Data · `[CUT-3]`

## Purpose
Correct the two most common misconceptions about what underwriting counts — and
name the one that hurts (childcare).

## Headline
**What counts. What doesn't.**

## On-slide content

| | **Counts** | **Doesn't count** |
|---|---|---|
| **Income** | Salary · hourly · 2-yr average of OT/bonus/commission · self-employment **net after write-offs** · documented rental | Unreported cash · a promised raise · a side gig with no history · "my parents will help" |
| **Debt** | Car · student loans (even deferred) · card minimums · personal loans · support paid · co-signed loans you don't pay | Utilities · phone · insurance · groceries · **childcare** |

## Graphic
Two-column table, Pine for "counts," Stone for "doesn't." Childcare highlighted in
Alpenglow in the "doesn't count" cell.

## Animation
Reveal by row. Childcare highlights last.

## Speaker notes
> "Fast, because people always ask."

> "**Income that counts:** salary, hourly at your average hours, overtime and bonus
> and commission on a two-year average, self-employment income, documented rental."

> "**Doesn't count:** cash tips you don't report, a raise you've been promised, a
> side gig with no history, and money your parents 'will help with.'"

> "**Debt that counts:** car, student loans — even deferred ones, a percentage or
> an IBR payment gets used and the rule varies by program — card minimums, personal
> loans, support you pay, and co-signed loans **you don't even pay.**"

*(Click.)*

> "**And here's the one that hurts.** Childcare doesn't count. Daycare can be
> fifteen hundred a month and underwriting will not see a dollar of it. **Your
> budget has to.** That's exactly the gap between the bank's budget and yours."

> "One more, for the self-employed: **write-offs.** If you had a great year on paper
> and wrote it all down to nothing for taxes, we qualify you on the nothing. Talk
> to a lender the year *before* you buy, not the March after."
