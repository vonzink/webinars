# Slide 053

**Module:** 6 · **Time:** 24:35 · **Duration:** 40s · **Type:** Story

## Purpose
Prove that the same rate can hide a five-figure difference — and that the
protection already exists, unread. Story 5. Seeds mistake #3.

## Headline
**Same rate. $6,400 apart.**

## On-slide content
Two Loan Estimates. Same term. Same loan amount. **Same interest rate.**
Section A: **~$1,300** vs. **~$7,700**

> *"She wasn't defrauded. Every number was disclosed correctly, on the government
> form, exactly as required. **The disclosure worked perfectly.**"*

## Graphic
Two documents side by side. The rate circled in Pine on both — visibly identical.
Section A circled in Clay on both — visibly not. **$6,400** stamped between them.

## Animation
Rate circles first (they match — let that land). Then Section A circles. Then the
number.

## Speaker notes
> "A client sent me two Loan Estimates and asked which was better. Both thirty-year
> fixed. Both the same loan amount. **Both exactly the same interest rate.**"

> "She'd already decided it was a coin flip. Same rate, right?"

*(Beat. Click.)*

> "Section A on the first one — that's origination charges, the lender's own fees —
> was about **thirteen hundred dollars.** Section A on the second was about **seven
> thousand seven hundred.**"

> "Same rate. **Six thousand four hundred dollars apart.** Sitting on page two, in a
> box neither of them had drawn her attention to."

*(Beat. Slow down here — the next three sentences are the payload.)*

> "She wasn't being defrauded. Every number was disclosed correctly, on the
> government form, exactly as required. **The disclosure worked perfectly.**"

> "**The disclosure only works if somebody reads page two.**"

*(Beat.)*

> "That's **mistake number three** — only talking to one lender. And in the next
> four minutes you're going to learn to read page two. It is the highest-paid four
> minutes of this webinar."
