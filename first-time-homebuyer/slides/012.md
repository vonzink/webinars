# Slide 012

**Module:** 2 · **Time:** 4:30 · **Duration:** 45s · **Type:** Data

## Purpose
Give the audience a question they can ask that most buyers don't know exists —
"can you get me underwritten up front?"

## Headline
**Three things people call "pre-approval."**

## On-slide content

| | Prequalification | Pre-approval | Underwritten approval |
|---|---|---|---|
| Credit pulled | Sometimes | Yes | Yes |
| Income & assets | Stated | Collected | **Verified** |
| Underwriter reviewed | No | Usually automated | **Yes, a human** |
| Time | Minutes | 1–2 days | 3–7 days |
| What a seller thinks | Very little | It's real | **Nearly as strong as cash** |

## Graphic
Three columns. Column 3 highlights in Alpenglow on the final build.

## Animation
Columns reveal left to right. Column 3's highlight is the last click.

## Speaker notes
> "Three different documents, and the industry uses the words loosely — sometimes
> on purpose."

> "**Prequalification** is a conversation. You tell somebody your numbers, they do
> some arithmetic. It's worth almost nothing to a seller."

> "**Pre-approval** — credit pulled, documents collected, usually run through
> automated underwriting. This is what most sellers expect."

> "**Underwritten approval** — sometimes called TBD approval. A human underwriter
> has already reviewed you. The only thing left to approve is the house, not you."

*(Point at column 3.)*

> "In a competitive situation that third column is a weapon. So if you're going to
> be competing for anything, ask your lender for it by name: **'Can you get me
> underwritten up front?'** Not everybody offers it. Now you know to ask."

## Transition
