# Slide 066

**Module:** 8 · **Time:** 33:35 · **Duration:** 45s · **Type:** Data

## Purpose
Give a genuinely two-sided comparison and deliver the property-tax trap that
blindsides new-construction buyers.

## Headline
**Don't negotiate price. Negotiate everything else.**

## On-slide content

| | New construction | Existing |
|---|---|---|
| Price negotiation | **Resisted** — protects subdivision comps | **Genuinely negotiable** |
| Incentives | **Substantial and negotiable** | Rare |
| Condition | New, with warranty | Known, inspectable, has a history |
| Timeline | Months. Delays are normal. | 30–45 days |
| Rate risk | **Long lock or float — real exposure** | Short, predictable |
| Extras | Landscaping, fencing, blinds, appliances, lot premium | Deferred maintenance you inherit |
| **Property taxes** | ⚠️ **Often land-only at first, then jump** | Established, predictable |
| Your leverage | Lower — many buyers | **Higher** — one house, one seller |

> **Ask, in writing, what the tax basis will be at FULL assessment.
> Budget from that number, not the first one.**

## Graphic
Two-column comparison. Each row's "winner" side highlights on reveal — **and it
genuinely alternates.** If one column wins every row, the graphic is lying.

## Animation
Rows reveal in sequence. The tax row gets a warning flag.

## Speaker notes
> "Honest comparison. Both of these win rows."

*(Walk it briskly.)*

> "Two things people consistently get wrong."

> "**First — builders would usually rather give you twenty thousand dollars in
> incentives than take twenty thousand off the price.** Because a price cut sets a
> comp that lowers every other house in the subdivision, and upsets everybody who
> already closed."

> "So don't negotiate price. **Negotiate everything else — and negotiate hard**,
> because there is far more room there than people use. Closing cost credits. Rate
> buydowns. Upgrades. Appliances. Landscaping. Fencing. Window coverings. Lot
> premium. HOA dues paid for a year."

*(Beat. Point at the tax row.)*

> "**Second, and this one bites hard.** Your first-year property tax bill on new
> construction may be assessed on an **unimproved lot.** Then the house gets
> assessed and your escrow payment jumps — sometimes several hundred dollars — and
> you get a bill for the shortfall. Both at once."

> "**Ask, in writing, what the tax basis will be at full assessment. Budget from
> that number, not the first one.**"

## Transition

> get. And I need to tell you something about my own bias before I analyze it."
