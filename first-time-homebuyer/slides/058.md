# Slide 058

**Module:** 6 · **Time:** 27:55 · **Duration:** 35s · **Type:** Build

## Purpose
Separate "money you spent" from "money you prefunded" — and hand over the
closing-date lever.

## Headline
**Prepaids aren't a fee. It's your money, early.**

## On-slide content

| **Closing costs** | **Prepaids & escrows** |
|---|---|
| Money spent to *get the loan* | Money that is **still yours** |
| Origination, appraisal, title, recording | Prepaid interest, first-year insurance, escrow reserves |
| Gone. You bought a service. | Front-loaded. You were always going to pay it. |

**The closing-date lever:** interest on this loan is **$76.14/day.**
Close on the 28th instead of the 3rd → roughly **$1,900** less cash at the table.
*(You still owe the interest. But if cash is your binding constraint, it's real.)*

## Speaker notes
> "These get lumped together constantly and they are completely different animals."

> "**Closing costs** — money you spend to get the loan and transfer the property.
> Origination, appraisal, title, recording. That's gone. You bought a service."

> "**Prepaids and escrows** — that money is **still yours**, you're just paying it
> early. Prepaid interest from your closing date to the end of that month. Your
> first year of homeowners insurance, paid in full. And a few months of taxes and
> insurance so the escrow account has a cushion."

> "That's not a fee. **That's your own tax and insurance money, prefunded.** You were
> always going to pay it. You're not losing it — you're front-loading it."

*(Click.)*

> "And here's a real tactic almost nobody tells first-time buyers. Interest on this
> loan is about **seventy-six dollars a day.** **Closing later in the month reduces
> your prepaid interest.** Close on the twenty-eighth instead of the third and
> you've moved roughly **nineteen hundred dollars** of cash-to-close."

> "It doesn't save you money in total — you owe the interest either way. But if
> cash at the table is your binding constraint, that's real."

## Transition
> "Which brings us to the single most expensive misunderstanding in this entire
> process."
