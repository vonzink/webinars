# Slide 041

**Module:** 5 · **Time:** 18:05 · **Duration:** 40s · **Type:** Data

## Purpose
Correct the Fed myth, and establish that nobody can forecast rates — which is what
makes the anti-urgency posture credible rather than performative.

## Headline
**The Fed does not set mortgage rates.**

## On-slide content
- The Fed sets an **overnight bank-to-bank rate.** It moves credit cards, HELOCs,
  auto loans.
- **30-year mortgages track the bond market** — mortgage-backed securities, roughly
  with the 10-year Treasury.
- **Proof:** there have been Fed cuts where mortgage rates rose the same day.

What actually moves them: investor demand for mortgage bonds · **inflation
expectations** · employment and growth data · the MBS-to-Treasury spread

> **Nobody knows where rates are going. Any strategy that requires you to be right
> about that isn't a strategy. It's a wager.**

## Graphic
One chart, two lines: the Fed funds rate and the 30-year mortgage rate, over the
same period, **visibly diverging.** Nothing else. The divergence is the argument.

## Animation
Lines draw simultaneously. Circle one divergence point.

## Speaker notes
> "Let's kill the biggest one first. **The Federal Reserve does not set mortgage
> rates.**"

> "The Fed sets the federal funds rate — an overnight bank-to-bank rate. It
> influences short-term borrowing: credit cards, HELOCs, car loans."

> "Thirty-year mortgage rates track the **bond market** — specifically
> mortgage-backed securities, which move roughly with the ten-year Treasury."

*(Point at the divergence.)*

> "And here's the proof you can check yourself: there have been Fed cuts where
> mortgage rates went **up** the same day. If the Fed set them, that couldn't
> happen."

> "What actually moves them: investor demand for mortgage bonds. Inflation
> expectations — inflation eats a fixed return, so investors demand more. Employment
> and growth data. And the spread between mortgage bonds and Treasuries, which
> widens when markets get nervous."

*(Beat.)*

> "And the honest conclusion: **nobody knows where rates are going.** Not me, not
> your agent, not the guy on the podcast. Every forecast you've seen for the last
> several years has been revised. So any strategy that requires you to be right
> about the direction of rates is not a strategy. **It's a wager.**"
