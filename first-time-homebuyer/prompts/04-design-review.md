# Prompt 04 — Design Review

Visual and presentation-design review. Run after slides are built, before rehearsal.

---

```
You are reviewing the visual design of a mortgage education webinar deck. The
audience is first-time homebuyers, 23–45, watching on laptops and phones, roughly
half of them on a replay. Many are anxious about the subject matter.

Review against the brand guidelines at [PATH] and report specific slide numbers.

## 1. LEGIBILITY — this is a webinar, not a conference talk
- Is any type below the minimum size for a phone screen?
- Does any text sit on a photo without sufficient contrast?
- Does anything rely on color alone to convey meaning?
- Would a viewer at 50% window size on a laptop be able to read every number?
- Is every number large enough to be read at a glance while the presenter talks?

## 2. COGNITIVE LOAD
- Any slide with more than one idea? (Should be zero.)
- Any slide the audience has to READ while the presenter is TALKING? Reading and
  listening compete. Flag any slide where they conflict.
- Any table over 5 rows? Over 4 columns? Both are too many for a webinar.
- Any build that reveals more than 5 elements?

## 3. CONSISTENCY
- Do recurring graphics (the canonical example's payment stack, the process map)
  look identical every time they appear? Any variation reads as a NEW thing and
  costs the audience a beat.
- Is the same idea always the same color? (E.g. if principal is one color in the
  payment stack, is it that color in the amortization chart?)
- Are numbers formatted identically everywhere — currency, decimals, percentages?

## 4. HIERARCHY
- On each slide, what does the eye hit first? Is that the most important thing?
- Are the hero slides visually distinct from the supporting ones? A deck where
  every slide has equal weight has no emphasis.

## 5. EMOTIONAL REGISTER
- Do the photographs look like real people or like stock photography? Stock
  imagery undermines a webinar whose entire premise is honesty.
- Is anything accidentally intimidating? This audience is already anxious.
- Is anything accidentally condescending?
- Does the deck's warmth match the script's warmth, or is the design colder than
  the words?

## 6. THE MOMENTS
Certain slides carry disproportionate weight. Identify them and confirm the design
gives them room:
- Any slide where a single number should land hard
- The emotional peak — is it visually quiet enough? (A crowded slide cannot carry
  an emotional beat.)
- Any reveal — is the setup visually held before the payoff?

## 7. ACCESSIBILITY
- Color contrast against WCAG AA
- Any information conveyed by color alone
- Would this be followable by someone with the audio off?

## OUTPUT
Specific slide numbers. Severity (BLOCKER / MAJOR / MINOR). What's wrong. Concrete
fix. Then: the three changes that would most improve the deck visually.
```

---

## Notes

- **Section 6 is the one generic design review misses.** Some slides carry an
  emotional beat, and the correct design for those is usually *less* — the emotional
  peak in this deck (slide 069) is deliberately black with one line of text and no
  logo.
- Section 3 catches the most real bugs in a deck like this. Recurring graphics that
  drift are the most common defect and the hardest to see when you built them.
- Run before rehearsal, not after — design changes invalidate timing.
