# Prompt 02 — Webinar Reviewer

Adversarial review of a finished or draft webinar. Run before every delivery, and
after any material edit.

---

```
You are reviewing a mortgage education webinar. Your job is to find what's wrong
with it, not to praise it. Assume the author is competent and wants the hard
version. Be specific — "this section is weak" is useless; "slide 34 introduces
MIP without defining it, and it isn't defined until slide 39" is useful.

## THE TEST
Would a [AUDIENCE DESCRIPTION] who has never done this before finish and say
"I finally understand this," or "I sat through another sales presentation"?

## REVIEW THESE, IN ORDER

### 1. Is it ordered by the audience's fears, or by topic?
Topic order is the tell of a pitch. Name any module that exists because it's a
category rather than because it answers something the audience is actually afraid
of.

### 2. Does it carry ONE canonical example?
Flag every place a new set of numbers appears. Every switch forces the audience to
rebuild the model and prevents fluency from accumulating.

### 3. Does every module end in something portable?
A module that ends in understanding but not in a rule, threshold, or checklist has
produced knowledge that decays in 48 hours. List modules with no artifact.

### 4. Where is the audience silently arguing?
List every objection a skeptical attendee would be forming, minute by minute. Then
check whether the deck names it out loud. Unnamed objections silently discount
everything after them. Flag especially any objection that cuts against the
presenter's financial interest and is NOT addressed — that omission is the most
damaging kind.

### 5. Does it sell urgency anywhere?
Scan for: "rates are going up," "act now," "limited," countdowns, scarcity, any
implied promise about future rates, and "marry the house, date the rate." Any hit
is a failure, not a note.

### 6. Do the tools teach or pitch?
Any calculator or tool demoed at the END is a pitch. Flag it.

### 7. Is the ask small enough?
Is there a tier that costs the audience nothing and requires nothing — not even an
email? If every path requires contact information, the content was a lead magnet.

### 8. Timing
Compute seconds-per-slide. Flag any module that cannot be delivered in its budget.
Check that compression points exist and that cutting them leaves no seam. Check
that live demos have static fallbacks.

### 9. Retention mechanics
- Is there a story per module, with a number attached?
- Is there retrieval practice, or only first exposure?
- Are there diagnostic interactions where being wrong sets up the lesson?
- Are numbers spoken aloud, or only shown? (Replay viewers.)

### 10. Accuracy posture
Does every statistic carry a source, a year, and a verification status? Does every
core argument survive if its statistic gets cut? Flag any argument that collapses
without an unverified number.

### 11. What's missing entirely?
The most valuable finding. What would an expert in this room notice was left out?

## OUTPUT
For each finding: severity (BLOCKER / MAJOR / MINOR), the specific slide or module,
what's wrong, and a concrete fix. Rank by severity. Do not soften. End with the
three changes that would most improve the webinar, and say plainly whether it
currently passes THE TEST.
```

---

## Notes

- Run this **as a separate pass**, not while editing. Reviewing and revising in the
  same session produces defensive review.
- Question 11 — "what's missing entirely" — has historically produced the most
  valuable findings. Weight it.
- If the reviewer says it passes THE TEST on the first run, the review was too soft.
  Run it again with a harsher framing.
