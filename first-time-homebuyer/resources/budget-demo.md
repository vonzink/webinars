# DEMO 1 — Budget Calculator

**Slide 027 · 12:00 · 40 seconds · Module 3**
**Fallback:** `slides/027-fallback.md`

---

## What this demo has to prove

Not "here's a calculator." The demo exists to make one argument visible:

> **Every other calculator online quietly tells you that you can afford more than
> you can, because none of them include the hidden five.**

The audience has just heard about the hidden five (slide 023) as an abstraction.
Watching the affordable price *drop by $60,000 on screen* when those costs are
turned on converts it from a caveat into a number they feel.

---

## The setup — do this before going live

1. Calculator open in a **separate browser tab**, pre-loaded, logged in if needed
2. Zoom to **150%** — webinar viewers are on laptops and phones
3. Pre-fill everything **except** the payment field:
   - Down payment: 5%
   - Property tax rate: 0.55%
   - Insurance: $150/mo
   - Mortgage insurance: on
   - HOA: $0
   - Rate: 6.500%, 30-year fixed
   - **"Include real ownership costs" toggle: OFF** ← starts off
4. Confirm the **payment → price** (reverse) mode is selected
5. Browser bookmarks bar hidden, notifications off, no other tabs visible

**You should have to type exactly one number on stage.** Any more and the demo runs
long.

---

## The run — 40 seconds

**[0:00] Share the tab.**

> "Every calculator online does this forwards: here's a price, here's a payment.
> Mine does it backwards, because backwards is the direction you actually think.
> You don't wake up wondering what a $450,000 house costs. You wake up wondering
> what you can handle."

**[0:10] Type the modal answer from Poll 2.**

> "Poll said most of you are around [X]. Let's put that in."

*(Result appears — the supported purchase price.)*

> "There it is. At [X] a month, all-in, you're looking at about [PRICE]."

**[0:22] The move. Flip the toggle.**

> "Now watch. This switch adds the maintenance reserve, the utility difference, and
> the furnishing budget — the stuff we just talked about that no calculator
> includes."

*(Price drops.)*

**[0:30] The point.**

> "Look at that. Same comfort level. About **$60,000 less house.** And that's the
> honest version."

> "Every calculator that leaves that off is quietly telling you that you can afford
> more than you can. It's not malicious — it's just that 'here's a bigger number'
> is a better user experience right up until you own the house."

**[0:40] Out.** → Poll 2 slide.

---

## Rules

- **Do not narrate the interface.** Nobody cares where the buttons are. Type,
  point at the result, make the argument.
- **Do not mention that you built it more than once** — it's on the slide, and the
  generosity reads better unstated.
- **One input. One toggle. Two numbers.** If you're clicking a third thing, stop.
- Say every number out loud. Replay viewers may not be watching the screen.

---

## Failure plan

**If the calculator won't load or the share fails — do not troubleshoot on camera.**
Bail in under 5 seconds.

**The bridge line:**
> "Tech's not cooperating — that's fine, I've got the output right here, and the
> link's in your download kit so you can run your own numbers tonight."

Advance to `slides/027-fallback.md`, which shows the same before/after with the
numbers pre-rendered. **Make the same argument. The audience will not know the
difference.**

**Refresh the fallback screenshot whenever the calculator's output changes.**
A fallback that doesn't match the live tool is worse than none.
