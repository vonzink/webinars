# Critique Pass 1 — against v1

Reviewer lens: *"Would a 29-year-old renter who has never talked to a lender finish this and say 'I finally understand mortgages'?"*

## Verdict: No. This is a table of contents, not a class.

### Failure 1 — It is organized by topic, not by the buyer's actual questions

The audience does not wake up wondering "what is a Loan Estimate." They wake up
wondering **"can I afford this, and am I about to get ripped off."** v1 answers in
lender order, not buyer order. That is the single biggest reason mortgage webinars
feel like sales presentations: they follow the lender's mental model.

**Fix:** Reorder around the buyer's four real questions:
1. What actually happens, and in what order? (fear of the unknown)
2. What can I afford — not what will you approve me for? (fear of overextending)
3. How much do I actually need up front? (the 20% myth — the #1 blocker)
4. How do I know I'm not being overcharged? (fear of being taken)

Credit, builder, and mistakes then become *supporting* material, not peers.

### Failure 2 — 4 minutes of intro is 4 minutes of attrition

Webinar drop-off is steepest in the first 3 minutes. Spending it on "here's my
background" spends the audience's attention on the speaker instead of on them.

**Fix:** Cold open on a stake-in-the-ground claim in the first 20 seconds.
Credentials compressed to ~25 seconds and *earned* by the claim, not front-loaded.

### Failure 3 — Calculators at the end is backwards

Demoing tools at the end reads as a product pitch. Used *during* teaching, the same
tools read as generosity and proof.

**Fix:** Three live demos, embedded where the confusion actually is:
- Budget Calculator → inside the affordability module
- APR Calculator → the moment after the APR reveal
- Amortization Calculator → the "where does my money go" gut-punch

### Failure 4 — Two polls is not interaction

**Fix:** 5 polls, and at least two must be *diagnostic setups* — the poll result
becomes the teaching moment (ask what they think the down payment minimum is,
then reveal). Plus 4 short written exercises and 3 mid-stream Q&A breaks.

### Failure 5 — No stories, so nothing will be remembered

Zero narrative in v1. People do not retain amortization tables. They retain
"the guy who bought a sofa nine days before closing."

**Fix:** Minimum 8 named stories, one per module, each with a number attached.

### Failure 6 — "Mortgage payment" and "Budgeting" are the same module

Splitting them creates a seam where there isn't one.

**Fix:** Merge into one affordability module. Frees ~3 minutes.

### Failure 7 — Nothing to do afterward

A webinar with no homework produces confidence that decays in 48 hours.

**Fix:** A 7-item homework list and 5 downloadable tools, given *before* the CTA
so the value lands unconditionally.

→ Produces **v2**.
