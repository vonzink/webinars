# Teardown of v4

External review, no deference. v4 is a competent webinar. It is not an unforgettable
one, and the gap between those two things is not polish — it is four structural
decisions.

---

## FINDING 1 — The runtime is fiction 🔴 BLOCKER

**~7,000 spoken words. At 145 wpm that is 48 minutes of talking**, before:

| Non-slide time | |
|---|---|
| 3 live demos | 2:00 |
| 5 polls | 1:30 |
| Exercise 1 (scripted silence) | 0:15 |
| Exercise 2 (45s timer) | 0:45 |
| 3 AMA breaks | 2:15 |
| **Total** | **6:45** |

**True runtime: 55–62 minutes. Labeled 40.**

Spot checks:
- Slide 064 — budgeted 110s, contains ~450 words ≈ **3:06**
- Slide 022 — budgeted 35s, needs ~60s
- Slide 067 — budgeted 100s, contains ~420 words ≈ **2:54**
- Slide 051 — budgeted 45s, contains a demo plus two cautions ≈ **1:15**

The compression plan recovers **2:25** against a **20-minute** overrun. That is a
rounding error presented as risk management.

**Consequence:** you blow the 13:00 checkpoint by minute 8 and improvise the final
third — which is where the Loan Estimate module, the credit module, and the
emotional peak all live.

**Fix:** rebuild to a **hard 4,700-word script**. That is 38 minutes at realistic
pace with interaction included. Everything else in this document follows from
having to cut 33% of the words.

---

## FINDING 2 — The best content sits behind the highest attrition wall 🔴

Webinar attendance decays hardest at 0–3 minutes and again around 20–26, when
people who came for one answer decide they got it.

v4 places:
- The **Loan Estimate module** (most valuable, most differentiated, most shareable)
  at **24:30–30:00** — dead center of the second trough
- The **emotional peak** at **37:15** — delivered to roughly half the starting room
- The **first genuinely actionable insight** at **13:20** (the 20% reveal)

Thirteen minutes of throat-clearing before the first real win.

**Fix:** open on the $6,400 Loan Estimate story at **0:00** — not minute 24. It is
the strongest hook in the file and it was being used as a mid-deck transition.
Front-load affordability. Demote the process module.

---

## FINDING 3 — The affordability module is polite, and politeness is why it's forgettable 🔴

Maya and Dev sit at **41.7% back-end DTI** — comfortable, approvable, unremarkable.
The module's punchline is that they could buy **$60,000 more house.**

That is trivia, not a lesson.

The webinar's own thesis (slide 018: *"I bought a smaller amount of fear"*) points
at the opposite and far more valuable claim: **the system approves you at a number
you should not accept.** v4 gestures at it and then declines to put a figure on it.

**Fix — the single largest improvement available:**

Re-cut the canonical example so the buyers sit at **exactly 45.0% DTI — approved at
the ceiling** — and then state the honest number out loud:

> **The bank says $450,000. The honest number is $345,000.**
> **That is a $105,000 gap between what you're approved for and what you can live with.**

Then immediately supply agency, or it's just discouragement. Five ways to close the
gap — led by the best number in the entire rebuilt deck:

> **Your $415 car payment is worth about $59,000 of house.**

That converts an abstraction ("reduce debt") into a trade the audience can actually
evaluate on a Saturday. It is the most repeatable line available to this webinar and
v4 does not contain it.

## FINDING 4 — Three of eight stories are weak

| Story | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 4 — VA / "it was only four years" | **Strongest.** Real turn, ends on a line. Keep untouched. |
| 7 — The $9,000 sofa | **Strong.** Universally relatable. Keep. |
| 5 — Two Loan Estimates | **Strong**, and wasted at minute 24. Promote to the cold open. |
| 1 — The Reyes offer | Solid, does its job. Compress. |
| 2 — Priya | **Damaged by over-construction.** Transmission + layoff + sister, all "same spring," is three misfortunes doing convenient work. Reads written. Cut to one specific misfortune. |
| 6 — $4,100 short | **Thin.** A fact with a parking lot attached. No person in it. |
| 8 — The builder's $15,000 | **Structurally the smartest story in the deck** — you argue against your own financial interest — and there is not one human beat in it. A spreadsheet with a client stapled on. |
| 3 — Tom & Ashley, six years | **The weakest thing in the deck.** "Prices went up" is a claim, not a scene. No moment, no image, no dialogue. Then a mandatory 40-word counterweight deflates it before it lands. |

**Fix:** rewrite 3, 6, and 8 with a scene, a specific object, and one line of speech.
Tighten 2. Rewrites in `../../knowledge/stories.md`.

## FINDING 5 — A verbal tic that is quietly self-flattering

**"Nobody tells you this" / "almost nobody does" / "nobody offers this"** —
slides 013, 034, 048, 056, 058, 060. **Six times.**

The subtext is *I am the only honest person in this industry.* By the fourth
instance an attentive audience hears the frame rather than the fact. The point lands
harder when the fact is allowed to be remarkable without narration.

Other repetition:
- **"Ask anyway" / "ask by name"** — 011, 013, 036, 048, 062, 069 — **six times**
- **MI removability** — taught on 034, 035, **and** 039
- **"Not a quote"** — so frequent it stops registering, which is a *compliance*
  failure, not a compliance safeguard

**Fix:** one "nobody teaches this" in the whole webinar, placed at the Page Two
artifact where it is literally true and structurally earned. Delete the rest.

---

## FINDING 6 — An 11-minute interaction desert

Poll 1 at **1:50**. Next interaction at **12:40**.

The gap covers the entire process module and half of affordability — precisely the
window where webinar audiences decide whether to stay.

**Fix:** interaction beat no later than every 4 minutes in the first half. First
one by 3:30.

---

## FINDING 7 — Stage technique imported into the wrong medium

**Slide 019's scripted 15-second silence.** In a room, powerful — nobody can leave.
In a webinar, fifteen silent seconds is when people open email, and some don't come
back. Same for the two-second holds on 051 and 069: they survive, but only because
something is on screen.

**Fix:** replace the silence with a **10-second poll** that captures the same
pre-math commitment and is clickable. Commitment preserved, retention risk removed.

---

## FINDING 8 — Slide 070 is a pamphlet, not a slide

Seven homework items + five downloads + three doors + a poll, in 90 seconds. Nothing
survives contact with an audience.

Also: **Door 1 is "do nothing."** It is a genuinely admirable gesture and it hands
the audience permission to disengage at the exact moment you want action. You can
remove pressure without explicitly inviting inaction.

**Fix:** homework moves to the download kit and gets **three** spoken items, not
seven. Two doors, not three. The "take it and go" sentiment survives as tone, not as
a numbered option.

---

## FINDING 9 — You peak, then do admin

Emotional climax at **37:15**. Then 2:30 of homework, downloads, and CTA.

The webinar ends on logistics.

**Fix:** invert. Mistakes → homework/downloads/CTA (fast, 60s) → **then the peak, as
the final thing said.** And make the CTA *part of* the peak rather than a segment
before it.

---

## FINDING 10 — Compliance labeling has gone homeopathic

A disclaimer paragraph on every numbered slide trains the audience to ignore all
fine print, including the fine print that protects you. Density is not diligence.

**Fix:** one persistent footer line + one spoken statement at minute one + full
disclosure on the downloads (which travel without context and genuinely need it).
Reviewed and confirmed against `../../prompts/05-compliance-review.md`.

---

## FINDING 11 — Concepts that cost time and earn nothing

| Cut | Why |
|---|---|
| **Front-end DTI** (025) | Only back-end governs. You teach a term they will never use again. −25s |
| **LPMI** (039) | Irrelevant to a first-time buyer choosing a program. −15s |
| **Sections E/F/G/H enumerated** (056) | The slide's entire point is *ignore these*. Then it lists them. −20s |
| **Prequal/preapproval/underwritten as three columns** (012) | Two matter. −20s |
| **USDA as a full slide** (037) | One sentence and a pointer. −25s |
| **The 9-stage process as a module** (008–016, 4:30) | Orientation, not insight. It reduces anxiety — real, but the lowest value-per-minute in the deck and the most freely available elsewhere. **−3:30** |

Demoting the process module is the largest single time recovery available, and it
funds the affordability rebuild. The map survives as **one slide plus the printed
download**, which is where it was always most useful.

---

## FINDING 12 — No villain, therefore no spine

v4 is scrupulously fair to every party. Ethically correct. Dramatically inert.

The antagonist is sitting in the deck already, unnamed, in the best paragraph of the
whole thing (slide 069): *you've done this zero times; the people across the table
have done it thousands.*

**Fix:** name it at minute two as **The Gap**. Every module closes one piece of it.
The final line of the webinar is that it's closed. That gives forty minutes a shape
instead of a sequence.

---

## FINDING 13 — No signature image

Nothing in v4 gets described to a friend on Monday.

**Fix:** **Page Two.** A physical sheet of paper, held up at 0:00, taught at 17:30,
and returned to at 38:00. Concrete, ownable, shareable, and it retitles the webinar.

---

## FINDING 14 — Questions the audience leaves with, unanswered

Ranked by how often a 23–45 first-time buyer actually asks them:

1. **Student loans** — the #1 DTI problem for this demographic, given one clause
2. **Buying with a partner you're not married to** — extremely common, zero coverage
3. **Self-employment / 1099** — one line, for a large share of this age group
4. **How do I actually win an offer?** — enormous anxiety, no coverage
5. **What happens to my money in the first year?** — nobody teaches the after
6. **What if I lose my job after closing?** — the unspoken terror
7. **How do I choose a lender or agent?** — you teach how to compare a *document*,
   never how to pick a *human*

**Fix:** #1 and #2 get a line each in the gap-closing slide. The rest become a named
"you're going to ask these" slide pointing to the download kit and Q&A. Naming them
costs 15 seconds and converts every one of them from a nagging omission into a
demonstration that you anticipated it.

---

## What v4 got right — do not lose these in the rebuild

1. **One canonical example throughout.** Correct, and the rebuild keeps it — with
   sharper numbers.
2. **Calculators inside the teaching, never as a close.**
3. **The unconditional Loan Estimate review.** The single best trust asset in the
   file. It survives untouched.
4. **Arguing against your own interest** (slide 067; renting can be right). Keep
   both. Add the human beat that's missing.
5. **Portable artifacts per module.**
6. **The refusal to forecast rates.**
7. **`knowledge/statistics.md`.** Directional-phrasing fallbacks are genuinely good
   practice and most decks in this category are worse.

---

## Verdict

v4 passes "did I learn something." It fails "I will tell someone about this."

Six changes close that distance:

| # | Change | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cut to a real 38 minutes / 4,700 words | Makes everything else deliverable |
| 2 | Open on the $6,400 story | Earns the room in 40 seconds |
| 3 | Re-cut the example to 45.0% DTI and say **$450,000 vs. $345,000** | Gives the webinar its argument |
| 4 | Add **"your car payment is worth $59,000 of house"** | The line that circulates |
| 5 | Name **The Gap** and close it module by module | Gives it a spine |
| 6 | End on the peak, not on admin | Gives it a landing |

→ **v5. See `OUTLINE-v5.md`.**
