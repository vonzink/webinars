# Slide 001 — THE COLD OPEN
**0:00 · 45s · Emotional beat**

## Purpose
Earn 38 minutes with a physical object and one number.

## Headline
*(none — the paper is the slide)*

## Graphic
**Near-black.** A single sheet of paper, photographed straight-on, filling the
frame — page 2 of a Loan Estimate, slightly angled, real paper texture, one corner
bent. No branding, no footer, no logo.

**And hold up an actual printed copy on camera while you say it.** The prop is not
optional. It is the signature of the entire webinar.

## Animation
None. The image is up when they arrive. `$6,400` fades in at the end of the line.

## Speaker notes
*(Hold up the paper. Let them look at it for a beat before you talk.)*

> "This is page two of a document somebody is going to hand you."

> "It's three pages. It's the same in every state, from every lender in America.
> You'll get one within three days of applying, and you will almost certainly skim
> page one and never turn to this."

*(beat)*

> "Page two is worth about six thousand four hundred dollars. I'll show you why in
> forty seconds."

## Transition
> "A client sent me two of these."

## Compliance
Sample document only — fabricated data, watermarked SAMPLE. NMLS/Equal Housing in
the footer of slide 004, not here.

# Slide 003 — THE GAP
**1:20 · 40s · Statement · ⭐ names the antagonist**

## Purpose
Give the webinar a villain, so every module afterward has stakes.

## Headline
**You've done this zero times.
They've done it thousands.**

## On-slide content
> **Every dollar you lose in this process lives in that gap.**
>
> Not because anyone lied to you.
> Because nobody's job was to close it.

## Graphic
Two figures on either side of a visual gap. Left: one person, one sheet of paper.
Right: a row of silhouettes and a wall of documents. The asymmetry is the image.

**This graphic recurs** as the progress indicator — the gap visibly narrowing across
the deck — and closes completely on slide 047.

## Animation
Left figure. Right figures. Then the gap opens between them.

## Speaker notes
> "Here's the actual problem, and it isn't that mortgages are complicated."

> "**You have done this zero times.** Every single person on the other side of the
> table has done it thousands. Your agent, the listing agent, the loan officer, the
> underwriter, the title company, the builder's sales rep."

*(beat)*

> "That's not a character flaw. That's arithmetic. But every dollar you lose in this
> process lives in that gap — and almost none of it gets lost because somebody lied
> to you. It gets lost because nobody's job was to close it."

> "For the next thirty-eight minutes, that's my job."

## Transition
> "Nine things. Each one closes a piece of it."

## Compliance
None.

# Slide 012 — THE HONEST NUMBER
**6:40 · 60s · Data · ⭐⭐ THE ARGUMENT**

## Purpose
The claim the entire webinar exists to make. v4 gestured at this and never put a
number on it.

## Headline
**The bank says $450,000.
The honest number is $345,000.**

## On-slide content

| | Monthly | Back-end DTI |
|---|---|---|
| **What they're approved for** — $450,000 | $3,183 *(all-in $3,633)* | **45.0%** ✓ approved |
| **What they can live with** — $345,000 | $2,450 *(all-in $2,850)* | 36.7% |

Rent today: **$2,150**
Step up at $450,000: **+$1,483/mo** · at $345,000: **+$700/mo**

> ### That's a $105,000 gap between what you qualify for and what you can carry.

*Hypothetical illustration for education only.*

## Graphic
Two house silhouettes at proportional scale. `$450,000` in grey, `$345,000` in
Alpenglow. `$105,000` between them, enormous, in the gap. **Reuse the slide-003 gap
visual** — same shape, now filled with a number.

## Animation
$450,000 with a green ✓ "APPROVED." Hold. Then $345,000 slides in beneath it. Then
$105,000 lands in the space between.

## Speaker notes
> "So a lender runs Maya and Dev's file and the answer is **yes.** Forty-five
> percent, right at the line. Four hundred fifty thousand dollars. The letter
> prints. Everybody's happy."

*(beat)*

> "And I want to tell you what that letter actually means, because it does not mean
> what people think it means."

> "**It means: our models predict you will most likely repay this.** That is the
> entire question being asked. It is not 'will you be okay.' It is not 'can you
> replace a transmission in March.' Nobody in the transaction is asking that,
> including me, unless somebody makes us."

> "The honest number for these two — where the step up from rent is seven hundred a
> month instead of fifteen hundred — is about **three hundred forty-five thousand.**"

*(beat — let it sit)*

> "**A hundred and five thousand dollars between what they qualify for and what they
> can carry.** And nobody on the other side of that table has a reason to mention
> it."

## Transition
> "Now — I know what a chunk of you are thinking, because it's what I'd be
> thinking."

## Compliance
🔴 On-slide hypothetical label. DTI ceiling described as "commonly used" and
AUS-driven, never as a fixed rule. This slide must not read as discouraging any
borrower from applying — it is followed **immediately** by slide 014.

# Slide 014 — CLOSING $105,000
**8:20 · 75s · Build · ⭐⭐ THE LINE THAT CIRCULATES**

## Purpose
Convert the gap from discouragement into agency — and hand over the most repeatable
sentence in the webinar.

## Headline
**Your car payment is worth
$59,000 of house.**

## On-slide content
Every **$1/month** of debt you remove ≈ **$141 of purchase price.**

| Remove | Buys back |
|---|---|
| **The $415 car payment** | **≈ $59,000** |
| The $290 student loan payment | ≈ $41,000 |
| **Both** | **≈ $100,000 — almost the whole gap** |

**Five ways to close $105,000**
1. Kill the car payment → **$59,000**
2. Down payment assistance → state, metro, county, **employer**
3. Add a co-buyer → income lands directly in the numerator
4. Move the search 20 minutes
5. **Eight months** — one credit tier, revolving paid down, more saved

## Graphic
A bar representing the $105,000 gap. Each lever **visibly shortens it** as it's
named. By item 5 the bar is nearly gone. That closing animation is the emotional
payload — the audience watches a problem get solved.

## Animation
Gap bar. Then one segment removed per click. Do not rush it — the shrinking is the
point.

## Speaker notes
> "First — the uncomfortable part, and I'm not going to skip it. In a lot of the
> Front Range, three hundred forty-five thousand dollars does not buy much. That is
> a real problem, and pretending otherwise is exactly what this industry does."

> "So let's actually close it."

*(click)*

> "Here's a number nobody ever tells first-time buyers. On this loan, **every dollar
> a month of debt you get rid of is worth about a hundred forty-one dollars of
> purchase price.**"

> "Which means Maya and Dev's car payment — four hundred fifteen dollars a month —
> **is worth fifty-nine thousand dollars of house.**"

*(beat — let that land)*

> "Their student loan payment is worth forty-one. Kill both and you've closed a
> hundred thousand of a hundred and five thousand dollar gap. Same income. Same
> credit. Same everything."

> "I'm not telling you to go pay off your car tomorrow — for some of you that's the
> down payment, and that's a real trade. **I'm telling you it's a trade you can now
> actually evaluate**, which forty seconds ago it wasn't."

*(fast through the rest)*

> "Down payment assistance. A co-buyer. Twenty minutes further out. Or eight months
> — one credit tier, revolving paid down, more saved."

> "Five levers. Most people know about none of them, and then they either overbuy or
> they give up. **Those are not the only two options.**"

## Transition
> "Let me show you what that actually looks like."

## Compliance
🔴 Hypothetical. The $141-per-dollar factor derives from the canonical example and
scales with rate, price, and program — say "on a loan like this one." Paying off
debt vs. preserving down payment cash is a genuine tradeoff — the script names it.
Not financial advice.

# Slide 046 — WHAT YOU'RE STILL GOING TO ASK
**35:40 · 80s · Build**

## Purpose
Name the seven questions the audience is leaving with — which converts seven
omissions into evidence you anticipated them — then deliver the kit and the ask.

## Headline
**Seven things I didn't have time for.
Here's where they are.**

## On-slide content
1. **Student loans** — how they're actually counted *(varies by program)*
2. **Buying with a partner you're not married to** — title, contribution, exit
3. **Self-employed / 1099** — you qualify on net, after write-offs
4. **How to actually win an offer**
5. **What your money looks like in year one**
6. **What happens if you lose your job after closing**
7. **How to pick a lender and an agent** — not just how to grade the paperwork

**→ All seven in the download kit. Or ask in Q&A in three minutes.**

**THE KIT — 5 files, no form, no email**
Checklist *(with the full 9-stage process map)* · Closing Cost Worksheet · Loan
Estimate Cheat Sheet · Questions to Ask a Builder · Glossary

**HOMEWORK — three things this week**
① Pull your credit ② Run the Payment-Shock Test ③ Call one lender — not to apply

**TWO DOORS**
🚪 **Send me a Loan Estimate.** Any lender. Free. No strings.
🚪 **A 20-minute Numbers Call.** No credit pull. No application.

## Graphic
Seven questions as a fast list, then they collapse into the download kit graphic.
Two doors, the first one twice the size of the second.

## Animation
Questions in fast. Collapse. Kit. Doors. Poll 5 overlays.

## Speaker notes
> "Seven things I know you're still wondering, because everybody is."

*(read them fast — do not answer any)*

> "Student loans. Buying with a partner you're not married to. Self-employment.
> Winning an offer. What year one actually costs. What happens if you lose your job.
> And how to pick a lender and an agent — not just how to grade their paperwork."

> "All seven are in the kit, and Q&A starts in three minutes."

*(kit)*

> "Five files. **No form, no email.** The nine-stage process map is in there too —
> print it, put it on the fridge."

*(homework)*

> "Three things this week. Pull your credit. Run the payment-shock test. Call one
> lender — not to apply. Twenty minutes."

*(doors — slow down)*

> "And two doors. The first one is the one I actually want you to take. **Send me a
> Loan Estimate from any lender — mine, theirs, the builder's — and I will mark it
> up and send it back.** Section A circled. Cash to close explained. No cost, no
> obligation, and I genuinely do not care whether I'm your lender."

> "Why? Because if I tell you somebody else's offer is good and it costs me the
> deal, you'll remember who told you the truth. That's the whole strategy. It's a
> slow one."

> "Second door — twenty minutes on the phone. No credit pull, no application."

*(POLL 5 — confidence 1–10.)*

## Compliance
🔴 NMLS ×2 + Equal Housing required. "No credit pull, no application" must be **true**
— if intake changes, this language changes. Service offer, not a credit offer.

# Slide 047 — THE LANDING
**37:00 · 60s · Emotional beat · ⭐⭐ THE LAST THING SAID**

## Purpose
End on the peak. v4 peaked at 37:15 and then did two and a half minutes of admin.

## Headline
*(builds to)* **The gap is closed.**

## On-slide content
Then, alone on black:
> **You are allowed to not know this.**

## Graphic
The slide-003 gap visual, returning — **and this time the two sides close.** Then
everything clears to near-black and one line appears.

**No logo. No footer. No branding.** Same rule as the other emotional beats.

## Animation
Gap closes over ~1.5s. Hard cut to black. Two seconds of empty black. The line
fades in over 800ms.

## Speaker notes
> "Thirty-eight minutes ago I told you there's a gap. You've done this zero times;
> they've done it thousands."

*(gap begins to close)*

> "So — you know that the bank's number and your number are different, and roughly
> how far apart. You know what your car payment is worth in house. You know that
> twenty percent was never required. You know where Section A is, and that cash to
> close is not closing costs. You know the breakeven question. And you know that
> shopping three lenders costs you nothing."

*(gap closes)*

> "**That's the gap. It's closed.**"

*(beat)*

> "One last thing. Every story I told you today — the woman with two Loan Estimates,
> the guy who served four years and never asked, the man with the color-coded
> spreadsheet who felt embarrassed at the closing table, the guy who bought a
> couch — none of those people were careless."

> "They just didn't ask. Because asking felt like admitting they didn't know. And
> this industry has been very comfortable with that, because a client who doesn't
> ask questions is a faster close."

*(hard cut to black. **TWO FULL SECONDS.** Then the line.)*

> "**You are allowed to not know this.**"

*(beat)*

> "You've done it zero times. That's not a flaw. That's a Tuesday."

*(beat)*

> "Ask everything. Q&A is open, and I'll stay as long as you do."

## Compliance
🔴 **No branding on the black.** A logo here turns the most sincere moment in the
webinar into an ad.
