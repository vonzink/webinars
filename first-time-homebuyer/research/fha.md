# FHA

**Primary source:** HUD *Single Family Housing Policy Handbook 4000.1*
**Used in slides:** 032, 035, 039
**Risk level:** 🔴 HIGH — MIP duration is the most consequential fact in this file
and it is policy-set.

---

## The honest framing for the webinar

FHA is a genuinely good program that has created an enormous number of homeowners.
The webinar's job is not to talk anyone out of it — it's to make sure they know the
**one tradeoff that rarely gets mentioned at the sales table**, so the choice is
informed.

Slide 035's structure: *what it's great at → the tradeoff → the strategy that
follows from the tradeoff.*

## Core parameters

| Item | Value | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum down payment | **3.5%** at 580+ credit score | 🟢 |
| Down payment at lower scores | **10%** in the 500–579 band (lender overlays commonly stricter — many lenders won't go below 580 or 620 at all) | 🟡 |
| Occupancy | Primary residence | 🟢 |
| Loan limits | By county, set annually | 🔴 VERIFY |
| Upfront MIP (UFMIP) | Percentage of base loan, typically financed into the balance | 🔴 VERIFY current rate |
| Annual MIP | Paid monthly; varies by LTV, term, loan amount | 🔴 VERIFY current rates |

## ⭐ MIP duration — the fact the module turns on

- **Less than 10% down → annual MIP generally lasts the LIFE OF THE LOAN** 🔴
- **10% or more down → MIP generally drops after 11 years** 🔴

> This is *the* structural difference from conventional. Conventional MI has an
> exit at 80/78% LTV. FHA MIP at minimum down payment does not.

**Slide 035 script (verbatim, keep):**
> "FHA's monthly cost is often competitive, sometimes better than conventional —
> especially if your credit is in the 600s, where conventional pricing gets
> expensive fast. But conventional's mortgage insurance has an exit and FHA's
> usually doesn't. Which means for a lot of FHA buyers the plan isn't 'FHA
> forever.' It's *FHA to get in, refinance out when it makes sense.* That's a
> completely legitimate strategy. It's just one you should choose on purpose,
> knowing the exit is a future refinance that depends on future rates. Nobody can
> promise you that refinance. Anyone who does is selling."

That last sentence is a compliance asset as much as an honesty one. Keep it.

## Where FHA genuinely wins

- **Credit flexibility** — lower scores, and more tolerance for recent derogatory
  history than conventional pricing allows
- **Higher DTI tolerance** in many scenarios, especially with compensating factors
- **Collections and charge-offs** treated more permissively (thresholds and rules
  apply — 4000.1, Credit)
- **Waiting periods after derogatory events** are generally shorter than conventional
- **Non-occupant co-borrowers** permitted in ways conventional restricts
- **Assumable** — a real and underrated feature, particularly if the loan carries a
  below-market rate. Q&A material; not on a slide (it would take 90 seconds to
  explain properly).

## Underwriting path

- **TOTAL Scorecard** (automated) vs. **manual underwriting**
- Manual underwriting carries stricter ratio requirements and requires documented
  compensating factors
- Lender overlays are extremely common on FHA — the same borrower can be declined
  at one lender and approved at another. **This is a real argument for a broker
  and it should be stated as a fact, not a pitch.**

## Property

- **Minimum Property Requirements (MPR)** — safety, security, soundness
- Required repairs can affect a transaction; a seller may refuse to make them
- Relevant to older housing stock; worth a Q&A mention if it comes up

## Assets

- Gift funds permitted from eligible donors (family, employer, charitable
  organizations, government entities) with documentation
- Down payment assistance is broadly compatible with FHA — this is why CHFA + FHA
  is a common Colorado stack

## FHA vs. Conventional — the comparison to run for every borrower

| | FHA | Conventional 3–5% |
|---|---|---|
| Min down | 3.5% | 3% |
| Credit flexibility | **Better** | Worse below ~660 |
| MI removable? | **Usually no** at <10% down | **Yes** at 80/78% |
| Upfront fee | UFMIP, financed | None (points optional) |
| Pricing at high credit | Worse | **Better** |
| Pricing at lower credit | **Better** | Worse |
| DTI tolerance | **Generally higher** | AUS-driven |
| Assumable | **Yes** | No |

> **Never say "FHA is worse."** Say: *"Different, not worse. Run both. The right
> answer depends on your credit and how long you'll keep the loan."*
