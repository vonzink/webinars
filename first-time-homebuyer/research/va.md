# VA

**Primary source:** VA *Lenders Handbook* (M26-7) · Certificate of Eligibility via VA
**Used in slides:** 032, 036, 039
**Story:** Story 4 — "It Was Only Four Years"

---

## Why this gets a full slide and a story

VA is the strongest financing available to anyone who qualifies, and eligible
borrowers routinely don't know they're eligible — or assume their service "didn't
count." The slide's real payload isn't the program mechanics. It's:
**ask anyway.**

## ⭐ Residual income — the smarter test

VA doesn't rely on a ratio alone. It asks: after the mortgage, taxes, insurance,
utilities estimate, taxes withheld, and all recurring debts — **how much cash is
actually left?** The required amount varies by **family size and region of the
country.**

> **Slide 036 line:** *"VA qualifies on residual income — cash left after all
> obligations, by family size and region — which is a more humane test than a pure
> ratio."*

This is worth teaching even to non-veterans, because it reframes what
affordability testing *should* measure. It reinforces Module 3's whole argument.

Borrowers can exceed typical DTI guidance with sufficient residual income and
compensating factors.

## Who is eligible

Broader than most people assume:
- Active duty (after a service-length requirement)
- Veterans meeting service requirements
- **National Guard and Reserve members** meeting service requirements
- Certain **surviving spouses**

**Get the Certificate of Eligibility (COE).** Homework item #7.

> **The story's whole point:** he did four years after high school, was 34, and
> nobody had ever asked. He was about to pay 3.5% down plus lifetime MIP for the
> privilege of not asking a question. **If service is anywhere in the household —
> including a spouse — ask.**

## Reusability — correcting a common myth

VA entitlement is **not once-per-lifetime.** It restores when the prior loan is
paid off or, in some cases, when eligibility is substituted. Borrowers can even
have more than one VA loan simultaneously under certain conditions.

FAQ entry exists for this. Common enough to expect in Q&A.

---

## Tradeoffs — state them, don't hide them

- **Funding fee** — real money, though financeable and waived for many
- **VA appraisal and Minimum Property Requirements** — stricter on condition;
  can complicate a competitive offer on a fixer
- **Some sellers/agents hold outdated bias** against VA offers (slower, pickier —
  largely a legacy perception). Worth naming: a well-prepared VA buyer with an
  underwritten approval is a strong buyer, and the listing side should be told so.
- **Condo projects** must be VA-approved

## Assumable

VA loans are assumable by qualified buyers. Underrated feature — potentially very
valuable on a below-market-rate loan. Q&A material.

## Compliance

- **Never** state a specific funding fee percentage on a slide without verifying it
  for the current period. Percentages are policy-set and have changed.
- The disability-compensation waiver is stated as a general rule — individual
  eligibility is determined by VA.
- Do not imply VA approval or eligibility for any individual.
- Slide 036's story must remain a composite with changed details.
