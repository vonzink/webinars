**Used in slides:** 057–059 · **Story 6**

## The three-bucket model — the mental model to install

Almost all confusion here comes from lumping three different kinds of money
together. Separate them and the whole thing clarifies.

### Bucket 1 — Loan costs *(you spent it)*
Origination, points, appraisal, credit report, flood cert, underwriting fees.
LE Sections A + B + C.

### Bucket 2 — Government and transfer costs *(you spent it)*
Recording fees, transfer taxes, title work. LE Sections C + E + H.

### Bucket 3 — Prepaids and escrows *(still yours, paid early)*
Prepaid interest, first-year homeowners insurance, escrow reserves. LE Sections F + G.

> **Buckets 1 and 2 are closing costs. Bucket 3 is not.** Bucket 3 is your own
> future tax and insurance money, prefunded.

**Cash to Close = down payment + buckets 1, 2, 3 − earnest money − credits.**

## What's negotiable, and by whom

| Cost | Negotiable? | With whom |
|---|---|---|
| Origination / lender fees (A) | **Yes** — by choosing a different lender | Lender |
| Points | **Yes** — it's a choice, not a fee | Lender |
| Appraisal (B) | No | — |
| Credit report, flood cert (B) | No | — |
| **Title & settlement (C)** | **YES — and almost nobody does** | You, via the Written List of Providers |
| Survey, pest (C) | **Yes** | You |
| Recording, transfer taxes (E) | No | — |
| **Homeowners insurance** | **YES — shop it** | Your insurer |
| Prepaid interest | Indirectly — via closing date | Timing |
| Escrow reserves | No | — |
| **Who pays what** | **YES — seller concessions** | The seller/builder, in the contract |

**The two most-missed levers:**
1. **Section C** — the lender hands you a Written List of Providers and you're
   allowed to bring your own. Four-figure variance in the same city.
2. **Homeowners insurance** — shop it early. In Colorado this matters more than it
   used to, and a high premium can affect qualifying, not just budget.

## Seller concessions

The seller pays some of your closing costs. **Limits vary by program and down
payment** 🔴 VERIFY — generally more restrictive at lower down payments on
conventional; FHA, VA, and USDA have their own caps and rules.

**Not on a slide** — it's contract-negotiation territory and belongs with the
agent. Q&A-ready:
> "It's negotiated in the contract, the limits depend on your program and down
> payment, and in a softer market it's often more available than buyers realize.
> Ask your agent to ask."

**Lender credits** are the parallel lever from the lender side — a higher rate in
exchange for the lender covering costs. Covered on slide 048.

## ⭐ The closing-date lever

Daily interest = loan × rate ÷ 365. Canonical: **$76.14/day.**

Closing on the 28th vs. the 3rd moves roughly **$1,900** of cash to close.

**State the honest caveat:** total interest owed doesn't change — you pay it either
way. But if cash at the table is the binding constraint, moving $1,900 is real.

> This is a genuinely useful, rarely-shared tactic. It reads as insider knowledge
> given freely, which is exactly the tone of the whole webinar.

## What buyers forget entirely

Not closing costs, but they hit in the same 60 days and they're why the emergency
fund matters:

- **Home inspection** — paid out of pocket, before closing, and non-refundable if
  you walk. That's the cost of information, and it's worth it.
- **Moving** — DIY or movers
- **Immediate needs** — window coverings, a lawnmower, the tool you don't own,
  the locks you should change on day one
- **The first repair** — the thing the inspection flagged as "monitor"
- **Utility deposits and setup**

> **Slide 023 framing:** *"Don't spend your last dollar at the closing table. The
> emergency fund is not part of the down payment. If buying the house requires
> spending the emergency fund, you're buying too much house."*
