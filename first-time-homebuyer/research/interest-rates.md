## ⭐ What moves mortgage rates — the correction

**The Federal Reserve does not set mortgage rates.**

- The Fed sets the **federal funds rate** — an overnight interbank rate. It
  strongly influences short-term consumer credit: credit cards, HELOCs, autos.
- **30-year mortgage rates track the bond market** — mortgage-backed securities,
  which move roughly with the 10-year Treasury, plus a spread.

**The proof to cite (it's the strongest teaching move in the module):** there have
been Fed rate cuts where mortgage rates rose the same day. If the Fed set mortgage
rates, that couldn't happen. 🟡 — state as a pattern that has occurred, not with a
specific date unless verified.

**Actual drivers:**
- Investor demand for mortgage-backed securities
- **Inflation expectations** — inflation erodes a fixed future return, so investors
  demand more yield
- Employment and growth data
- The **MBS-to-Treasury spread**, which widens when markets get nervous about
  prepayment risk or volatility

**The required conclusion — do not soften:**
> "Nobody knows where rates are going. Not me, not your agent, not the guy on the
> podcast. Every forecast for the last several years has been revised. Any strategy
> that requires you to be right about the direction of rates is not a strategy.
> It's a wager."

## ⭐ The 7 things that change *your* rate

Market sets a base; your file adjusts it:

1. **Credit score** — the largest single lever, and it moves in **tiers**
2. **LTV** — your down payment
3. **Loan program** — conventional / FHA / VA / USDA price differently
4. **Occupancy** — primary vs. second home vs. investment (large jumps)
5. **Property type** — SFR vs. condo vs. 2–4 unit
6. **Loan term** — 15-year prices below 30-year
7. **Points or credits** — buying the rate down or taking a credit up

**Why "what's your rate today" is unanswerable:**
> "It's like asking a doctor 'what's the dose.' Dose of what, for whom? Anyone who
> fires back a number without knowing five of those seven things is quoting a rate
> you may not qualify for. That's not a quote. That's bait."

This line does double duty: it teaches, and it's the compliance-safe answer to the
question that *will* come up in chat.

## ⭐ APR

**APR = the note rate plus certain lender costs, amortized across the full loan
term, expressed as an annual rate.** Required by TILA/Reg Z.

**Analogy:** rate = sticker price; APR ≈ out-the-door price.
**Why it exists:** lenders advertised low rates and recovered it in fees. APR was
the regulatory response.

### Where APR misleads 🟢 structural
1. **It assumes you hold the loan the full term.** Most people don't. Upfront costs
   get spread across 360 months you won't use.
2. **ARMs** — APR must assume future adjustments from today's index.
3. **Inclusion varies** — not every cost is treated identically, and shoppable
   third-party costs muddy comparison.

**The teaching conclusion:** *use APR as a flag, not a verdict.* A wide gap between
rate and APR means large upfront costs — go find them. That's what it's for.

## ⭐ Points and breakeven

**One point = 1% of the loan amount**, paid at closing, to permanently reduce the
rate. Prepaid interest.

**Lender credits are the same trade in reverse** — accept a higher rate, the lender
covers some closing costs. **Frequently the right move for a cash-constrained
first-time buyer, and almost never volunteered.** Tell people to ask.

### The canonical comparison
$427,500 loan. Loan A: 6.750%, 0 points. Loan B: 6.250%, 2 points ($8,550).
P&I $2,773 vs. $2,632 → **$141/month.**

```
Breakeven = 8,550 / 141 = 60.6 months ≈ 5 years 1 month
```

**PORTABLE RULE:** *Points are a bet on how long you keep this exact loan. Compute
the breakeven in months. If you won't clearly pass it, don't.*

**The subtlety worth teaching:** it's the **loan**, not the house. A refinance ends
the loan even if you never move. First-time buyers systematically overestimate how
long they'll hold a specific loan — and "I don't know" is itself an argument
against points.

## Temporary buydowns

**2-1 buydown:** year 1 acts ~2% lower, year 2 ~1% lower, year 3+ the note rate.
Difference prefunded into an escrow account, **usually paid by a seller or builder
as a concession.**

**Upside:** real cash-flow relief in the two most expensive years, often on
somebody else's money. Unused funds (from a refinance or sale) are **generally**
credited toward the loan rather than forfeited 🟡 — varies by lender/investor,
hedge with "generally."

**The trap — say it plainly:**
> "You qualify at the **real** rate, not the bought-down rate. You will be paying
> the full payment in year three. If the plan depends on refinancing before then,
> that plan depends on rates you cannot control."

**The builder-specific version:**
> "Sometimes a rate buydown is funded by a price that was set high enough to pay
> for it. A price reduction is permanent. A temporary buydown is two years. Ask
> which one you can have. Sometimes you can have either."

## Amortization

$427,500 @ 6.500%, 30-year:
- Payment 1: **$2,315 interest / $387 principal** — 83% interest
- Crossover (principal exceeds interest): **month 218**
- Lifetime interest: **≈ $545,200**

**The $100 experiment:** +$100/month → paid in ~27.0 years, interest ≈ $480,900.
**Saves ≈ $64,300 and ~3 years.**

**Two required cautions:**
1. Confirm the servicer applies extra payments to **principal** — some apply it to
   the next payment instead, which does nothing.
2. Don't do this ahead of an emergency fund. Money in the house is hard to retrieve.

## 🔴 COMPLIANCE — the tightest section in the deck

- All rates are **hypothetical illustrations. Not quotes. Not offers. Not
  commitments to lend.** This must appear **on-slide**, not just be spoken.
- **Never project rate direction.** Not up, not down, not "experts expect."
- Under **Reg Z**, stating certain credit terms in an advertisement triggers
  additional required disclosures. This content is **educational and
  non-advertising** — the labeling on slides 043, 045, and 049 is precisely what
  keeps it there. Do not remove it to clean up the design.
- No urgency framing tied to rates. Ever. Slide 005 promised this on camera.
- **"Marry the house, date the rate" is banned** — it smuggles in a promise of a
  future refinance that nobody can make.
