# First-Time Buyer Statistics

**Register with status flags:** `../knowledge/statistics.md`
**Verification evidence:** `../statistics/`
**Procedure:** `../prompts/06-fact-check.md`

> **This file describes what to look for and how to speak it. It deliberately does
> not hardcode figures.** A number written here becomes a number on a slide, and a
> number on a slide that was last checked eight months ago is how educational
> content quietly becomes wrong.

---

## The five claims that carry weight in this deck

### 1. Median first-time buyer down payment is well below 20% ⭐
**Slide 032. This is the load-bearing statistic of the entire webinar.**
**Source:** NAR *Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers* (annual)

Look for: median down payment for **first-time** buyers specifically (not all
buyers — repeat buyers roll equity forward and skew it badly).

**Safe phrasing if unverified:**
> "The median first-time buyer puts down far less than twenty percent — and has for
> a long time."

The slide-032 argument works entirely on the **program minimums** (3% / 3.5% / 0%),
which are rule-based and stable. The statistic is corroboration, not the argument.
**If it can't be verified, cut it and lose nothing.**

---

### 2. Most buyers get only one quote ⭐
**Slide 053. Second most important.**
**Source:** CFPB research on mortgage shopping behavior

Look for: share of borrowers who seriously considered only one lender.

**Safe phrasing if unverified:**
> "A large share of buyers get exactly one quote."

---

### 3. Meaningful savings are available from shopping ⭐
**Slide 053.**
**Sources:** CFPB; Freddie Mac shopping studies

Look for: documented savings from obtaining multiple quotes.

**Safe phrasing if unverified:**
> "The savings available to people who get more than one quote are well documented,
> and they are not small."

Story 5's $6,400 is a **composite illustration**, not a study finding, and must
never be presented as an average.

---

### 4. Median homeowner tenure is far under 30 years ⭐
**Slide 049 — the breakeven argument.**
**Sources:** NAR *Profile*; Census American Housing Survey

**Watch the definitional trap:** "median tenure in a home" ≠ "median time holding a
specific mortgage." A refinance ends the loan without a move, so **loan tenure is
shorter than home tenure.** That distinction *strengthens* the breakeven argument
and should be stated.

**Safe phrasing:**
> "Most people don't keep a mortgage anywhere near thirty years. They move, or they
> refinance — and refinancing ends the loan even if you never move."

---

### 5. Down payment is the top perceived barrier
**Slide 032, supporting.**
**Sources:** NAR; Fannie Mae National Housing Survey

**Safe phrasing:**
> "When renters are asked what's stopping them, the down payment is consistently at
> the top of the list — and the number in their head is usually wrong."

## Audience-profile context (background, not slide content)

Useful for calibrating tone; not spoken.

- First-time buyer median age has trended upward over the past decade 🟡
- First-time buyers make up a smaller share of purchases than their historical
  norm 🟡
- Dual-income and co-buying arrangements have grown as affordability tightened 🟡
- A large share of first-time buyers receive gift or family assistance with the
  down payment 🟡 — relevant to how the gift-funds FAQ is framed

> **Tone implication that matters:** the audience is *older and more financially
> established* than "first-time buyer" implies. They are not kids. Several are
> professionals who feel embarrassed that they don't understand this. **That
> embarrassment is the emotional core the webinar targets** (slide 069). Do not
> talk down to this room.

## Statistics deliberately excluded

| Excluded | Why |
|---|---|
| Home price forecasts | Nobody knows. Violates slide 005. |
| Rate forecasts | Same. |
| "Homeowners have Nx the net worth of renters" | True in aggregate, heavily confounded by selection. Presenting it as causal is dishonest. |
| "X% of buyers regret their purchase" | Survey-dependent, unstable, and it's fear-selling. |
| "You're losing $X/month renting" | Ignores maintenance, taxes, transaction costs, opportunity cost. Manipulative. |
| "The average American moves every N years" | Conflates all moves with homeowner tenure. |

## The standing rule

**A cut statistic costs nothing. A wrong one costs the webinar.**

Every core argument in this deck was built to survive without a precise figure:
- The 20% myth stands on **program minimums** (rule-based)
- The breakeven lesson stands on **arithmetic** (self-evident)
- The shop-lenders lesson stands on **Section A** (verifiable on a form the
  audience will hold in their own hands)

The statistics are corroboration. Never load-bearing.
