## Why this is the most valuable module in the webinar

Everything else teaches **understanding**. This teaches **verification.** After
Module 6 the audience can independently check any lender's work — including the
presenter's.

That is the highest-trust act available to a loan officer on camera, and it's what
makes the no-strings CTA at slide 070 credible rather than cute.

## What the form is

Standardized **three-page** disclosure required under the TRID rules, effective
**October 2015**. Replaced the old Good Faith Estimate and initial TIL.

**Every lender in America must use the same form, in the same order.** That's the
gift — before it existed, comparison shopping was nearly impossible because every
lender had a different layout.

### Two consumer protections to state
1. **Delivery timing:** must be provided within **3 business days** of a completed
   application. 🟢
2. **Tolerances:** certain disclosed costs are subject to limits on increase. Some
   may not increase without a valid changed circumstance; others are limited in
   aggregate. If exceeded, the lender generally must cure. 🟢

> **Compliance phrasing:** describe tolerances **generally**. Do not enumerate the
> categories as legal advice on a slide. Scripted as: *"certain costs are subject
> to tolerance limits... if they do go up, the lender generally has to cure the
> difference."*

### The sentence that unlocks shopping
> "**Asking for a Loan Estimate does not obligate you to anything.** It's not a
> commitment. It's not a hard sell. Get three. Genuinely, get three."

Pair with the credit shopping window (slide 064) — together they remove both
reasons people don't shop.

## Page 1 — the five boxes

1. **Loan Terms** — amount, rate, monthly P&I, plus yes/no columns: *can this
   increase after closing?* prepayment penalty? balloon payment?
   > On a standard 30-year fixed these should all be the boring answer. Anything
   > surprising is a stop-and-ask.
2. **Projected Payments** — payment over time, including MI and when it drops off
3. **Estimated Taxes, Insurance & Assessments** — the escrow line
4. **Costs at Closing** — **two different numbers**: *Estimated Closing Costs* and
   *Estimated Cash to Close* ← the whole of Story 6 lives here
5. **Rate lock status and expiration**

**Critical comparison rule:** Loan Estimates issued on different days compare
*days*, not lenders. Rates move. **Same day, same loan amount, same program, same
lock period.**

## ⭐ Page 2 — the letters

| Section | Contents | Shoppable? |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Origination Charges** | Lender's own fees + points | **No — but shop lenders. THIS IS THE BOX.** |
| **B. Services You Cannot Shop For** | Appraisal, credit report, flood cert | No |
| **C. Services You CAN Shop For** | Title, settlement, survey, pest | **Yes — and almost nobody does** |
| **D** | Total of A+B+C | — |
| **E. Taxes & Government Fees** | Recording, transfer taxes | No |
| **F. Prepaids** | Prepaid interest, first-year insurance | Not really |
| **G. Initial Escrow Payment** | Escrow cushion | No |
| **H. Other** | Owner's title policy, home warranty | Sometimes |

**The whole lesson: A and C.**

- **Section A** is where lenders differ from each other. Story 5: ~$1,300 vs.
  ~$7,700 at the *same rate*.
- **Section C** is unused leverage — the lender must provide a **Written List of
  Providers**, and the borrower may use their own. **Title and settlement fees can
  vary by four figures between companies in the same city.** Nearly nobody shops it.
- E through G are largely not the lender's doing. Nobody competes on your property
  taxes.

**Analogy (slide 056):** Section A is the **labor line** on a repair estimate. Parts
cost what parts cost. Labor is where shops differ.

## ⭐ Prepaids and escrows ≠ closing costs

**Closing costs** — money spent to get the loan and transfer the property. Gone.

**Prepaids and escrows** — **still your money**, paid early:
- **Prepaid interest** — from closing date to end of that month
- **First year's homeowners insurance**
- **Escrow reserves** — a few months of taxes/insurance as a cushion

> "You're not losing it. You're front-loading it."

### The closing-date lever
Daily interest on the canonical loan is **$76.14**. Closing on the 28th instead of
the 3rd moves roughly **$1,900** of cash to close.

**State the caveat honestly:** it doesn't reduce total interest owed — you owe it
either way. But if **cash at the table** is the binding constraint, it's real, and
almost nobody tells first-time buyers about it.

## ⭐ Cash to Close vs. Closing Costs — Story 6

| | |
|---|---|
| Down payment | $22,500 |
| Closing costs | $5,400 |
| Prepaid interest (~15 days) | $1,142 |
| Insurance, 12 months | $1,800 |
| Escrow reserves | $918 |
| **Cash to Close** | **$31,760** |
| Less earnest money | −$5,000 |
| **Due at the table** | **$26,760** |

> "Closing costs: $5,400. Cash to close: $31,760. Those are not the same word, and
> they are twenty-six thousand dollars apart."

**The one physical location to memorize:** **Cash to Close, page 1, bottom right.**

## ARTIFACT #5 — Compare two lenders in 60 seconds

1. Both LEs issued the **same day**
2. Same loan amount, program, and lock period
3. Compare **Section A only**
4. Note **points** on page 1 — a bought-down rate is not a lower rate
5. Check **Cash to Close**, page 1
6. Ignore B, E, F, G — that isn't the lender competing

> "That's the entire skill. It took four minutes to teach. It's been kept from you
> for no reason other than that nobody's incentive was to teach it."
